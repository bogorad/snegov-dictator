# Dictator — Complete Chapter-by-Chapter Summary

## Introduction

Sergei Snegov's *Dictator* is a political science-fiction novel about the effort to end war through power, persuasion, terror, and mercy. This English account follows the Russian EPUB supplied in this repository: six parts and all 73 chapters, in their original order. The [source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md) identifies their boundaries, including chapters that share an HTML file or continue into another. **Full spoilers follow, including the final revelations and the ending.**

The story begins in a world of rival states whose leaders turn a small border dispute into a widening war. Latania faces the military and economic strength of Cortezia, while smaller countries bargain, change sides, and pursue their own interests. Adan, the Latanian capital, and the threatened city of Zabon become centers of political and military action. The [places catalog](docs/knowledge/places.md) traces these relationships. Technology brings the war into fields, homes, and hospitals: concentrated energy-water powers machines and weapons, while weather generators alter rainfall and unleash destructive storms, with imperfect control. Food production and civilian survival are therefore central to strategy. The [world reference](docs/knowledge/world.md) explains these technologies and the institutions built around them.

At the center stands [Alexei Gamov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#alexei-gamov), an astrophysicist whose force of personality and military invention carry him toward dictatorship. He argues that peace requires an attack on the prestige, rewards, and habits that make war acceptable. His methods include payments for battlefield success, humiliating punishments, public threats, international courts, and carefully managed broadcasts. He also demands mercy toward enemies and costly aid for their children and hungry populations. These policies repeatedly pull against one another. The novel tests the relationship between effective action and moral responsibility, including the harm done by those who claim to act for humanity.

The account comes from [Andrei Semipalov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#andrei-semipalov), an engineer who becomes a commander, Gamov's deputy, and an important maker of policy. He is an active participant with ambitions and loyalties of his own. He admires Gamov, protests against him, carries out decisions he opposes, and sometimes discovers that his sincere reactions have served a plan concealed from him. His retrospective narration can explain what he learned later while preserving the fear or certainty he felt at the time. A reported execution, a public declaration, or an accusation must therefore be read with attention to who knows what. Some apparent reversals reveal deception or mistaken belief; others remain unexplained.

Private relationships give these political choices their human cost. Semipalov's marriage to Elena suffers as public duty absorbs his life. Friendship becomes command, trust becomes an instrument of intelligence work, and compassion can become a means of securing obedience. The contrast between Gonzalez's role in terror and Pustovoyt's role in mercy is especially important, but their public reputations do not disclose everything they do. The [character catalog](docs/knowledge/characters.md) follows changing offices, relationships, and identities. Speeches about conscience, patriotism, religion, and sacrifice remain arguments made by particular characters. Their force, and even their practical success, do not make every premise an established fact or a direct statement of the author's position.

The [final chapter](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c15) also explains the narrative frame. After an involuntary journey to another world, Semipalov records the history of his life with Gamov for the authorities holding him under military supervision. They see strategic value in his society's inventions. His effort to explain a struggle for peace may thus supply another divided world with means of domination. The novel ends as he opens a meeting with its government; the result of that meeting is not shown. His later thoughts about people left behind remain hopes or conjectures. Connections between the worlds, and claims about Gamov's origin, require the same distinction between observation, testimony, and interpretation.

Use the chapter summaries for the sequence of events and the [knowledge catalog](docs/knowledge/index.md) for related evidence. The [timeline](docs/knowledge/timeline.md) helps separate the order of events from later recollections and revelations. The [continuity register](docs/knowledge/continuity.md) distinguishes possible errors, numerical tensions, deliberate mysteries, character deception, and later resolutions. An open flag identifies a limit in the evidence; it does not automatically establish a contradiction. Chapter links lead to the source map and detailed part ledgers so that a claim can be checked in context. The descriptive English chapter headings are editorial reading aids; the supplied novel numbers its chapters. The summaries preserve the story's unfolding discoveries while the catalog provides a place to compare them across the whole book.


## Part 1 — The Impulse to Power

<a id="p1-c01"></a>
### Part 1, Chapter 1 — The Thursday Warning

Source: [P1-C01 source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p1-c01) ([source text](src/text/part0002.html)).

Andrei Semipalov first meets [Alexei Gamov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#alexei-gamov) at Gottlieb Bar's weekly Thursday gathering. Gamov bursts into the room with an ordinary, compact appearance and the manner of an enraged bull. His abrupt shifts between anger, warmth, mockery, and gravity are already a deliberate rhetorical weapon. Bar, the literary cynic and chief engineer who hosts the meetings, answers Gamov's question about whether he likes something with a flat “no,” while Gamov insists that the latest news means war will begin the next day. The quarrel reveals the political crisis: Arthur Marutzian has promised Patina support in its old dispute with Lamaria over three border villages. Gamov calls the promise reckless and predicts a world war; the others dismiss him because no visible mobilization or material preparation exists.

The gathering introduces the men who will shape the later drama. [John Woodworth](docs/knowledge/characters.md#john-woodworth), a Cortezian expatriate in Latania and a committed party man, defends Marutzian. [Nikolai Pustovoyt](docs/knowledge/characters.md#nikolai-pustovoyt), then a mild-voiced building-office accountant, asks why a local quarrel should become war; the narrator later identifies that gentleness as the truest part of the future Minister of Mercy. [Arkady Gonzalez](docs/knowledge/characters.md#arkady-gonzalez) appears beautiful but is described retrospectively as a fanatic of cruelty. [Kazimir Shtupa](docs/knowledge/characters.md#kazimir-shtupa), a military meteorologist, reports no dangerous weather changes and a stable two-day forecast. Bar argues that modern war is a meteorological contest, while Gamov calls the coming conflict purposeless and criminal. His first sustained speech overwhelms Semipalov emotionally: the narrator is not yet a declared follower, but recognizes that he is ready to become one.

After the meeting, Gamov and Semipalov walk through Latania's unsafe outskirts. Gamov predicts that Patina will betray its ally after its first defeat. They find five gang members assaulting two women. Semipalov faces two attackers at a time; Gamov takes on a larger man with a knife, wrenching his arm and biting his chin with shocking ferocity. Woodworth arrives and helps end the fight. Gamov then releases the two injured men after ordering them to pale, lose their voices, and run at the sight of him. He treats words as physical force and says that Marutzian's speech will become machines, fire, ash, and the deaths of women and children. The retrospective narrator connects the threats with Gamov's later punishments, but explicitly says that he did not foresee those consequences that night. Gamov's exact political predictions remain an [unresolved question](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c001), not proof of secret information.

Semipalov returns home after midnight to find his estranged wife [Elena](docs/knowledge/characters.md#elena) asleep on the sofa. She has been away for a month, but says she cannot live with him and cannot live without him. They first met twelve years earlier; the marriage duration is unstated, and the relationship remains loving, hostile, and unstable. Before dawn, an unforecast hurricane strikes. The news reports that Patina has attacked Lamaria, that Lamaria has sought help from Cortezia and Roder, that Cortezia has activated its overseas weather stations, and that Marutzian has called volunteers aged eighteen to fifty-five. The power fails while the storm drowns out the broadcast. Gamov's warning has come true within hours, while the unexplained storm leaves [continuity item C002](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c002) open.

<a id="p1-c02"></a>
### Part 1, Chapter 2 — The War Turns

Source: [P1-C02 source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p1-c02) ([source text and chapter anchor](src/text/part0002.html#1T18G-e57e33ea454c44b1aa8a6263a5c5ad60)).

The war initially appears to vindicate the alliance. Patina defeats Lamaria, captures Lama, and crosses into Roder, Cortezia's principal ally. Roder has disarmed for three decades but still fights with discipline. Latania's army waits on the border while rapidly formed volunteer divisions enter Patina, and Marutzian delays the order to invade Roder. Cortezia's president [Amin Amentola](docs/knowledge/characters.md#amin-amentola) sends numbered regular units and weapons through Roder's ports. The contrast with Latania's encouraging names for volunteer formations exposes the political language that [Gamov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#alexei-gamov) had warned would disguise violence.

After the first victories, allied leaders gather in [Adan](docs/knowledge/places.md#adan), Latania's capital, for banquets and Marutzian's three speeches per day. The coalition includes Nordag, Great Lepin, Sobrana, Patina, and Torbash, whose weak-looking King Knurka IX attends in person and will later prove more perceptive than the other rulers. The conference celebrates universal solidarity and predicts the enemy's defeat; once it ends, the enemy counterattacks. Front lines change hands, and the promised victory becomes real retreat. Elena hears official broadcasts claiming Patina's advance while food queues report abandoned cities. Semipalov admits that political leaders treat truth as an inefficient weapon, a conflict between public claims and lived evidence recorded in [C003](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c003).

During the first months of war, the household and city change. The institute closes at night to prevent sabotage, forcing Semipalov home early and giving the couple more time together. Ration rumors prove accurate: meat disappears for the month because authorities fear a second meteorological attack and a failed harvest. Semipalov explains that [weather generators](docs/knowledge/world.md#weather-generators) can create cyclones but cannot reliably steer them. Shtupa compares an uncontrolled cyclone with a tiger whose critical mass and volume are unknown. Latania's meteorologists repel the first surprise weather attack after one night, but the land offensive drives Patina and Latanian volunteers back, retaking Lama. The war now turns toward the enemy.

Semipalov receives a summons and is made a volunteer captain. His rank rests on a military-science course and field training completed three years earlier, while his laboratory work had kept him from home. Elena is also mobilized to synthesize medicines at a pharmaceutical factory in Adan. At the call-up, [Pavel Prishchepa](docs/knowledge/characters.md#pavel-prishchepa) recruits Semipalov into the Steel Ram division commanded by Pavel's father, General [Leonid Prishchepa](docs/knowledge/characters.md#leonid-prishchepa). The division uses radio-pulse devices, resonance guns, and electro-artillery; Semipalov will serve as a radio saboteur. Pavel reports that Gamov is now a major and deputy technical officer, recognized by Leonid as a born soldier. On their last evening together, Elena and Semipalov share an unusual closeness before she leaves for Adan and he leaves for Patina's wooded mountains. Blackouts, scarce street lighting, nighttime robberies, and women traveling in groups show the war's pressure on civilian life.

Continuity: [C002](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c002) is partly resolved: the first storm is described as a surprise meteorological attack, repelled after one night. The chapter does not name its operators. The compulsory order to become a “volunteer” also exposes the gap between official language and coercion.

<a id="p1-c03"></a>
### Part 1, Chapter 3 — The Barta Position

Source: [P1-C03 source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p1-c03) ([source text](src/text/part0003.html)).

By the third month of Semipalov's service in the [Steel Ram division](docs/knowledge/places.md#steel-ram-division), the army has retreated for two months. The division now holds two wooded hills above the Barta River, which separates Latanian forces from Roder. Semipalov supervises the main electro-barrier and hides its condensed-water energy stores away from the guns. He remembers the Golden Wings division's energy depot exploding a month earlier: water became fire, smoke, and lightning, the unit fled, and Roder's guards reached the exposed line. General [Leonid Prishchepa](docs/knowledge/characters.md#leonid-prishchepa) kept the front continuous through prepared positions. He also rejected Commander-in-Chief Komlin's order to withdraw, choosing to resist and then move only to the next position. A recent resonance strike has left him shaking and chilled, but he refuses hospital evacuation and continues to command.

Gamov's military rise accelerates. Three days before this chapter, he moved from major and deputy technical officer to colonel and deputy division commander. A quiet morning worries Semipalov because enemy weather generators normally silence birds and gather storm clouds before an attack. The capital's broadcast says the western front is worsening and that Latania has been driven from Lamaria by the combined Lamaria–Cortezia–Roder armies. It then calls Steel Ram an impregnable bastion and praises Gamov's attacks. Pavel admits that he supplied the flattering report at Komlin's request, while Leonid demanded that Gamov be named because he sees in him a commander who might save the army. [Pavel Prishchepa](docs/knowledge/characters.md#pavel-prishchepa) now commands divisional reconnaissance as a captain; Semipalov has become a major. The broadcast's exaggeration revisits [C003](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c003), where public praise masks a worsening material position.

A fresh radio message explains the enemy's silence. Patina has requested a separate peace. [Vilkomir Torba](docs/knowledge/characters.md#vilkomir-torba) orders Patina's troops to stop fighting, claiming that Marutzian deceived them with a volunteer army. Leonid calls this treason; Gamov says he warned that Patina would abandon Latania after its first defeat, strengthening [C001](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c001). The map shows Steel Ram nearly trapped: Patina corps stand behind and on both flanks, the battered Golden Wings division lies behind them, and professional Latanian units guard the road to Adan. Leonid orders circular defense. Semipalov proposes a mobile sabotage force for sudden strikes into the encirclement and takes command of it.

The operators reveal a darker political layer. [Albert Peano](docs/knowledge/characters.md#albert-peano), a twenty-two-year-old planner and Marutzian's nephew, is assigned to the front. Rumors say he is out of favor with his uncle, but Semipalov has heard warm conversations between them. Gamov suspects that Marutzian placed Peano in the division so a resonance strike might kill him; Peano says one never tells his uncle what he dislikes. [Arkady Gonzalez](docs/knowledge/characters.md#arkady-gonzalez) remains a capable but war-hating operator and wishes all warmongers hung from one worldwide gallows. Peano's joking answer and Gonzalez's ominous look foreshadow a future political rupture. Their differing accounts of Peano's family relationship remain [C004](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c004), an unresolved character claim.

<a id="p1-c04"></a>
### Part 1, Chapter 4 — The Captive and the Money Convoy

Source: [P1-C04 source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p1-c04) ([source text](src/text/part0004.html)).

Reconnaissance confirms that Roder's troops are replacing Patina's departing units on Steel Ram's flanks. Patina's fourth corps remains behind the division. [Leonid Prishchepa](docs/knowledge/characters.md#leonid-prishchepa) is too ill to direct operations for long, so Gamov issues orders without seeking his approval. The enemy's silence gives the division a short rest while its encirclement forms.

[Pavel](docs/knowledge/characters.md#pavel-prishchepa) brings in a captured Roderian captain, [Birker Shtok](docs/knowledge/characters.md#birker-shtok). Shtok refuses to disclose military information and declares that neither death nor torture can defeat his honor. Gamov attacks that honor instead. He orders Gonzalez to bring a dirty pig, a medical attendant, and recording equipment. He threatens to drug Shtok into submission, force him to kiss the pig's rear, and broadcast the scene in both countries. Gonzalez eagerly arranges the threat. Shtok breaks before receiving an injection and answers clearly. Gamov explains that his aim is to make war disgusting: the supposed nobility of war already excuses murdered children and destroyed art. Semipalov recalls the street fight, but still does not understand that Gamov is developing a coherent [doctrine of humiliation](docs/knowledge/world.md#humiliation-doctrine).

Shtok reports that Patina is negotiating entry into the war against Latania. The fourth corps retains its weapons so that it can attack Steel Ram and Golden Wings from behind; the other corps are being withdrawn and disarmed until the agreement is settled. He also describes pressure on the Central Front, where two professional Latanian armies protect the road to Adan. These are a prisoner's reports, not yet independently observed outcomes.

His immediate mission supplies an opportunity. Two vehicles carrying three hundred million kalons from Adan to Patina's government have been captured. Lieutenant Colonel [Peter Parp](docs/knowledge/characters.md#peter-parp) is escorting the money toward Roder. Shtok, Parp's deputy, was caught while returning from arrangements for Patina's disarmament. Their road passes at least thirty kilometres west of Steel Ram, beyond its electro-guns' range. Semipalov proposes a night raid with his sabotage detachment, Pavel's scouts, and Gamov.

Shtok asks to be shot because he has betrayed his oath. Gamov refuses: he expects to need him alive. He then tells Peano to secure a national broadcast praising Steel Ram's heroic resistance under Prishchepa and Gamov. No assault or full encirclement has yet occurred. Gamov calls the invented report anticipation, while the retrospective narrator marks a political purpose that the others do not yet see. [C003](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c003) now records Gamov's own deliberate use of propaganda.

<a id="p1-c05"></a>
### Part 1, Chapter 5 — Victory for Cash

Source: [P1-C05 source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p1-c05) ([source text](src/text/part0005.html)).

The sabotage detachment crosses the Barta at night in inflatable suits, leaving its crossing equipment concealed on the bank. [Pavel's](docs/knowledge/characters.md#pavel-prishchepa) secret Small Field Videoscope tracks Parp's slow-moving regiment and lets the raiders adjust their route. Meanwhile, Golden Wings is under attack. Its commander, [Filipp Korkin](docs/knowledge/characters.md#filipp-korkin), still promises to hold, but Leonid distrusts his habitual optimism. Marshal [Anton Komlin](docs/knowledge/characters.md#anton-komlin) orders Steel Ram to remain on the Barta, limiting its ability to help.

At a forest halt, Gamov explains why he sought national publicity: fame creates freedom of action, making it harder for the marshal to bully or remove commanders. Semipalov divides the detachment into three groups. Pavel attacks the advance battalion, Semipalov cuts off the rear, and Gamov captures the central money vehicles. At dawn, the surprise attack succeeds. The advance and rear units flee; the money guard resists longer. Resonance fire produces terrible suffering. Semipalov orders treatment for a young enemy soldier, then ensures that the wounded from both sides receive aid. The raiders capture the money, weapons, and 350 prisoners.

Gamov opens the money packages and assembles the soldiers. Declaring that they have earned a share of national property through danger and blood, he knowingly breaks regulations and pays [combat rewards](docs/knowledge/world.md#combat-rewards). He sets two hundred kalons per enemy killed and one thousand per prisoner, pays for captured weapons, gives two thousand to each wounded soldier, and promises ten thousand to each dead soldier's family. The troops cheer; Pavel supports him without reservation. Semipalov objects but sees that an attempt to stop the distribution could destroy discipline.

During the return march, Gamov argues that money can reinforce courage, duty, and the wish to survive for one's family. He also states his larger aim: to end war permanently by destroying its reputation for nobility. Semipalov identifies a central [tension in this program](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c005): payments make fighting profitable, while humiliation is supposed to make it repulsive. Gamov distinguishes rewards from plunder and says he is still searching for the rules of a war against war. Semipalov refuses to endorse his breach of authority, although he will not openly oppose him. Wounded soldiers plan to send money home and threaten to destroy it rather than surrender it; one threatens anyone who tries to retrieve it. The rewards bind their interests to Gamov's survival in command.

Back at headquarters late that evening, Leonid neither approves nor reverses the distribution. The larger emergency now dominates. Golden Wings has been driven toward the sea and risks destruction. Komlin's message admits that Korkin's reports disguise the collapse, yet orders Steel Ram only to strengthen its own defenses. Leonid asks permission to help. Semipalov prepares to dismantle the electro-barrier for a rescue operation, while Gamov drafts another triumphant broadcast about the convoy raid. Peano observes that the marshal's need for good news may turn this publicity against him.

<a id="p1-c06"></a>
### Part 1, Chapter 6 — The Price of Courage

Source: [P1-C06 source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p1-c06) ([source text](src/text/part0006.html)).

By morning, Golden Wings has surrendered. Gamov expects Roder to remove the prisoners before attacking Steel Ram from north and south, with Patina's fourth corps potentially joining from the east. The fortified western front across the Barta is, paradoxically, the least likely direction of attack.

The cash rewards create an immediate internal crisis. About two hundred soldiers gather at the money vehicles and demand equal shares. Their spokesman, Private [Semyon Serbin](docs/knowledge/characters.md#semyon-serbin), argues that everyone has endured marches, trenches, and danger. Gamov insists that rewards must reflect actual deeds and promises future payments. [Varella](docs/knowledge/characters.md#varella), one of the raiders, challenges Serbin's record. As Serbin tries to lead a rush, Semipalov prevents the guards from firing into the crowd and prepares his own force to intervene. The raiders seize Serbin.

Gamov rejects execution and orders Serbin thrown into the latrine trench. The crowd's solidarity dissolves into laughter at the filthy, struggling man. Gamov then orders that Serbin wash himself and his clothes, and says he can still earn a reward in battle. He explains that shooting the leader might have inspired resistance; ridicule makes the same fate unattractive. This extends his [humiliation doctrine](docs/knowledge/world.md#humiliation-doctrine) from an enemy prisoner to his own troops. Semipalov initially thinks Gamov's dark mood is shame, but Gamov says he is thinking about their military predicament.

Semipalov proposes a way out. The captured Golden Wings troops will pass within about fifty kilometres. Steel Ram can break west through the weakly held river line, free them, combine the two divisions into a corps, and then fight east toward the main army. The other officers support the plan. [Leonid](docs/knowledge/characters.md#leonid-prishchepa) hesitates because Komlin has ordered him to hold, then explicitly chooses the breakout. Its success depends on Pavel's precise reconnaissance.

Semipalov also withdraws his objections to continued rewards: canceling Gamov's promise would damage morale before the dangerous operation. Gamov, Peano, Gonzalez, and Semipalov draw up a formal [price list](docs/knowledge/world.md#combat-rewards). Captured equipment earns twice the rate for destroyed equipment; a living prisoner earns five times the rate for killing someone of the same rank. Gonzalez protests that prisoners are a burden and potential future enemies. Gamov answers that saving a coerced enemy soldier preserves a human life, reduces revenge, and encourages surrender. Peano and Semipalov support him. All four now accept the new methods, although they do not yet understand the full scale of Gamov's aims. The [tension between incentives and anti-war purpose](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c005) gains a clear humanitarian argument, without disappearing.

<a id="p1-c07"></a>
### Part 1, Chapter 7 — A Division Freed, a Corps Created

Source: [P1-C07 source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p1-c07) ([source text](src/text/part0007.html)).

[Leonid Prishchepa](docs/knowledge/characters.md#leonid-prishchepa) refuses to sign a permanent reward scheme, although he tolerated the first distribution. Gamov signs it himself. The soldiers study the tariff, imagine homes and support for their families, and volunteer to protect the cash. The narrator says these volunteers may include men from the earlier mob. Their eagerness for action rises. Leonid circulates a false rumor that an army is coming to help; Gamov later tells the troops their real task is to rescue Golden Wings. Their anger at abandonment turns toward the marshal and ministers.

[Peano](docs/knowledge/characters.md#albert-peano) develops the operation. Semipalov's stronger mobile regiment will block the prisoners' road forward; Pavel's smaller force will block retreat; Gamov's two heavily armed regiments will destroy the escort. Leonid will coordinate. Pavel lends Semipalov a paired [secret transmitter numbered 77](docs/knowledge/world.md#paired-transmitter), said to prevent interception. Its clear contact helps the separated commanders act together.

At 8:15 in the evening, Steel Ram's electro-guns open two paths through the opposite bank. Semipalov crosses between nine and ten-thirty and marches into the enemy rear. He notices a pine vibrating under resonance damage and cannot help it, extending his horror of war to the living landscape. The next night his exhausted regiment reaches a deserted village and nearby hills beside the prisoners' road. Pavel assures him that the surrounding population has been evacuated and no enemy field units are nearby. Gamov delays his own crossing to hold enemy attention while the blocking forces take position.

The escort arrives the following evening. Semipalov fears that guards may mix with prisoners and make supporting fire impossible, but their formation lets him strike the leading troops. The enemy checks his first attack and begins a methodical assault next morning, concentrating overwhelming strength against successive positions. Semipalov yields ground and tries to last until dark. Pavel attacks the rear, improving morale but drawing no troops away from the main pressure. Late in the day Gamov's heavy guns arrive. Within half an hour the escort's command orders surrender.

The rescued soldiers embrace their liberators and return to their old units. Their former guards become their prisoners. [Korkin](docs/knowledge/characters.md#filipp-korkin), badly injured by resonance, says Komlin rejected repeated pleas for help while citing Steel Ram's supposedly continuous heroic defense. Leonid rejects that account of events on the Barta: the earlier heroic reports had invented battles. Gamov proposes combining both divisions into a corps under Leonid, but Leonid instead appoints Gamov commander. He retains Steel Ram, takes Korkin as deputy, and proposes Semipalov to lead Golden Wings. These are field appointments awaiting higher approval.

Two enemy corps are now converging. Gamov plans one day to organize, then an attack eastward before they combine. To protect the new appointments, he dictates a public report that names the commanders, admits the reward payments, blames headquarters for abandoning Golden Wings, and demands relief. Leonid hears a declaration of war on military command. The narrator sees the wider challenge to the government, especially Marutzian. Peano's immediate feelings remain [uncertain](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c004), despite his later loyalty to Gamov.

<a id="p1-c08"></a>
### Part 1, Chapter 8 — Reconciliation and an Execution

Source: [P1-C08 source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p1-c08) ([source text](src/text/part0008.html)).

The next distribution of rewards leaves one money vehicle two-thirds empty. After a formal corps review, soldiers push [Serbin](docs/knowledge/characters.md#semyon-serbin) forward with the money he has now earned for two resonators, an officer, and a wound. Gamov embraces him; Serbin drops the notes and sobs, while the troops erupt in approval. Semipalov sees a remarkable bond form between commander and soldiers. He cannot explain why the same crowd that enjoyed Serbin's humiliation now celebrates his restoration. He wonders whether the reconciliation also absolves their own betrayal. The retrospective narrator warns that Serbin too gains a future power whose sinister consequences no one yet understands.

The second event brings [Danilo Mordasov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#danilo-mordasov), an emissary of Marutzian and personal representative of Komlin, by waterplane. Although initially called a general, he identifies himself as a state councillor. He praises the officers' national fame, then gives a grim report: two hundred thousand prisoners lost on the Western Front, a defense stabilized partly by enemy mistakes, food reserves diverted to armies and allies, crops damaged as Cortezia intercepts rain-bearing cyclones, and banditry fed by desertion. These are Mordasov's reports of conditions beyond the corps' direct observation.

Gamov forces him to state his mission. He must recover both the remaining cash and the rewards already given to soldiers; confirmation of Gamov's command is held out as an inducement. Gamov says confiscation will break morale before the breakout and calls the demand betrayal. Mordasov insists that soldiers must fight from patriotism under the same rules as other armies. [Peano](docs/knowledge/characters.md#albert-peano) intervenes, alleging that the money has already been written off and will enrich officials' families. Mordasov then reveals Marutzian's instruction to arrest his supposedly unreliable nephew if he resists. This supports the earlier evidence of [family hostility](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c004), while Peano's corruption charge remains an allegation.

Mordasov says Komlin ordered him to return with the money or die. Gamov takes that formula literally, refuses the money, and sentences him to death for betraying the army. Gonzalez advances with a pulsator; Mordasov draws a hand resonator, but Gonzalez kills him first with a beam. Leonid watches without intervening. The corps has now crossed from insubordination into a direct conflict with the government.

Gamov immediately prepares a defensive public account. He portrays Mordasov as an infiltrating saboteur who falsely claimed government authority and slandered the beloved leaders. This lets him repeat Mordasov's damaging criticism of Komlin and Marutzian while formally defending them. He promises the corps will obey orders that lead to victory. Pavel confirms that he recorded the entire meeting, giving substance to Gamov's offer of evidence to an inquiry. The manipulation extends [C003's propaganda record](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c003). Once Gamov finishes, Leonid offers his hand and pledges support in everything, to the end.

<a id="p1-c09"></a>
### Part 1, Chapter 9 — The Offer of Power

Source: [P1-C09 source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p1-c09) ([source text](src/text/part0009.html)).

After a week of almost continuous fighting, the corps breaks through to friendly forces. Rewards empty both cash vehicles. Headquarters orders the troops to [Zabon](docs/knowledge/places.md#zabon) for replenishment and reorganization. Before departure, a delegation including Serbin, Sergeant Serov, and [Grigory Varella](docs/knowledge/characters.md#varella) promises to protect the commanders and resist disarmament or confiscation. Gamov warns against mutiny, then learns that Pavel, Peano, and Gonzalez have secretly organized the soldiers. Varella is Pavel's man. Peano argues that the government may disguise destruction of the corps as routine reorganization. Semipalov and Gamov had not been told of this preparation.

Ten days of public celebrations follow as the corps travels home. At [Parku](docs/knowledge/places.md#parku), where rail lines lead to Zabon and Adan, Elena meets Semipalov with Woodworth's help. The reunion is affectionate, though Semipalov briefly suspects her friendship with Woodworth. She dismisses his jealousy. Woodworth is now Marutzian's adviser on international affairs and commands the officers' train. Gamov meets Elena, compliments her, and says that he is unmarried.

On the slow train, Woodworth explains that transport has lost its supply of condensed-water fuel and locomotives are reverting to coal and oil. After Elena leaves the meeting, he apologizes to Gamov for defending Marutzian at Bar's gathering: working directly for the leader has changed his judgment. He reveals a plan to separate the officers from their loyal soldiers, promote them, disperse them among new assignments, and dissolve the corps. He has refused his part. The raiding battalion now shares the commanders' train, the divisions remain organized, and their weapons remain available.

Woodworth also exposes preparations before Mordasov's death. Pavel, Peano, and Gonzalez had secretly isolated the envoy's guards before the headquarters meeting. Pavel confirms this but says Gamov, Semipalov, and both generals were not informed. Thus Woodworth's inference of a prior collective death sentence is broader than the facts confirmed. Woodworth states that the recovered money was already allocated as secret government bonuses, including a share for himself, and that Peano was to be removed from the front. He thinks prison awaited Peano. This strengthens the [corruption and family-hostility evidence](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c004), while preserving its source.

Woodworth then asks Gamov to take supreme power and save the country. He promises support and says the people will welcome him. When he calls his trusted guards, Varella appears instead: Pavel has already disarmed and replaced Woodworth's men against the possibility of another hostile envoy. Woodworth is delighted by their readiness and offers an explanation for preserving the corps: his orders prohibited provoking a mutiny.

Gamov does not deliver a formal answer. Semipalov and the others begin practical planning on the basis of his evident intent, and he lets them proceed. Before leaving, Gamov unexpectedly asks whether Semipalov is jealous of Elena. He denies romantic intent. The narrator identifies the question as preparation for a much later, still-uncreated situation; its purpose remains [unresolved](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c008).

<a id="p1-c10"></a>
### Part 1, Chapter 10 — The Seizure of Power

Source: [P1-C10 source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p1-c10) ([source text](src/text/part0010.html)).

The officers travel from Zabon to [Adan](docs/knowledge/places.md#adan), officially to report to the government and actually to seize power. Semipalov has planned the operation; Pavel coordinates agents in the capital through his secret communications system. The city is visibly diminished: most shops are shut, queues stand outside the rest, and the absence of evacuated children makes the streets unnaturally quiet. Soldiers in civilian clothes are already in position. Woodworth meets the party at the government building and checks that the plan is proceeding.

Before Gamov's report, the officers hear the state's problems discussed. Energy-water production is inadequate. [Shtupa](docs/knowledge/characters.md#kazimir-shtupa) warns that Cortezia is building powerful weather stations in Roder and Lamaria. Its strategy is to withhold rain in spring and flood the fields in summer. Latania has resisted but cannot prevent a wet harvest. Marutzian agrees to further civilian ration cuts while protecting military supplies. The worn, aged chairman contrasts with the energized but uncomprehending Komlin.

As Gamov speaks, Pavel reports that the railway station and broadcast station are held and heavy vibrators cover the security troops' barracks. The seizure of telephone communications follows. Gamov refuses a ceremonial account of victories and accuses high command of abandoning the two divisions. Komlin calls him a self-appointed commander and threatens to deny promotion. Marutzian tries to turn the debate into a comparison between Gamov's success and Korkin's defeat, disclosing that Korkin has been demoted.

[Colonel Morokhov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#morokhov), head of government security, reports the loss of communications. Gamov then produces a document assigning the recovered money to senior officials, including two million kalons for Komlin. When the marshal demands arrests, he cannot contact the security police. Internal palace communications also fail, making a routine city fault implausible. Varella leads armed soldiers into the chamber; Serov and Serbin are among them. Guards stand behind the ministers. Varella tears off Komlin's shoulder boards and forces him to sit under threat of resonance fire.

Gamov demands that Marutzian transfer power to him, offering no trial if he complies quietly. Marutzian initially refuses and challenges him to an open prosecution. [Peano](docs/knowledge/characters.md#albert-peano) then approaches with a pulsator and gives his uncle one minute to agree. Marutzian says he should have imprisoned Peano instead of sending him to the front, directly confirming their [hostile relationship](docs/knowledge/continuity.md#c004). As Peano counts, he submits.

Woodworth supplies a prepared resignation text, and the camera crew is already present. Under Peano's warning against subversive tone or gestures, Marutzian calmly tells the country that he voluntarily gives power to the younger, nonparty Colonel Alexei Gamov to improve the war effort and civilian life. Gamov briefly accepts and promises a government and program after studying conditions. Semipalov orders the former ministers taken away. He mistakes Komlin's shock for permanent mental collapse; his later narration explicitly corrects that judgment. Gamov expresses dizziness at the leap into the unknown and thanks Semipalov. The political seizure has succeeded through Semipalov's plan, Pavel's control of key sites, Woodworth's access, and the soldiers' force.

## Part 2 — Sacred Terror

<a id="p2-c01"></a>
### Part 2, Chapter 1 — Gold, Humiliation, and a Dictator

One week after the change of government, the men who seized the palace are already running the country, although no formal cabinet exists. Cortezian and Roderan weather attacks test their strength. Flooding has turned Adan's streets into rivers; military meteorologists have finally pushed the rain a hundred leagues away, but a dry electrical storm still hangs over the capital. Semipalov asks Gamov to state the new regime's philosophy. Gamov says its philosophy must emerge from experience and offers an immediate program instead. ([Source and chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p2-c01); [government and weather evidence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c01).)

Gamov proposes to release the weapons and ammunition that Marshal Komlin stored for three years of war. A sudden offensive can regain the initiative, but Semipalov warns that spending the reserves could win a battle and lose the war. Gamov therefore wants military production raised by roughly half. He will reward workers who exceed their quotas with a new gold currency and access to scarce, high-quality goods from state reserves. Woodworth predicts that people will hoard the gold. Gamov welcomes even this outcome: transferring idle gold into private hands will already have purchased extra production. Woodworth also proposes permanent gold rewards for military exploits, so that soldiering offers the prospect of enrichment that now attracts men to banditry. ([Economic and military program](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c01).)

The group agrees to abandon costly alliances. Gamov hopes former allies will seek Cortezian aid and disperse the enemy's resources. Against domestic crime, he proposes public, disgusting humiliation, citing his earlier punishment of Semyon Serbin. The others hesitate because the method itself seems criminal; Semipalov is the first to accept it. Experienced officials will remain, but must submit secret confessions of past misconduct. Disclosed offenses receive an amnesty; concealed offenses bring full punishment and public exposure. When Semipalov asks what title can express Gamov's immense power, Gamov chooses “Dictator.” ([Coercion, administration, and the new title](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c01).)

<a id="p2-c02"></a>
### Part 2, Chapter 2 — The First Address and the Sacred Terror

Semipalov and Elena now live in an Adan hotel, leaving their Zabon apartment locked. On a walk to their work, they hear citizens doubt the coup, complain of shortages, and fear robbers. Elena calls Gamov a dreamer. Semipalov brings these complaints to the governing group, which presses Gamov to explain its program. He hesitates because a ruler's spoken promise becomes an obligation, then agrees to address the country that evening. His insistence on formal names and forms of address has already separated public duty from their old friendships. ([Source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p2-c02); [household and public mood](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c02).)

Gamov's two-hour broadcast overcomes even Semipalov's prepared skepticism. He speaks as if privately to each listener. First he accuses the population of sharing responsibility for defeat and hunger, while assigning greater blame to the old government. Then he explains the apathy behind poor production: extra work buys little, quotas rise, crops face destruction, and families cannot move safely through the streets. He promises to release military and energy reserves, protect the harvest, freeze production quotas until the war ends, and pay above-quota work in gold-backed **lats**, exchangeable for reserve goods in special shops. Military exploits will receive rewards by achievement rather than rank. These are announced policies and assurances; the speech does not establish their results. ([Currency and production rules](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c02).)

He then declares the **Sacred Terror**. Repeated murderers already in prison are to be shot; other inmates are to enter harsh northern labor or penal battalions, and prisons are to disappear. Bandits who do not surrender face degrading death. Gang leaders will be drowned in excrement before stereo cameras. Their parents must witness the execution, then lose their property and undergo forced labor; those who benefit from stolen goods also face confiscation and exile. Police who continue protecting criminals will be hanged outside their stations, and their families expelled. Gamov admits that these methods are barbarous and criminal. His defense is that disproportionate penalties will make crime intolerably costly. He promises women safe streets by winter. Semipalov presents the speech's emotional conquest of the audience as the beginning of Gamov's reputed “devilish magic.” ([Terror orders and the narrator's interpretation](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c02).)

Gamov names an unelected, irremovable ten-person **Core**, with ordinary elected and accountable ministers to follow. Semipalov becomes deputy dictator and war minister; Bar handles organization, Woodworth foreign relations, Peano army command, Shtupa weather, Pavel state protection, Gonzalez Terror, Pustovoyt Mercy, and Omar Isiro information. Elena doubts whether Semipalov can remain loyal in a role wholly subordinate to Gamov. As an approaching enemy cyclone ends the weather respite, Semipalov embraces her. His greater fear is the unpredictable future. ([Core appointments and closing scene](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c02).)

<a id="p2-c03"></a>
### Part 2, Chapter 3 — Rewards, Executions, and International Courts

The cyclone lasts more than a week, flooding western lands but failing to destroy the grain-growing steppes. Floods also stop the enemy advance. Pavel reports that the enemy has spent over two-thirds of its stored energy-water, giving Latania a respite until winter. Bar takes Semipalov to a new currency shop. Its abundant reserve goods contrast with the poor ration department. Workers can already buy with certificates for extra output, though some prefer to wait for gold coins. Government members receive none of the new currency. ([Source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p2-c03); [weather and incentives](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c03).)

Semipalov then shows Bar the hanging body of police chief Anton Karmanyuk. He concealed serious corruption in his confession and later arranged two bandits' escape. Gonzalez ordered his death without a trial; Pustovoyt protested, but Semipalov upheld the sentence. Karmanyuk's parents and wife have been exiled and his three sons sent to military school. Privately, Semipalov remembers that one escapee was Karmanyuk's brother-in-law, whom he released after his wife's pleas. Semipalov struggled to sign and reduced Gonzalez's proposed drowning in excrement to hanging. Yet he knows he will hesitate less next time. Another recalled dispute shows Mercy's limited restraint: Pustovoyt defeats Gonzalez's demand to intern everyone ever imprisoned by demonstrating the prohibitive cost of guards. ([Terror and its checks](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c03).)

At the palace, Gamov permits opposition leader Konstantin Fagusta to revive *The Tribune*, despite Fagusta's promise of relentless criticism. The government's *Herald of Terror and Mercy* will be edited by Pimen Georgiu, a former Marutzian loyalist and Fagusta's bitter enemy. Gamov then proposes international **Black and White Courts**, initially funded with ten billion lats divided equally. The Black Court will identify those who organize, finance, or glorify war, warn them, judge proven guilt, and pay agents to execute its sentences abroad. Pavel offers intelligence channels to deliver gold; Woodworth expects paid executioners to be available in Cortezia. The White Court will fund aid and redress. Gamov insists that mercy must prevail over terror, but Semipalov points to Karmanyuk as a contrary example. Gamov's further plan for equal enemy participation and funding meets Semipalov's disbelief. ([Press and court proposals](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c03).)

In a restricted meeting, Pavel suspects foreign-ministry expert Jean Voityuk of spying through Torbash's ambassador, Shirbai Shar. Voityuk's wife wears a valuable necklace missing from Shar's family collection. Semipalov observes that a private sexual affair could explain the gift. Gamov therefore orders a test: feed Voityuk news of a northern offensive while the real attack is planned in the south. Semipalov objects that enemy preparations might become an offensive against Zabon; Gamov accepts the risk and orders Woodworth to plant the information. ([Espionage evidence and military risk](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c03).)

Finally, Gamov asks to make Elena Bar's deputy, ostensibly to represent the women who form two-thirds of the home-front population. Semipalov recognizes a concealed purpose involving his jealousy. Gamov promises an explanation after Voityuk is exposed but requires Elena's appointment first. Semipalov agrees while suspecting that Gamov's real plan predates the spy case. ([Elena's appointment and unresolved purpose](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c03).)

<a id="p2-c04"></a>
### Part 2, Chapter 4 — The Opposition as an Instrument of Fear

The first issues of the government *Herald of Terror and Mercy* and Fagusta's *Tribune* cause a sensation. Both emerge from the same printing house by Gamov's permission, but one threatens punishment while the other attacks the regime. The *Herald* announces the Black and White Courts, with five billion lats in gold for each and an invitation for all countries, including enemies, to join. Gonzalez summons a hundred prominent enemy figures to Adan, from President Amin Amentola to the banker and arms-industry leader Eduard Konveizer. Death alone excuses nonattendance. He also warns war-promoting journalists, clergy, and industrialists. ([Source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p2-c04); [public court notices](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c04).)

The Black Court sentences eighty-four alleged war criminals to death in absentia, including bombers of civilian cities, punitive officers, clergy who bless bombs, and prisoner-camp killers. Anyone may execute a sentence for an enormous reward: at least 100,000 lats, 300,000 for an airman, and 500,000 for the camp commandant. Gonzalez states that killing without a court sentence remains banditry. Pustovoyt announces that the White Court can briefly suspend sentences, hear appeals, and provide defense counsel. Citizens of participating countries can also appoint their own advocates. ([Sentences, rewards, and appeals](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c04).)

Fagusta argues that Gamov corrupts war's remaining moral restraints by turning wounds, killing, and bereavement into private profit. He challenges judgments made without hearing the accused, mocks the impossible demand that enemy citizens travel through battle lines for trial, and predicts that bounty killing will recruit professional criminals and endanger innocent bystanders. His criticism extends to domestic Terror. A recently executed gang leader murdered a family, but the regime also deported his elderly parents and a young woman who had dated him for only a week and said she knew nothing of his crimes. Fagusta can accept severe punishment of the murderer under wartime conditions; he cannot accept punishment of the innocent. He ends by warning that justice will eventually hold today's executioners to account. ([Fagusta's argument and the reported execution](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c04).)

Semipalov demands Fagusta's arrest, as Gonzalez has already done. He concedes that the facts are true but calls their interpretation enemy sabotage. Gamov refuses. Terror, he explains, works through public fear rather than the amount of blood shed. Fagusta's moral force and vivid descriptions spread that fear more effectively than official notices. Crime has declined, although the expected voluntary surrenders have not yet begun. Asked whether Fagusta showed him the article before sending it to print, Gamov carefully repeats that precise denial. The retrospective narrator marks the wording as significant but does not yet explain it. ([Gamov's use of criticism and the unresolved wording](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c04).)

<a id="p2-c05"></a>
### Part 2, Chapter 5 — Making Enemies and Exposing Zabon

Allied rulers arrive in Adan expecting the aid that Marutzian exchanged for speeches and promises. Knurka IX of Torbash arrives first, accompanied by Shirbai Shar; Semipalov finds the small king unusually perceptive. Great Lepin's President Lon Chudin stages a grand musical entrance and carefully observes rank. After receiving a dismissive greeting, Woodworth irritates him by warmly welcoming his brother Kir Kirun. Semipalov withdraws from ceremonial duties to prepare the offensive. Reserve weapons are reaching the army, whose misleading “volunteer” designation is abolished. At the formal dinner and ball, Elena appears publicly as a deputy minister and speaks about the human cost of war. ([Source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p2-c05); [visitors and changing roles](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c05).)

Before the conference, Bar reports rising production. Workers seek overtime, gold coins are hoarded, and convertible notes buy reserve goods. Output is approaching a thirty-percent increase. Energy-water remains the critical shortage. Shtupa warns that autumn and winter weather attacks will overwhelm Latania unless supplies double. Gamov gives new energy-water factories the highest priority and orders enhanced wages, paid entirely in the new currency. ([Production results and energy risk](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c05).)

The allies demand weapons, food, hard currency, and even Latanian soldiers; Great Lepin requests two thousand fine wool carpets for barracks. Semipalov supports paying only for actual assistance. Woodworth warns that refusing aid will turn nominal friends into effective enemies and surround Latania with war. Gamov openly wants the break. Each new enemy will first demand Cortezian support, draining resources from Cortezia's armies. If those states declare war, the Black Court will sentence their leaders and war promoters to death and offer irresistible rewards. Gamov expects rulers who value their own safety above their soldiers to divert still more resources to personal protection. He accepts that professional criminals will carry out the killings. Semipalov supports the policy; Pustovoyt visibly suffers. The press receives only vague information. ([Alliance strategy and its dissenters](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c05).)

The restricted meeting then reviews the Voityuk test. A week earlier, Woodworth told him that a Latanian offensive would move west from Zabon along the coast, potentially alarming neutral Korina across the narrow strait from northern Roder. Pavel has no direct evidence of transmission: Voityuk met a grocer and Shar, neither alone. Yet General Ferdinand Vaksel has arrived, and enemy forces are massing on the northern flank. Pavel treats this as decisive indirect proof. Semipalov instead sees the feared danger becoming real. Zabon's fixed defenses could resist several divisions, but not several corps. Once the enemy recognizes the supposed offensive as a deception, it could use its concentration to capture Latania's second center. Gamov argues that the shift improves the chances of Latania's own main attack. Semipalov cannot persuade him that no gain elsewhere would compensate for losing Zabon, and retrospectively calls this a tragic error. ([Spy evidence, northern threat, and disputed consent](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c05).)

<a id="p2-c06"></a>
### Part 2, Chapter 6 — Refusing Retreat and Staging Betrayal

At the allied conference, Gamov follows Woodworth's advice to avoid needless insult. He thanks the visitors for their sympathy, then announces that Latania will stop supplying weapons, goods, and money because its own economy and army are in crisis. Aid might resume after military recovery. The rulers are stunned. Lon Chudin hides his anger, while Kir Kirun complains to Sobrana's President Mgobo Mardoba. Knurka IX protests that Torbash has risked conflict for Latania's sake. Woodworth answers that no Torbash soldier has actually fought; Knurka admits that his proposed war requires Latanian help to raise an army and a Cortezian landing to provide an enemy. ([Source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p2-c06); [conference and allied positions](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c06).)

The danger Semipalov predicted now materializes. Finding no substantial Latanian force in the north, the enemy advances on Zabon with superior troops and equipment. Semipalov proposes surrendering the city. Holding it would require all reserves intended for the main offensive and sacrifice the wider strategic aims. Letting the enemy commit more forces to Zabon would weaken its center, enabling a Latanian attack to swing west of the invading armies and trap them. Gamov recoils from deliberately sacrificing the city and its people. Semipalov argues that failure to win the wider campaign would kill still more. Woodworth adds that enemy forces near Nordag could break that alliance. Gamov orders Zabon defended and the main offensive begun immediately, hoping pressure in the center will divert the enemy. Semipalov prepares to inspect the city with Peano and Pavel. ([Competing strategies for Zabon](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c06).)

Before departure, Gamov summons Semipalov and Elena for a secret discussion. He wants to continue using Voityuk to mislead enemy intelligence, despite the first operation's dangerous result. Voityuk will move from Woodworth's ministry to Semipalov's staff as an adviser on international affairs. Ordinary friendly indiscretion would not convince the enemy, so Semipalov must appear to be Gamov's rival and leak useful-looking secrets from hostility. Political ambition alone is too weak a motive. Gamov therefore asks Elena to pretend to be his mistress, making her husband's supposed jealousy a credible reason for betrayal. ([The proposed intelligence channel](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c06).)

Semipalov realizes that Gamov's old questions about his jealousy anticipated this scheme before either man knew of Voityuk. He feels that the parts have already been assigned and agrees to the performance. Elena begins to enter her role as a political actress. But the planned fiction creates a real effect: Semipalov remains loyal and obedient, yet feels the first inner coldness toward Gamov. The scheme depends on counterfeit personal hostility and has begun to produce the genuine thing. ([Resolution of Elena's appointment mystery and its cost](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c06).)

<a id="p2-c07"></a>
### Part 2, Chapter 7 — The Deluge and Nordag's Attack

In Zabon, public news accurately shows the worsening front. Fagusta denounces intelligence failures, angering Pavel, who vows to uncover the editor's secrets. Enemy divisions outnumber the defenders five to one. Semipalov again thinks surrender after declaring an open city would protect civilians, but cannot tell the defenders that he doubts their resistance. Peano promises arriving troops and field weather generators. Semipalov sees the problem: Vaksel is closer and can act sooner. ([Source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p2-c07); [public information and military balance](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c07).)

Pavel shows them the intelligence system. Tiny concealed sensors measure the total iron passing along roads, while personal instruments link officers to agents. The instruments show immense enemy traffic southwest of Zabon and a worrying buildup across Nordag's border in the northeast. Semipalov reports both threats, sends Peano back to accelerate operations, and stays to command. Shtupa arrives that night; weather equipment follows by morning. Reinforcements need one week for the first division and two for full concentration, but Shtupa can promise only three or four days of deluge. Semipalov delays the storm to gain time and intends to flood Vaksel's army in the lowlands below Zabon's defensive hills. ([Intelligence and the timing constraint](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c07).)

With all Latania's weather stations supplying cloud masses, Shtupa expects to overpower Vaksel's local countermeasures. Yet Vaksel does not attack the heights as predicted. Pavel discovers that his troops are secretly withdrawing under darkness, leaving fixed weapons and camouflage behind. Semipalov believes Vaksel means to provoke an expensive storm and escape it, so he orders the weather attack at once. Opposing cloud fronts collide in a violent electrical storm. Wind nearly carries Semipalov away from his observation point until Shtupa and an operator drag him into shelter. He then orders the deluge despite Shtupa's warning that enemy resistance will make much of the water fall on Latanian positions. ([Vaksel's withdrawal and the weather battle](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c07).)

The flood wrecks equipment and postpones the enemy offensive, but sealed vehicles and amphibians save much of Vaksel's force. Two days and two nights of rain also inundate Zabon. Gamov demands that the storm pursue the retreating army; Shtupa refuses to spend the remaining safety reserves on moving the clouds so far. He releases control, and the sky clears over devastated woods and mud-covered ground. Semipalov expects the terrain to prevent a renewed attack for at least a week. ([Results and energy limits](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c07).)

Then comes the consequence he failed to anticipate. The storm also flooded Nordag, whose President Franz Putrament declares war on the ground of meteorological aggression. Its prepared forces break through the border and begin encircling Zabon from the northeast. Semipalov recognizes that Vaksel has drawn him into a trap and condemns his failure to act on the earlier sensor warning. Recalled urgently to Adan, he leaves Shtupa temporarily in command of the encircled city's defense and flies out with Pavel. ([Nordag's declaration and the encirclement](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c07).)

<a id="p2-c08"></a>
### Part 2, Chapter 8 — A False Threat Becomes an Order

Semipalov accepts responsibility for the disaster, but Gamov says the leadership shares the blame. Nordag has captured Zabon's outer defenses and food depots. The city holds only two weeks of food, and Putrament publicly promises to starve its population before entering in triumph. Gamov halves Zabon's rations and imposes the same allowance on his government. He stops western offensives and orders troops north to relieve the city within three or four weeks. Peano says six weeks is the minimum needed. Pustovoyt suggests an airlift, but the available twenty waterplanes could add only hours to a large city's survival. ([Source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p2-c08); [siege, food, and relief limits](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c08).)

Gamov instead orders an airborne raid on Nordag's headquarters to capture its command, and perhaps Putrament. Semipalov objects that using the aircraft exposes Latania's secret expansion of waterplane production. Gamov appeals personally to their shared guilt and their duty to prevent starvation. Semipalov consents. The operation is already moving. Meanwhile, Voityuk has joined Semipalov's ministry. Reading his confession, Semipalov again doubts whether the necklace proves espionage: Voityuk's beautiful wife, Anna Kursai, might explain it through an affair. He decides nevertheless to treat Voityuk's bland manner as a possible professional disguise. ([The raid and remaining uncertainty about the spy](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c08).)

In their first interview, Semipalov repairs the earlier deception. He tells Voityuk that the northern offensive was a real plan abandoned for lack of resources, preserving the credibility of Woodworth's leaked information. He then performs dissatisfaction with Gamov's foreign policy and asks for expert advice. After receiving Gamov's coded confirmation that the air operation has begun, he reveals the raid and invents a far greater threat: captured leaders will be starved and drowned in filth, Nordag conquered, its prominent people executed, and the rest of its population deported to northern camps. Voityuk calls this genocide. Semipalov continues to pose as a troubled subordinate who lacks the power or an alternative plan to stop the dictator. He requests a written assessment of international consequences and hints that cabinet unity might break. ([Deliberate disinformation and its purpose](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c08).)

At the airfield, Semipalov reports that Voityuk's reactions have convinced him he is a spy. Gamov says the invented threat has created obligations: the enemy will believe it only if actions follow. The raid has captured roughly eighty people, including eight generals and thirteen colonels; Putrament escaped by leaving headquarters two hours earlier. Gamov orders the prisoners caged in Zabon's main square and fed half the city's reduced ration, a quarter of the former allowance, until the siege ends. If Putrament refuses, they are to be drowned in manure and Nordag's capital attacked from the air. These specific orders make part of Semipalov's fiction real; the chapter does not show adoption of the entire invented extermination plan. Angry but obedient, Semipalov returns to Zabon after a cold farewell. ([Capture results and the actual orders](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c08).)

<a id="p2-c09"></a>
### Part 2, Chapter 9 — Zabon Is Saved, but Mercy Fails

Back in Zabon, Semipalov learns that the capture of Nordag's commanders has stalled its advance, while flooded ground still separates it from Vaksel. The food depots remain inaccessible through heavily defended narrow approaches. Grigory Varella guards the captured officers, with Ivan Serov and Semyon Serbin. Semipalov reads Varella's smile as a sign of mutual forgiveness after the earlier mutiny. They helped seize the headquarters before its occupants could use their weapons. Colonel General Sumo Pariona and Lieutenant General Kinza Vardant protest their rough capture and confinement in a cowshed, demanding accommodation appropriate to their rank. ([Source map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p2-c09); [captives and military position](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c09).)

Their assumption of privilege enrages Semipalov. He announces the public cage, the quarter ration, and permission for guards to suppress any protest with blows, insults, or manure. The treatment will last until Putrament opens access to Zabon's food. Alone afterward, Semipalov is disturbed that he has not merely obeyed a cruel order but embraced it. The generals ask to contact their president. Putrament responds publicly the next day: he will make no concession, demands the prisoners' release and Zabon's surrender, and promises to honor his captive officers' suffering. ([Semipalov's anger and the failed appeal](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c09).)

The prisoners are placed in a brightly lit glass enclosure with an opaque roof, denied privacy even to relieve themselves. Instead of abusing them, the townspeople pass in silence. Vardant's sorrowful look awakens Semipalov's sympathy. With no military solution ready, he anxiously waits for his secret threat to reach Putrament through Voityuk. A public threat could be dismissed as propaganda; an apparent intelligence report of hidden plans should be harder to ignore. He still fears that the supposed spy may be an error. ([The cage and the unproved intelligence gamble](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c09).)

On the following day, Putrament announces a unilateral ceasefire, withdrawal to the old border, and a request for peace talks. Shtupa confirms that Nordag's forces are leaving the depot approaches. Semipalov weeps with relief. Pavel, Gonzalez, and Pustovoyt arrive from Adan; together they see the withdrawal that reopens the city's routes. Privately, Pavel explains the delay: Cortezia had not yet passed Voityuk's report to Putrament when he rejected the generals' first appeal. This is the chapter's reported explanation of the reversal. ([Withdrawal and reported cause](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c09).)

Semipalov intends to give the prisoners humane conditions. Gonzalez instead reads a notice approved by Gamov: in recognition of Putrament's retreat, the Black Court cancels starvation and drowning in filth and orders all captive generals and officers shot, then buried away from the battlefield. Semipalov forbids it. Gonzalez claims court decisions cannot be canceled or appealed and removes Semipalov's military authority by declaring Zabon no longer a war zone and recalling him to Adan. Pustovoyt says his protests secured only decent treatment of the bodies. Semipalov condemns this as the ruin of mercy and leaves, unable to bear his colleagues' presence. The chapter ends with the execution order standing; it does not depict the shooting itself. ([Execution order, failed intervention, and the chapter's limit](docs/knowledge/parts/part-2-ledger.md#p2-c09).)

## Part 3 — Turning Point

<a id="p3-c01"></a>
### Part 3, Chapter 1 — The Price of Terror

[Semipalov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#andrei-semipalov) opens a meeting of the Core by condemning the execution of captured enemy leaders. He threatens to leave the government. [Gamov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#alexei-gamov) answers with Semipalov's own earlier threats, passed to the spy Voityuk. Those threats promised personal vengeance against the men who ordered war. Gamov insists that words must become actions if they are to frighten the enemy. He distinguishes allowing Nordag's army to withdraw from punishing its leaders through Sacred Terror. Pavel Prishchepa reports that, within forty minutes of hearing about the executions, President Putrament ordered his troops to withdraw thirty leagues from the border. The apparent success of the threat defeats Semipalov's protest. He stays in office and sends Putrament a contemptuous rejection of talks about nonexistent border disputes. His opposition has become cooperation ([catalog review](docs/knowledge/parts/part-3-ledger.md#p3-c01)).

The ministers then assess the state's position. Zabon is safe and the enemy offensive has stopped, but the main strategic objectives remain unmet. Two-thirds of Patina has been abandoned, allies are bargaining with Cortezia, and peace before the end of the year is no longer plausible. Bar reports stronger production but depleted reserves and a poor outlook for winter supply. Gamov orders Gonzalez, the Minister of Terror, to destroy the country's bandits before the frosts, giving him troops and control of the police. Pustovoyt, the Minister of Mercy, receives authority to offer leniency to those who surrender and to stop punishments. Semipalov notices the difference between Gamov's hard commands to Gonzalez and his almost parental manner toward Pustovoyt.

In a restricted meeting, Semipalov, Gamov, Woodworth, and Pavel agree to keep using Voityuk as an intelligence channel. Semipalov will give him true information to establish trust. At one decisive moment, he will use that trust to deliver a falsehood capable of changing the war. As the next step, he asks Gamov to delay the public announcement of the anti-bandit campaign so that Voityuk can first receive it as a secret.

At home, Semipalov reads Konstantin Fagusta's attack on the government in the *Tribune*. Fagusta asks why a program aimed at rulers and warmongers has instead produced executions of helpless prisoners. Semipalov recognizes his own suppressed objections in the article. He hates Fagusta because the journalist is right, while he has accepted responsibility for the policy he condemns.

[Source: P3-C01](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p3-c01).

<a id="p3-c02"></a>
### Part 3, Chapter 2 — Mercy Under Threat

During autumn, [Gonzalez](docs/knowledge/characters.md#arkady-gonzalez) dominates the public broadcasts with daily totals of bandits captured, killed, or surrendered. [Pustovoyt](docs/knowledge/characters.md#nikolai-pustovoyt) promises protection to those who give themselves up: the fit will join the army, the sick will receive treatment, and others will receive suitable work. Operation Cleansing advances slowly until the first natural snowfall. Gonzalez then announces three stages: a week without police or army operations, a week of amnesty, and an extermination campaign of whatever length proves necessary. He warns that forests and mountains will offer no protection from military forces and artificial cold. Gang members can qualify for mercy by bringing in their leaders, tied up or dead.

The threat produces a rush to surrender. Numbers rise through the third day of amnesty, then decline until only a few people appear on the last day. Gamov announces that 273,565 people have laid down their arms. He declares organized banditry defeated, while admitting that isolated crimes can still occur. Internal-security troops can now provide ten divisions for the front; the amnestied men should provide at least as many. He tells women that they can safely walk the streets at night and celebrates the return of their relatives to ordinary life ([campaign evidence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-3-ledger.md#p3-c02)).

Privately, Gamov attacks Pavel's inaccurate estimate of gang strength. Pavel explains that armies can be tracked by agents and sensors, whereas small, shifting gangs are hard to penetrate. Gamov turns this failure into a broader warning. Their unified state prevents open political argument, so the government cannot reliably know what people think. Bar reports approval at his factories and farms, but Gamov says present approval does not remove the danger of future ignorance. Fagusta is useful as a licensed critic, yet he cannot stand for the silent majority. Exceptional events may be needed to make that majority speak.

After the meeting, Semipalov asks Gonzalez to arrest someone whenever he requests it and hold that person until he orders release or trial. Gonzalez readily agrees. Semipalov chooses him because the Black Court inspires more fear than intelligence custody. The intended prisoner's name remains unstated.

[Source: P3-C02](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p3-c02).

<a id="p3-c03"></a>
### Part 3, Chapter 3 — A Spy Becomes a Channel

[Semipalov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#andrei-semipalov) confronts Voityuk with evidence that he has sold state secrets, including an emerald necklace received from Knurka IX's minister, Shirbai Shar. Voityuk first denies the charge, then tries blackmail. His pen has recorded their conversations; if he is arrested, the world will learn that the deputy dictator supplied a foreign spy. He demands continued cooperation and begins asking about new divisions, condensed-water production, and the growing waterplane fleet ([identities and evidence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-3-ledger.md#p3-c03)).

Semipalov has prepared for this moment. He calls Gonzalez and orders the immediate arrest of Voityuk's wife, Anna Kursai. Voityuk's confidence collapses. Semipalov threatens both their lives and demands complete obedience. He does not need Voityuk to obtain Cortezian secrets. He needs him to transmit selected information to Cortezia. He promises genuine facts, but refuses information that would damage Latania. To make the arrangement credible, he presents himself as an opponent of Gamov's brutality who might help replace him with a government willing to negotiate. Voityuk accepts this explanation and imagines that he is helping to clear Semipalov's path to power.

Semipalov then provides a supposed secret: Gamov intends to ask the enemy governments for peace terms and use their reply to discover the true state of public opinion. Voityuk expects the allies to answer in a way that destroys Gamov's support. Semipalov also demands an initial political credit of one hundred million didans, divided among one hundred anonymous accounts at a bank in Klur. Test withdrawals will establish that the funds are real. The large demand makes the proposed alliance appear proportionate to its political ambition. Before ending the interview, Semipalov releases Anna, leaving the threat of another arrest intact.

Alone, he is disturbed by his growing pleasure in intrigue and coercion. His planned great lie depends on many small truths, and the logic of events is taking him down a path he once considered alien to himself. Gamov, Pavel, and Woodworth have heard the encounter and praise the performance. Gamov dismisses the possibility that Semipalov truly wants his position: each man has a different, necessary role. He does point out one invention. He had never planned the peace appeal Semipalov announced. Semipalov tells him to make the announcement true. The invented intelligence thus becomes the starting point for a major declaration of policy.

[Source: P3-C03](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p3-c03).

<a id="p3-c04"></a>
### Part 3, Chapter 4 — War Before the Court of Humanity

[Gamov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#alexei-gamov) turns Semipalov's invented peace initiative into a Declaration on War. He calls the war a crime against humanity and demands judgment of its leaders and supporters. His ministers object that this condemns them too, although they inherited the conflict and must defend their country. Gamov accepts that implication. He will publish the declaration as his own appeal and let his colleagues express their objections through Fagusta. Open disagreement may reveal public opinion. It will also strengthen the enemy's belief that Semipalov intends to remove him ([declaration review](docs/knowledge/parts/part-3-ledger.md#p3-c04)).

The declaration attracts less attention at home than Gamov expects, but causes outrage abroad. While governments delay stating their peace terms, two visitors from neutral Klur arrive in Adan: philosopher Orest Biber and novelist Arnold Falk. Falk admires Gamov as a military hero and cannot accept his attack on war. Biber tries to expose contradictions in the argument. Their long discussion makes Gamov's position more precise. He does not condemn every past war or every killing. Defending a child, resisting oppression, or fighting for survival can be justified. His accusation concerns the present war, which he believes has no objective necessity.

Gamov presses Biber to identify an economic or political difference that makes coexistence impossible. Biber names different systems, national interests, alliances, and spheres of influence, but cannot show why they require millions of deaths. Gamov argues that modern wealth and production remove the old struggle for essential resources. Rulers instead turn small disputes into war because they want to exercise power. Officials who could prevent the disaster and fail to act share responsibility. Power itself carries a potential for crime, which must be checked by accountability.

He applies that principle to himself. After the war, he will submit to a court of the peoples. If Cortezia wants to try him earlier, it can finance a section of the Black Court and attempt to capture him, just as Latania hopes to capture Amentola. Biber then argues that war expresses human nature: rulers could not send willing populations into battle unless people wanted to fight. This briefly unsettles Gamov. He replies that creation is humanity's deeper impulse. Families, cities, knowledge, art, and longer lives are the work of builders. Destruction usually serves or distorts that creative drive.

Finally, Biber asks why Gamov did not seek power in richer Cortezia. Gamov says Latania is his homeland and the place where his wider purpose can succeed. He considers Cortezia a mature system at its limit, while Latania can begin a new stage of development and end interstate war. These remain his convictions. Falk leaves the argument still unable to reconcile the celebrated soldier with the man who condemns war.

[Source: P3-C04](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p3-c04).

<a id="p3-c05"></a>
### Part 3, Chapter 5 — The Fleet Behind the Front

Fagusta attacks the contradiction between rewarding soldiers now and threatening to judge them after the war. [Gamov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#alexei-gamov) tells Semipalov that their policy has two aims: win this war, then use victory to make later wars impossible. Present rewards need not become permanent praise. Meanwhile, the Black Court's campaign abroad seems weak. Its journal publishes sentences and rewards, but only a few minor targets have been attacked ([policy and military review](docs/knowledge/parts/part-3-ledger.md#p3-c05)).

Pavel reports a much greater danger. By spring, Ferdinand Vaksel should have five times the force he used against Zabon. Cortezia is expanding weather stations and concentrating energy production for a massive attack. Shtupa asks for twice his current supply of condensed water to protect Latania's fields and planting season. Semipalov silently objects, and Gamov refuses the full increase. Former allies are also moving toward Cortezia. Only the Cortezian politician Leonard Bernoulli argues against accepting these burdens; he favors concentrating Cortezia's own strength against Latania.

After the meeting, Semipalov gives Pavel the codes for one hundred accounts at Orion bank in Klur. Voityuk says Amentola has accepted the supposed alliance to overthrow Gamov and has provided the requested hundred million didans. He also reports that Cortezia, Roder, and Nordag are preparing a Declaration of Peace intended to clear the way for Gamov's opponents. Gamov stops Pavel from tracing Voityuk's contacts. Discovery of surveillance could destroy their channel.

Gamov, Pavel, and Semipalov then visit a secret factory inside a mountain. Its purpose explains the energy shortage: Latania is building a waterplane fleet on a scale the world has never seen. Thousands of condensed-water reservoirs are reserved for the machines. Semipalov knows that these reserves could make Shtupa's task much easier, but refuses to sacrifice the fleet. The aircraft use released steam for lift, forward movement, and braking. To conceal production from spies in the capital, factory reports understate output by secret factors; this plant reports one-third of its true production.

At home, Elena serves food bought with a reward for installing her technology at a factory. She earns more than Semipalov, but remains dissatisfied. Although she holds government office, constant travel keeps her from the capital and from the important role she was promised. Semipalov tells her that the role has been delayed, not canceled, and asks her to trust Gamov's word.

[Source: P3-C05](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p3-c05).

<a id="p3-c06"></a>
### Part 3, Chapter 6 — Gamov Risks His Mandate

The enemy publishes its Declaration of Peace as spring begins. Its flattering account of Latania's future cannot hide its demands: Latania must acknowledge responsibility for the war, accept a settlement of territorial and other disputes, and remove Gamov, Gonzalez, and Woodworth. The remaining ministers are left to the people's judgment. Semipalov initially sees only terms designed to prevent negotiations. Pavel points out their further purpose: they leave room for Semipalov to replace Gamov, just as the false alliance promised ([terms and referendum record](docs/knowledge/parts/part-3-ledger.md#p3-c06)).

[Gamov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#alexei-gamov) reacts with unusual excitement. At the Core, some ministers favor a formal rejection and others contemptuous silence. He instead proposes a referendum. The declaration has created the exceptional situation that can make the whole population reveal its views. Pavel suggests that the government can control the totals. Gamov sharply refuses. False figures would defeat the purpose of the vote, which must tell both the rulers and the public what the country thinks.

He sets four separate questions: whether Latania should accept guilt for aggression, whether his government should resign, whether the country should pay reparations, and whether it should satisfy its neighbors' territorial claims. Isiro says he will use procedures Gamov gave him two months earlier. Semipalov realizes that preparations have long preceded this apparent sudden decision.

In private, Gamov explains that he and Isiro had already considered a direct appeal to the people. He expects Cortezia to wait for the result: an attack could provoke national anger, while a vote against him might spare the enemy further fighting. Semipalov is more troubled by the separate question about the government. People may reject humiliation and territorial loss but still sacrifice Gamov to obtain peace. A narrow majority would give a dictator who seized power too weak a foundation for his larger plans.

Gamov accepts the risk. He needs to know whether the nation supports his mission. If it does not, he will leave and Semipalov will continue their work. Semipalov hears the certainty of a prophet in this answer and fears it. The chapter ends with the verdict still ahead.

[Source: P3-C06](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p3-c06).

<a id="p3-c07"></a>
### Part 3, Chapter 7 — Victory at the Ballot, Danger on Every Front

Fagusta surprises [Semipalov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#andrei-semipalov) by reporting the referendum without his usual attacks. He explains that he has always supported Gamov's government because every plausible replacement would be worse. He can expose its mistakes without claiming that he could govern better. His restraint is meant to avoid needless agitation before the vote ([character and event review](docs/knowledge/parts/part-3-ledger.md#p3-c07)).

Isiro completes preparations in ten days, although he admits the machinery was ready from the start. Peano wants every extra day to strengthen the defenses. Vaksel and Bernoulli press Amentola to attack, but the president orders Vaksel to wait. The vote gives Gamov a strong mandate: 93% reject guilt for aggression, 87% oppose the government's resignation, 99% reject reparations, and 96% reject territorial concessions. The main exception is Floria, the western autonomous region through which army communications run. Its larger dissenting minorities concern Peano. Gamov is pleased that support for the country exceeds support for its current leaders. Latania rejects the enemy's conditions and offers negotiations with a suspension of fighting.

Vaksel attacks the next morning. He breaks the first defensive line, but Peano has anticipated the blow and withdraws his troops behind the main defenses. Enemy losses are heavier, and hidden sensors give the Latanian command a clear view of the advancing forces. Abroad, however, territorial loss looks like defeat. A week later Great Lepin, Sobrana, Konduk, Klur, and Korina enter the war against Latania. Torbash stays formally neutral but presses new border claims through Shirbai Shar. Gamov orders Woodworth to receive him courteously and prolong the talks.

Two further shocks follow. During a factory visit, three attackers rush Gamov. Workers and guards overcome the two armed with impulsators. The third reaches him with a knife, but Semyon Serbin pushes Gamov aside and takes the wound. Two attackers die under the crowd's assault; security chief Grigory Varella preserves the third for questioning. Semipalov condemns the failure of protection. Gamov answers that the guard Pavel assigned has saved him. Serbin's loyalty is especially striking because Gamov once publicly humiliated him, then publicly reconciled with him.

At headquarters, Semipalov sees the weather front moving east as Cortezian pressure overcomes Shtupa. He then sees Sorbas burning. Konduk has used Cortezian-supplied aircraft to cross the border and bomb the declared peaceful town with resonance weapons. Early reports say half its people have died and the rest have fled into the desert. Peano sends rescue units. Pavel meanwhile reports that the surviving assassin's testimony points toward Komlin. He has arrested the marshal, Marutzian, and several former officials. The consequences of both crises now go before Gamov.

[Source: P3-C07](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p3-c07).

<a id="p3-c08"></a>
### Part 3, Chapter 8 — The Punishment of Konduk

The destruction of Sorbas drives [Gamov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#alexei-gamov) into cold fury. Pavel describes Konduk as a poor agricultural country ruled by priests and landowners. Tarkhun-khor heads its religion as the living representative of the prophet Mamun; Maravan-khor controls parliament and ordered the bombing without consulting it. Sorbas itself has sacred importance in Mamun's tradition. Gamov demands a conquest that will punish the attack and deter others. Peano cannot move a large army across the desert, so Gamov proposes one hundred waterplanes. Semipalov objects that this will reveal their secret strength. Gamov answers that it will also frighten Cortezia's allies and force Amentola to spend resources protecting them. Semipalov agrees ([occupation and diplomatic record](docs/knowledge/parts/part-3-ledger.md#p3-c08)).

The airborne attack surprises Konduk completely. While its army fights at the border, Latanian troops land in the capital, Kondina, and seize the government, parliament, generals, and religious leader. Maravan-khor orders surrender. Gamov sends Isiro to direct the occupation, with Gonzalez and Pustovoyt administering punishment and mercy.

Their judgments follow each parliamentarian's vote on the war. Supporters are hanged, their property confiscated, and their families deported to northern Latania. Abstainers lose half their property and receive forced labor. Opponents, only 17% of parliament, gain confiscated enterprises and form a government under military occupation. Whole provinces receive corresponding penalties or rewards. Soldiers from pro-war provinces are sent to rebuild Sorbas. The courts discard ordinary safeguards and broadcast the bodies of the condemned, including Maravan-khor. Priests are exempted from these executions. Gamov intends a separate approach to their religion, but does not yet explain it fully. He reveals that both he and Isiro know Mamun's teachings, and that he grew up among the prophet's followers.

The conquest changes the wider coalition. Great Lepin pauses its advance; other allies take defensive precautions or send limited reinforcements. Amentola promises two hundred aircraft to Latania's neighbors. Bernoulli again objects that aid to weak allies drains the decisive western front. Gamov orders Semipalov to use Voityuk to support Amentola's mistaken policy and undermine Bernoulli's advice.

Two recordings show the effect on Torbash. Before Konduk falls, Shirbai Shar demands territory, a gold loan, and free supplies within three days. Afterward, Woodworth rejects those demands and instead requests roads for Latanian troop movements and food sold partly on credit. Shirbai then produces Knurka's unexpected defense: the king wants shares in the White Court so that he can help judge any future case against himself. Semipalov sees this as a flaw in courts organized as share companies. Gamov admires Knurka's intelligence and accepts the principle, even when Semipalov asks whether Gamov might one day have to pass sentence on himself.

[Source: P3-C08](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p3-c08).

<a id="p3-c09"></a>
### Part 3, Chapter 9 — A False Agent and a Real Doubt

Vaksel continues a slow offensive designed to exhaust Latania's equipment before confronting its mobile reserves. Peano gives up positions when defense becomes too costly and avoids expensive counterattacks. Nordag again blocks Zabon from the north and east, while the western front moves from Patina into Floria. Artificial storms flood the fields and damage bridges. Latania can weaken the rain but cannot protect a good harvest ([front and intelligence record](docs/knowledge/parts/part-3-ledger.md#p3-c09)).

[Pavel](docs/knowledge/characters.md#pavel-prishchepa) brings plans to neutralize Bernoulli, whose insistence that every resource go to Vaksel threatens Gamov's strategy. Killing or simply abducting him would confirm that his warnings are dangerous. The preferred scheme will discredit him first. A Latanian agent will give money to his fund for Vaksel under the name of a nonexistent industrialists' society. Amentola's police can then “discover” the hostile connection. Before Bernoulli can clear himself, abduction will make his disappearance look like flight from justice. The money will come from Cortezia's own payment for the supposed campaign against Gamov.

Semipalov supplies the other half of the trap through Voityuk. He says the enemy's peace declaration has strengthened Gamov by making him the defender of national honor. He nevertheless claims to remain committed to replacing him. Gamov, he says, intends to abandon the main struggle temporarily, conquer weaker states to the south and east, seize their food, and later turn back against Vaksel. He falsely describes the Konduk force as Latania's entire serviceable air fleet. This account encourages Amentola to keep protecting his allies, which will divert resources from the decisive front.

Semipalov then hints that an influential Cortezian politician secretly works for Gamov. Once the donation is exposed, Amentola should identify that supposed agent as Bernoulli. Voityuk leaves eager to transmit the information. The performance succeeds partly because Semipalov's disagreement is no longer wholly invented. As he describes the suffering caused by Gamov's policy, he feels real bitterness and imagines governing differently. Gamov hears that moment too, though he praises the operation.

Their discussion is interrupted by a more immediate danger. Waterplane crews have mutinied, arrested their commanders, loaded combat weapons, and threatened to fight. Gamov announces that he, Semipalov, and Pavel will go to the airfield themselves.

[Source: P3-C09](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p3-c09).

<a id="p3-c10"></a>
### Part 3, Chapter 10 — The Mutiny That Demanded Battle

[Gamov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#alexei-gamov), Semipalov, and Pavel fly to an unnamed secret base with 120 waterplanes. Its crews are carefully selected and isolated from outside contact to hide the fleet's size. Pavel's liaison had reported heated discussions, then sent a warning that commanders were under arrest and aircraft being armed before communication stopped. Gamov condemns the liaison for failing to discover the cause of the unrest ([base and personnel record](docs/knowledge/parts/part-3-ledger.md#p3-c10)).

Four men identify themselves as leaders: Alfred Palman, Ivan Kordobin, Sergei Skripnik, and Jean Vilta. They obey Gamov's first order and release the officers. Kordobin then explains their actions. The crews trained with intense pride throughout winter, expecting to help win the spring battles. Instead, they remained idle while the army retreated and enemy weather attacks ruined the fields. The conquest of Konduk briefly promised that their own turn had come. Further delay made them suspect sabotage at the top. When their officers refused a sortie and threatened punishment, they arrested them and prepared to act alone. They accept responsibility, but still ask to fight.

Gamov answers in a way Semipalov did not expect. He thanks the crews for their readiness to serve the country, condemns the arrest of honorable officers, and forgives them. He also forgives the commanders, praising them for having trained such devoted soldiers. Colonel Korney Kaplin, whom Semipalov remembers from the earlier encirclement, struggles to hold back tears. The crews' shame and fear turn into relief and affection.

Gamov then admits that he is the man keeping them from battle. He reveals the secret: they are one division in a huge fleet still being completed. Committing it in fragments would waste the force that can reverse the war. The army must hold on until that reserve is ready and the enemy's advance makes its use decisive. He ends by asking the young men to accept death for their country if required. The crowd lifts him and carries him around the square. Semipalov eventually has Kaplin stop them because the shaking is making Gamov ill.

The four leaders were already outstanding trainees and candidates for advancement. Gamov creates four brigades of two regiments each and appoints them commanders. He praises their teachers for producing men capable of surpassing them. Yet he keeps the liaison's punishment: that officer failed at his specific duty to understand and report the division's condition. On the return flight, Gamov also makes clear that the mutiny has not changed the timetable. The fleet will fight when strategy requires it.

[Source: P3-C10](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p3-c10).

<a id="p3-c11"></a>
### Part 3, Chapter 11 — The Captive Who Understands the Plan

The trap against Bernoulli begins with a five-million-didan donation from the fictitious industrialists' society. After he publicly acknowledges it, police find neither the organization nor its representative. Amentola attacks him in the senate. Bernoulli denounces the accusation and demands the president's removal. The scandal does not develop as neatly as Pavel hoped. Bernoulli's honesty is widely respected, and many people suspect a provocation organized by Amentola. The president becomes cautious, holding back reserves from both his allies and Vaksel. Gamov needs those reserves dispersed beyond recall, not merely delayed ([operation and evidence review](docs/knowledge/parts/part-3-ledger.md#p3-c11)).

[Pavel](docs/knowledge/characters.md#pavel-prishchepa) has already moved to the next stage and abducted Bernoulli. He prepares announcements identifying the senator as a Latanian agent, promises of honors and money, and forged photographs showing him on friendly terms with Gamov's government. Woodworth reluctantly agrees to supply a false story that they pledged as students to undermine Cortezia. Gamov worries that a strategy built on so many deceptions may still fail.

Three days later, Bernoulli is brought before them. He first suspects an Amentola plot, but recognizes Woodworth and understands where he is. He refuses the hand of the man he considers a traitor. When shown the photographs, he looks for weaknesses in the montage and mocks the predictable plan to reward him as a supposed agent. More seriously, he identifies the risk Gamov already fears. Amentola may publicly welcome the accusation while privately concluding that Bernoulli was right: Latania abducted him because his demand to concentrate supplies on Vaksel threatened its strategy.

Gamov changes his approach. Bernoulli prides himself on understanding what lesser politicians miss. Gamov proposes to praise that intellect so extravagantly that even friends will believe he defected to a ruler who finally recognized his worth. The promise of recognition could supply the motive that forged photographs alone lack. Bernoulli sees the danger and advises Gamov to kill him, because otherwise he will eventually expose the scheme. Gamov keeps him alive, intending to delay that exposure until it can no longer matter.

Pavel also has testimony from Marutzian and Komlin about the assassination attempt, but Gamov postpones it. Serbin is recovering, and the reserve problem is more urgent. The ministers support publishing the false evidence, yet Gamov insists they need something stronger. Semipalov has an idea and asks himself whether he is ready to propose it.

[Source: P3-C11](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p3-c11).

<a id="p3-c12"></a>
### Part 3, Chapter 12 — The Traitor He Agrees to Become

[Semipalov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#andrei-semipalov) finds his answer in the pilots' response to Gamov. They rejoiced because he made them feel uniquely needed. Semipalov now proposes a sacrifice of his own: the government should accuse him of treason for Cortezia, condemn him, and stage a public hanging. His exposure would confirm the intelligence Voityuk supplied and make Bernoulli appear to have revealed a genuine traitor. Gamov, Pavel, and Woodworth accept. Semipalov agrees to a death sentence, not death itself. Pustovoyt will arrange the false execution and conceal him until he returns to lead the offensive against Vaksel ([plan and personal cost](docs/knowledge/parts/part-3-ledger.md#p3-c12)).

Semipalov refuses to tell Elena. He believes she cannot hide the truth in her expression, even if she keeps silent. Given twenty-four hours, he calls Voityuk and claims that Bernoulli has learned about the money transferred to a senior Latanian official. Voityuk panics and offers to arrange escape to Cortezia. This is the response Semipalov wants: the danger will be reported immediately. He then asks Gamov to arrest him before the escape instructions arrive, avoiding a real attempt to flee.

Pavel takes him into custody the next morning in front of military-factory directors. Serbin, the soldier who saved Gamov, treats him as a hated traitor and pushes him toward the guards. Pavel stops the abuse, but remains cold. In solitary confinement, Semipalov discovers that knowing the arrest is staged does not make the prison unreal. Gamov refuses to visit, and Semipalov feels the loss of control over his own life.

Pustovoyt comes that evening with practical reassurance and unexpected tenderness. His engineers have made equipment that will hang the enclosing sack without stopping Semipalov's breathing. He has also persuaded Gamov to spare him an actual confrontation with Gonzalez. The government can announce a closed trial and withhold its record as a state secret. Semipalov is deeply moved. The newspapers already report his betrayal. Fagusta responds with a puzzled essay about the opposing alleged defections: if each eminent man finds justice in the enemy's country, neither side can claim a clear moral advantage.

Pustovoyt has also allowed Elena to visit. She repeatedly asks whether Semipalov is guilty. He refuses to reveal the secret and insists that her knowledge of him should be enough. She recalls his frustration at being second to Gamov and fears that rivalry has become treason. He feels betrayed by her doubt; she feels abandoned by his evasions and insults. Finally, in anger, he repeats the accusations as a confession while warning that their full meaning will become clear later. Elena leaves saying that love has turned into hatred and contempt. The state deception has inflicted a real wound on their marriage, and Semipalov is left struggling to breathe through his grief.

<a id="p3-c13"></a>
### Part 3, Chapter 13 — Darkness Beneath the Gallows

[Semipalov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#andrei-semipalov) is brought to execution at noon in bright sunshine. Shtupa has committed the weather generators to keeping clouds away from Adan. Guards bring the prisoner to a crowded square, where Gonzalez, Pustovoyt, court officials, executioners, and a band await him. The gallows is eight lans high, exactly as Pustovoyt promised ([execution record](docs/knowledge/parts/part-3-ledger.md#p3-c13)).

Peano watches from a vehicle, crying instead of smiling. Semipalov assumes that he has not been told the secret. Elena is absent, and the crowd receives the sentence in silence. Semipalov makes no farewell speech and tells the executioners to proceed.

They enclose him in a leather sack, fasten the straps, and adjust the noose. In the darkness, his confidence collapses. He suddenly interprets Peano's tears as proof that the execution is real and that he has been deceived. He jerks in fear, rises into weightlessness, and experiences darkness that he calls eternal. Part 3 ends inside that terrified perception, without confirming whether the planned rescue has failed.

## Part 4 — Offensive

<a id="p4-c01"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 1 — A secret return to life

[Source](src/text/part0032.html#UGI06-e57e33ea454c44b1aa8a6263a5c5ad60) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c01)

[Andrei Semipalov](docs/knowledge/characters.md#andrei-semipalov) wakes from a terrifying state in which his whole body seems to break apart through vibration. Pavel Prishchepa and Nikolai Pustovoyt have watched over him for two days. His execution was a deception, but the apparatus could still have harmed him, and his recovery took longer than expected. Pavel reports that half of Floria has fallen and that local rebels fired on the retreating Latanian troops. Amentola is drawing Latania’s former allies toward his own conference. Yet this military and diplomatic danger also serves the hidden plan: Semipalov’s apparent death has strengthened the enemy’s confidence in its strategy.

[Pustovoyt’s orders](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c01) require Semipalov to remain in a guarded house until Gamov calls him. He can contact Pavel, but direct contact with the dictator would risk exposure. Grigory Varella guards him chiefly against being seen. His receiver also lets him hear Semipalov’s voice outdoors; only the screened rooms offer privacy. The concealment that keeps Semipalov alive also makes him a closely monitored prisoner of the plan.

Pustovoyt describes two scientists in Pavel’s nearby secret laboratory. One seeks the energy hidden in matter. The other seeks access to other worlds and claims that he belongs to one of them. Pavel values both, although Pustovoyt considers them mad. The conversation also forces Semipalov to confront his own public character: Pustovoyt says that ministers can argue with Gamov but dare only report to Semipalov, whose refusal is final.

Alone, Semipalov thinks about the unfinished [waterplane program](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c01) and Shtupa’s shrinking energy-water reserves. He hopes production can soon shift back to weather defense. He also fears that the southern states may attack before Gamov can strike Vaksel. His private thoughts return to Elena’s hatred during their last prison meeting. To escape political and emotional isolation, he asks Varella to invite the two scientists to his screened room.

<a id="p4-c02"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 2 — A window onto nuclear destruction

[Source](src/text/part0033.html) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c02)

The visitors identify themselves as [Berthold Shvurts, a nuclear physicist, and Berthold Kozyura, a chronophysicist](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c02). Marshal Komlin founded their laboratory; Pavel now funds it. Shvurts explains that their world normally draws energy from molecular changes. Energy-water stores power by compressing water’s molecular structure, then releases it when that structure returns to normal. Nuclear processes offer far greater power. His excited account of the possible destruction of Adan and even the planet appalls Semipalov, who first thinks the two men are mad.

Kozyura says that he came from a neighboring universe. His uncertain memory places him in his parents’ laboratory before a nuclear accident threw him into a different time stream and left him near Sorbas. He attributes his baldness to the passage. Shvurts lost his hair during an unsuccessful attempt to make the reverse journey. Before Semipalov can close the laboratory, Kozyura insists that he examine its instruments.

The walk through the park briefly restores Semipalov’s pleasure in ordinary nature. Inside, automated nuclear equipment supplies power to the laboratories and his house. Upstairs, Kozyura adjusts a viewing system that first shows a century’s events superimposed as mist. Once he narrows its time focus, Semipalov sees a huge passenger aircraft, fields, and a vast city. He is astonished by its traffic and buildings of ninety or a hundred floors; buildings in his own world normally have no more than five. Kozyura explains that this civilization uses combustion engines and lives without energy-water or programmed rain. What its people accept as ordinary weather seems intolerable to Semipalov.

Kozyura then shifts the [view into another time and place](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c02). A nuclear flash overwhelms a city’s morning sun. Buildings melt. A little girl vanishes in flame. Burned survivors struggle toward a river, and one dying man seems to beg Semipalov for help. He orders the screen off and condemns the scientists’ wish to bring such power into his world.

The scientists answer that nuclear energy also has peaceful uses, just as energy-water powers both farms and weapons. Their familiarity with the images has dulled their response. Semipalov calls the other world a criminal reflection of his own. Kozyura reverses that claim: he says the world they watched occupies the main stream of time, while Semipalov’s world is only one diminished branch. This remains [Kozyura’s cosmological explanation](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c02), although the images have broken Semipalov’s initial disbelief. Sickened, he asks Varella to take him home.

<a id="p4-c03"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 3 — Doubt, complicity, and a delayed return

[Source](src/text/part0034.html) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c03)

The nuclear victims haunt Semipalov. He first tells himself that his own war is different: soldiers fight soldiers, and no black sun consumes children. He then recalls [Konduk, Gonzalez’s terror, and weather attacks that starve civilians](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c03). He can find no clear boundary between acceptable killing and criminal slaughter. Varella reminds him that Gamov also condemned the killing of children after Sorbas. Soldiers still call Gamov “the colonel,” despite his supreme office; their loyalty rests on the commander they first knew.

Semipalov returns to the scientists and asks to see peaceful life. The images finally persuade him that the other world is real. However, [Kozyura cannot prove his own origin](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c03). He was found as an adolescent in the steppe, near death, with no memory and no knowledge of the local language. He later excelled at university, built instruments that altered intra-atomic time, and developed chronotelescopes. The landscapes on their screens brought back memories that he interpreted as his childhood. His nuclear discoveries brought him into partnership with his student friend Shvurts. Komlin supported the work because he expected it to secure victory. Gamov has not yet been told. Semipalov permits the laboratory to continue, although its discoveries disturb him.

A week later, Pavel arrives with news that Peano needs Semipalov back. All Floria is now under enemy control. No more than a third of its people, especially the young and those seeking change, have welcomed the invaders. Former allies are moving toward Latania’s borders and await a signal from Amentola’s conference in Klur, due in ten days. [Knurka IX](docs/knowledge/characters.md#knurka-ix) alone stays away, claiming that divine judgment will show which side deserves his support. Meanwhile he bargains over joining the Terror and Mercy companies with goods instead of gold.

Semipalov decides to remain hidden until Amentola speaks. Pavel explains that he initially doubted the scientists and therefore withheld their work. His investigation found no ordinary explanation for Kozyura’s appearance. Both men now have altered blood, sensory abnormalities, and little interest in food, amusement, or women. Semipalov’s retrospective voice stresses that neither he nor Pavel drew the conclusions these [changes should have suggested](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c03). The chapter leaves those conclusions unstated.

<a id="p4-c04"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 4 — The hidden air army

[Source](src/text/part0034.html#10DJ5R-e57e33ea454c44b1aa8a6263a5c5ad60) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c04)

Semipalov watches the conference in [Fermor, Klur’s capital](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c04). Its public face is an extravagant performance centered on Amentola; any practical negotiations remain off screen. After the president’s advertised speech, Semipalov orders a waterplane for the next morning and returns to the palace. The governing Core, ministers, and newspaper editors greet him. Most have only now learned that he survived. Peano admits that Pavel warned him about the deception, but the execution looked so real that he doubted it at the last moment. Semipalov remembers having the same fear.

Gamov lets him chair the meeting, then joins the military discussion. Semipalov immediately resumes his duties as war minister and deputy dictator. When Pavel asks whether Elena should be told, he refuses: she can learn with everyone else. He treats the offensive as more urgent than their relationship, leaving her belief in his death intact for now.

Peano shows the result of the [secret waterplane program](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c04). Hundreds of new machines are hidden in forest bases, artificial caves, and hangars. Ordinary aerial reconnaissance shows only trees and rocks. All air divisions have reached their starting positions. The long retreat can now become a surprise attack. Vaksel’s formation remains disciplined: his own forces lead, Patina supports them, and Roder’s troops hold the rear against a possible breakthrough. Peano thinks this reflects careful doctrine, rather than knowledge of the coming strike.

The plan combines a main attack behind Vaksel with a separate blow at the conference. Most air divisions will seize prisoner-of-war camps and release Latanian soldiers. [Colonel Korney Kaplin’s division](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c04), with its recently rebellious brigade commanders, will capture Fermor and the assembled leaders. Two other air divisions will engage Klur’s defending aircraft. Shtupa promises clouds and storms at the required positions and heights; the energy-water reserve is ready. Semipalov sets the attack for the next day.

<a id="p4-c05"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 5 — A storm opens the prison camps

[Source](src/text/part0035.html) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c05)

[Shtupa’s weather offensive](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c05) begins without the old restrictions on energy-water. Mobile generators move close to the fighting, rear stations intensify their work, and a vast cloud mass rolls west. Marshal Ferdinand Vaksel initially refuses to believe reports of clouds ten leagues thick. He assumes Gamov has spent his last reserves on a short display for the Fermor conference. When counter-cyclones fail, Vaksel conserves his remaining energy and orders troops, civilians, vehicles, and aircraft to stay under cover until the storm passes.

That apparently rational order leaves the sky open to Latania’s hidden air army. Its leading divisions cross above the clouds toward Roder and Klur. Landing forces attack barracks and headquarters before the enemy grasps the scale of the offensive. Gamov and Semipalov watch Pavel’s live feeds as scouts seize Vaksel. The marshal fires at the first attacker and repeatedly fights free, while his generals remain passive. Orders require his capture alive. His captors refuse to let him dress, intending to display him barefoot in his shirt. Vaksel denounces this humiliation and resists until they bind his legs and carry him out.

Semipalov admires his courage and questions the treatment. Gamov admires it too, but approves the humiliation. The narrator explains [Gamov’s hostility to military honor](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c05): he regards shame as war-makers’ proper reward. Semipalov does not yet understand how fully Gamov will later impose that view on his own followers.

Roder’s air scouts detect the forces heading for Fermor where cloud cover is thinner. Roder and Klur launch aircraft, but Peano’s escorts clear the route while Kaplin’s division keeps flying toward its targets. More than 250 escort waterplanes overwhelm about forty Klur machines. Kordobin’s troops enter the five-story Svetoch hotel, which houses the conference delegates. Familiar rulers and diplomats emerge as terrified prisoners. Amentola is not yet found.

Semipalov insists that Pavel turn to the [prisoner camps, the operation’s main objective](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c05). Freed soldiers are to be rearmed into a force that will strike Vaksel from behind while the existing army attacks from the front. The plan depends on this combined pressure, not on storms alone or on capturing particular leaders. In a Lamaria camp, guards fight briefly before surrendering. In a smaller Patina camp, they surrender without resistance and occupy the prisoners’ empty bunks. Starved men embrace and dance with their rescuers, collapsing as stronger soldiers support them. The joy reveals such suffering that Semipalov cannot bear to watch another camp.

<a id="p4-c06"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 6 — Terror, mercy, and public resurrection

[Source](src/text/part0036.html) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c06)

The next day confirms an immense success with small losses, but [Amentola has escaped](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c06). He left Fermor before the Latanian aircraft crossed the frontier. Other important guests also avoided capture. Peano flies the captured leaders, observers, and journalists out; he does not intend to hold Klur against approaching reinforcements. Within a week, Latanian forces leave. Gamov forbids damage even to its fortifications, a restraint Semipalov and Peano do not yet understand.

The liberated prisoners expose a serious weakness in the main plan: many are too starved to fight. Semipalov redirects the airborne troops toward enemy supply depots, both to feed the prisoners and to deprive the enemy of food and ammunition. Gonzalez sends Black Court officials to judge camp staff. Pustovoyt demands White judges with power to overrule unjust punishments. Semipalov supports restraint, and Gamov permits White judges to attend, without endorsing one minister against the other.

The narrator then looks ahead to a [broadcast trial in Roder](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c06). Commandant Ishim Samino and his staff stole prisoners’ money, food, and medicine. Records contrast two doctors. Pop Barvell hoarded drugs and falsified their use; only thirteen of his seventy-two patients survived. Gabl Khota used his supplies and secretly gave his own blood; thirty-two of his forty-eight patients survived. He could not ask prisoners to donate because they were already dangerously weak.

Gonzalez turns punishment into a warning for every uncaptured camp. Samino is sentenced to die with stolen money forced into his stomach. His deputy Purp Gorgon is to be whipped to death with his own whip. Barvell is to receive the drugs he withheld in a fatal overdose. Guards must carry out these sentences or face execution themselves. The White judge does not stop them. He instead frees Khota and awards him two million lats for his blood donations. The [award contains a numerical discrepancy](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c06): the stated units produce 40,000 drops at one lat each, not two million lats.

Gamov welcomes the combination of terrifying punishment and exceptional reward. Semipalov sees that both ministries serve the dictator’s policy. Gamov then orders his public return. Before the broadcast, Semipalov proposes arresting fugitive spy Voityuk’s wife, Anna Kursai, to force his surrender. Gamov declines to use such a strong device for a minor target, but marks the idea for a more important purpose.

On air, Gamov explains that Bernoulli was a genuine enemy, abducted and falsely branded a paid Latanian agent to discredit his warnings. Semipalov then proposed his own false treason and execution to make the larger deception credible. Gamov praises him as the creator of the air fleet and architect of victory. Deeply moved, Semipalov needs several minutes before he can appear. His brief thanks is sincere, but Pavel later thinks it lacked the political force Gamov always gives his emotion.

<a id="p4-c07"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 7 — Six strongholds close around the enemy

[Source](src/text/part0037.html) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c07)

The enemy recovers faster than expected under [Mart Tronshke, a Roder corps general](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c07). He recognizes that the liberated prisoners are becoming a new army and turns his main forces against them, leaving a defensive front in Latania. Unlike Vaksel, he takes the threat seriously from the start. Peano openly condemns the failures of intelligence and command, including his own. Evacuating the prisoners would save them but abandon the whole strategy. Instead, he concentrates them in six towns, orders all-round defenses, and uses Latania’s air superiority to deliver troops, weapons, food, and portable weather generators.

Meanwhile, Pavel asks Semipalov to see Elena, who is happy that he lives and desperate to reach him. She regretted her prison outburst even before learning the truth. Pavel admits that her genuine grief and anger were planned parts of the deception. Semipalov still refuses to meet her. He recalls Pavel’s former attraction to Elena and their enduring friendship, but gives him the task of comforting her. Pavel tells him that he has changed. Semipalov’s joke about returning damaged from death does not ease the rebuke.

Bar shows that industrial output has risen despite the strain of producing the air fleet and weather reserves. The old stores are exhausted and luxury goods are scarce, but arms and supplies are ready. Semipalov delays the frontal offensive until Tronshke is deeply engaged against the [six fortified towns](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c07). Tronshke takes the first town’s outer defenses and nearly wins. Repeated Latanian air landings retake lost positions and force his units to fight in several directions. This buys the time Peano needs.

The western front then breaks in two places. Territory gained by Vaksel over a year falls back in days. Tronshke abandons the sieges to rescue his front, but all six strongholds open at once. Rearmed prisoner divisions form corps and attack his returning troops from behind. Amentola sends Klur and Korina into the fighting. Their intervention cannot reverse the encirclement; Klur’s tired columns are driven back.

[Gamov writes an ultimatum in Peano’s name](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c07). Surrender within twenty-four hours brings decent captivity. Continued fighting brings punitive camps, while resistance for empty military glory brings degrading execution without appeal to Mercy. Tronshke accepts the military facts but rejects the choice between rewarded dishonor and punished courage. He releases his generals to decide for themselves and kills himself with an impulsator. Peano orders a burial with music supplied by surrendered Roder soldiers.

Capitulation follows unevenly. Some commanders fight for another two weeks. Less than a month after the Fermor raid, Latanian troops control Patina, Lamaria, and Roder and stop at Klur’s frontier. Gamov refuses another invasion. With winter approaching, the leaders expect a pause until spring; the narrator warns that events will defeat that expectation.

<a id="p4-c08"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 8 — The person beneath the mask

[Source](src/text/part0038.html) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c08)

An unusually calm autumn follows the fighting. Both sides stop artificial storms while they rebuild energy-water reserves. The narrator describes nature itself as exhausted: even natural storms disappear. The quiet comes too late to save the harvests, but civilians can go outside again. [Freed Latanian prisoners return home](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c08) with special uniforms, one hundred gold lats each, and thirty days’ leave. Enemy prisoners travel toward prepared rear camps through hostile, silent crowds. Bar controls their transport; the Ministry of Mercy takes charge at the camps. Semipalov does not yet understand Gamov’s reason for this arrangement.

Pavel finally brings Elena to Semipalov’s office. Both immediately say that the other has changed. Semipalov prevents an embrace and meets her with deliberate coldness. She asks why officials learned he was alive before his own wife. He answers that she is his former wife: in some sense, he died at the execution. Yet his private account admits that he still loves her intensely. The barrier comes from the person he has become, as well as from his wound at her prison words.

Elena’s [distinction between Semipalov and his performed treason](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c08) becomes the center of their argument. She does not deny speaking words of hatred. She denies addressing them to the real man. He deliberately convinced her that she faced a traitor who sold honor for money and power. Her belief was necessary to his success, so he cannot fairly punish her for accepting the role he played. Like an audience hating a villain, she hated the mask, not the actor beneath it.

She describes grief so severe that she considered suicide, followed by overwhelming joy at seeing him alive and innocent on screen. She refuses to retract hatred of the supposed traitor. Semipalov cannot refute her, but fears that yielding would be a weakness his changed self could never forgive. He turns instead to her wish for revenge. Elena admits that she considered becoming Gamov’s lover. Gamov showed no interest; his attempt to bring her into government was political. She enjoyed Semipalov’s jealousy, but says she could not have chosen another man merely to hurt him.

Elena asks him to return. He finally admits that he wants the same, but asks for time before they live together again. Each says there is no path away from the other. She kisses him and leaves. Pavel then appears silently on the screen, sees his exhausted friend, and disconnects. The marriage has a promise of recovery, not an immediate reconciliation.

<a id="p4-c09"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 9 — Independence as punishment, agreement by force

[Source](src/text/part0039.html) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c09)

The Core confronts unrest in [Floria](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c09), whose wooded, marshy terrain threatens army communications. Pavel describes a talented but exclusive people whose prosperity depends on state subsidies. Gonzalez proposes deporting the entire population. Pustovoyt rejects punishment for a rebellion that has not occurred. Semipalov offers a different collective penalty: give Floria independence and neutrality, withdraw subsidies, and require trade at world prices. Since Latania and occupied Patina surround it, Latania would control its access to essential supplies. He expects poverty to force its return. Latanians would leave Floria, Florians elsewhere would return there, and compensation would cover abandoned property. Roads would bypass the territory. Gamov approves; Woodworth submits, and the government issues a manifesto.

Patina presents another problem. Torba is missing, industry is idle, and rival factions fill public life with slogans. Gamov, Semipalov, Pavel, and the press travel to Laina, its capital. Passing the Barta battlefield, Semipalov scarcely recognizes the ruined landscape or his former self. Fagusta attacks the Floria decree as punishment of a whole people. Semipalov answers that the regime already wages war against humanity’s faults; Fagusta sees a disturbing change in him.

The televised talks bring in [Optimate leaders Ponsiy Markvard and Lyudmila Miloshevskaya](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c09). Miloshevskaya is a remarkable pianist and forceful political figure whose striking appearance captivates Semipalov. Fagusta recalls her earlier attempt to push him into revolt against Marutzian, but insists that his own politics seek practical compromises. The Patinian Optimates demand domestic power and offer a renewed alliance and three army corps. Gamov asks why occupation forces need their party to do what they can already order. He also draws out their determination to execute Torba regardless of any defense at trial.

Then Pavel’s guards bring Torba into the room. Dirty, hungry, and thirsty after hiding in a cellar, he expects death but refuses to beg. Gamov gives him food. Torba mocks his opponents and the dictator’s spectacular punishments, then makes a substantive defense: he sought Patina’s claimed western lands and broke with Marutzian and Komlin because their incompetence threatened his country. Gamov likewise betrayed and imprisoned that government. Why punish Torba for an act resembling their own? An alliance with victorious Latania now serves Patina’s interests. His argument briefly leaves Gamov without an answer.

The Optimates demand Torba’s surrender and threaten to leave. Gamov instead has Varella force Markvard back into his chair by the threat of a beating. He persuades Miloshevskaya to sit through a pointedly courteous appeal and then warns Torba against further provocation. None will be permitted to dictate terms.

Gamov imposes a [four-man governing Core](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c09): Torba and an assistant, Markvard and an assistant. General Leonid Prishchepa becomes wartime supreme administrator. The four must give unanimous yes-or-no answers to major questions. Disagreement earns each ten lashes; failure to agree within another guarded day earns hanging. They cannot escape by resigning. Miloshevskaya is excluded from this body but offered a separate Women’s Control Committee, which may approve or reject its decisions. Its meetings are secret, men are excluded, and even the military governor cannot reverse its decisions. She accepts, warning that Leonid will regret having to deal with her. Patina receives civilian institutions, but their operation rests on occupation and threatened violence.

<a id="p4-c10"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 10 — Food pressure and a terrible reprisal

[Source](src/text/part0040.html) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c10)

Patina accepts the new constitution with delight at the prospect of rival politicians being punished. Miloshevskaya begins to act above party divisions, as Gamov intended. He relies on the country’s respect for women and music to give her authority beyond ordinary factional leadership. The source’s street taunts contain a [reversal of the established party-leader labels](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c10), but the governing arrangement remains clear.

Amentola has returned by ship to Kordaza and is rapidly expanding Cortezia’s waterplane fleet. Peano urges further occupation to deny enemy bases and gain industrial capacity. Bar objects that ruined harvests already threaten hunger. More conquered people would mean more mouths to feed. Gamov therefore asks Bar, Gonzalez, and Pustovoyt to combine punishment and mercy in a [prisoner-aid scheme](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c10) that shifts the food burden to Cortezia.

The scheme divides camps into ordinary soldiers and officers; guards and gendarmes accused of war crimes; and generals, military prosecutors, and punitive-unit commanders. The first group receives the actual meager ration once given Latanian captives, the second one third less, and the third half that basic ration. Semipalov objects that this means mass death. The answer is to allow Cortezian aid while confiscating half of ordinary prisoners’ parcels, four fifths of the second group’s, and nine tenths of the third group’s. The diverted food goes to Latanian children’s homes and hospitals. An aid administration of the Cortezian state, charities, relatives, and Latania’s Mercy ministry will organize deliveries. Inspectors may examine camps and recipients; family visits depend on its proper operation, and daily broadcasts will show prisoners and their appeals.

Semipalov stresses that he did not devise the scheme, although he later becomes associated with its execution and comes to admire its historical effect. He doubts that Amentola will accept a plan that feeds his enemy. Woodworth passionately answers that Cortezian families will demand the rescue of their own people. Their concern will force the president to accept a military disadvantage. Isiro receives the task of making that emotional pressure effective.

Cortezian aircraft then attack civilian towns from island bases near Klur. They fail to reach Adan and are driven from Zabon, but destroy smaller settlements. Watching the aftermath, Gamov weeps at the body of a little girl. Peano proposes destroying the air bases. Semipalov instead demands a reprisal aimed at the enemy population’s emotions: capture the pilots, condemn them for attacks on civilians, and drop them alive from waterplanes over their own country.

To prevent interception, [Semipalov adds human hostages](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c10). Each aircraft will carry about ten condemned men and twenty or thirty other prominent prisoners. Their names and images will be public, and safe return will secure their release. Amentola would have to kill his own people to stop the execution flights. Gamov approves. Peano remains troubled by the operational danger but begins preparations to seize the enemy bases.

<a id="p4-c11"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 11 — The execution flight and its unexpected welcome

[Source](src/text/part0041.html) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c11)

Peano’s air fleet strikes the island bases before the enemy recovers from its celebrations. Landing troops capture the pilots and crews. Isiro repeatedly broadcasts the faces of about three hundred prisoners so the world will recognize those chosen for punishment. Gonzalez’s Black Court condemns the participants in the civilian raids and announces that the executions will occur over their home cities at specified times.

[Kaplin’s division receives the mission](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c11). Gamov praises the former rebels and says only madness could make the enemy shoot down aircraft carrying fellow citizens promised freedom. In a separate meeting, Gonzalez threatens nearly two hundred captured conference participants with possible death sentences, while Pustovoyt explains their role as hostages. They may speak to the world from cameras in their cabins and appeal to their own leaders for protection.

Semipalov recognizes that interception need not be irrational: Cortezia might accept greater immediate losses to avoid the deeper political humiliation. Gamov answers that such long-term reasoning cannot overcome public feeling quickly enough. Broadcasts show that Cortezians demand rescue and dispute whether the original raids were victories or crimes. The operation’s protection depends on this public pressure.

Ten aircraft carry prisoners, each with about fifty hostages above and twenty condemned men below. Armed craft escort them across the continent and ocean without attack. The [escort totals change without explanation](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c11) between departure, ocean crossing, and return. Semipalov is horrified by the condemned men’s passive despair and briefly hates his own plan. Yet a young pilot’s plea that he only obeyed orders recalls the dead child and renews his desire for revenge. The hostages, still able to hope, argue and plead for their lives. One young journalist repeatedly calls on her father, an aviation general, not to let her die. Gamov identifies her as Gonzalez’s deliberate choice.

The aircraft reach Cortezia unopposed. Below them, citizens have stretched nets and cloth over streets, squares, and rooftops to catch the falling men. Pavel knew materials were being gathered but not the full rescue effort. Semipalov proposes changing the target cities. Gamov refuses to break the published promise, despite Semipalov’s observation that this resembles the military honor he attacks. The executions proceed. The narrator reports that more than a quarter die at once and more than half of the survivors become permanent psychiatric patients.

On the return journey, the ten prisoner aircraft land in Klur and release their hostages. Crowds welcome them with flowers, carry the young journalist on a wooden shield, and then wave farewell to the Latanian crews. The same visitors who brought terror now seem to leave as benefactors. Gamov believes this is the decisive [change in public feeling](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c11), more important than battlefield victory. Semipalov regards it as emotional volatility and remains unconvinced.

<a id="p4-c12"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 12 — Gamov moves the war into public feeling

[Source](src/text/part0042.html) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c12)

The expected turning point does not immediately come. Klur resumes military routines, Cortezia prepares new battles, and Latania’s former allies grow bold again. Woodworth warns that the earlier trick of diverting Amentola’s resources to these states has become dangerous: without Vaksel’s army to support, Cortezia can turn that network into a coordinated attack from several directions. Gamov admits Latania cannot defeat the whole world by force. He proposes instead to [“occupy” its enemies’ minds](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c12), so their people will no longer consent to war.

He presents terror and mercy as parts of this campaign. Spectacular punishments, rewards for kindness, outside aid to prisoners, family visits, and worldwide broadcasts should engage emotions that casualty statistics cannot. The execution flight showed him that rescue affects people more powerfully than punishment. Semipalov calls this political theater. Gamov accepts the term: art gives events an audience and lasting force. His actors will be real rulers, soldiers, judges, and executioners. Military preparations continue, but Semipalov and Peano must delay planned attacks on Nordag, Korina, and the southern threat until the new campaign works.

The first major spectacle is the [trial of the Fermor conference prisoners](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c12), including philosopher Orest Biber and writer Arnold Falk. Gonzalez dismisses the need for prosecution and defense. He judges how each profession aided war, assigning punishments by category. Pustovoyt asks defendants for evidence that they ever obstructed war, but otherwise barely intervenes. Journalists face death through their own articles forced into their throats; arms manufacturers face the same treatment with company shares. An inventor of fortified biscuits and dry soup wins release and expansion funds because children also benefit from his products. Semipalov finds this one act of mercy inadequate beside the many death sentences, although the Core had approved the planned severity.

Before the executions, Amentola publicly asks for delay until his special envoy, Tom Torkin, can negotiate release. Gamov considers whether this might open peace talks. Woodworth, who knows Torkin, expects deception instead. A second intervention comes from [Shirbai Shar](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c12), now a member of the White Court. Knurka IX has finally paid Torbash’s entry fee through Bar and sent Shar with a protest against every joint-court sentence. Gamov postpones the executions and accepts the new member’s role.

Pavel reports that Cortezia has formed its prisoner Aid Administration with funds of several billion didans. Its first visiting group consists of women. He also reveals that about fifty captives concealed their identities. Two supposed nurses are Luiza Putrament, daughter of Nordag’s president, and Zhanna Torkin, the envoy’s stepdaughter, captured at the conference. Both have been secretly moved to Adan. Their fathers may know they were captured, but may not know whether they are alive.

<a id="p4-c13"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 13 — Private love and failed diplomacy

[Source](src/text/part0043.html) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c13)

[Shirbai Shar’s private request](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c13) concerns Anna Kursai. She has disappeared, believes Voityuk dead, and may be planning revenge. Shar asks Semipalov to show mercy if she is caught. Semipalov assumes they are lovers because Shar gave her a family jewel. Shar denies it: she rejected his advances, but he loves her more than diplomatic success or his king’s interests. He admits that this love led him to persuade Knurka IX to join the White Court, giving him free access to Latania so he could find and protect her.

Semipalov mocks the costly membership as a political comedy. Shar turns the insult back on him: would Gamov accept his deputy treating Mercy as useless? Perhaps the political disagreement Semipalov performed for Voityuk was partly real and could eventually split the regime. Semipalov angrily threatens him, but they settle on remembering only the request for Anna and Semipalov’s promise of leniency. Afterward, Semipalov is relieved that the link to Gamov was not switched on. He then promptly puts Anna out of mind.

The government receives [Tom Torkin](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c13) amid competing press arguments. Georgiu praises punishment of those who promote war. Fagusta asks whether brutal punishment is itself a crime that will demand another punishment, creating an endless chain. Torkin begins with compliments, then claims Latania won only one battle through deception. Cortezia’s industry, he says, is three times larger and will decide the long war. He threatens pressure on partly surrounded Zabon and attacks by allied states.

When Gamov rejects intimidation, Torkin offers a bargain: release the roughly one hundred conference defendants, and Cortezia will persuade Nordag to lift its pressure on Zabon. He says President Putrament agrees. Gamov calls this trading an ally for Cortezia’s interests, yet considers the bloodless gain. Torkin spoils the opening by threatening again, boasting of the weapons sent to Nordag. Gamov says Latania can solve Zabon’s problem itself.

He then reveals [Zhanna Torkin’s identity and sentence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c13). She used her fiancé’s surname, Garmish, and wrote a poem glorifying martial honor. Gonzalez praises its artistry as the very reason it is dangerous: it can send young men to war. Gamov announces that she will be executed the next morning. Torkin leaves with outward control but faints beyond the door.

Semipalov questions the haste. Gamov refuses small bargains: famous promoters of war must die to shake public opinion and save the anonymous people killed every day. Only peace will count as success. Pustovoyt has authority to call for mercy, but remains silent. The chapter ends with the sentence announced, not executed.

<a id="p4-c14"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 14 — Mothers confronted with the war

[Source](src/text/part0044.html) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c14)

The executions take place at dawn. Isiro announces them but withholds the unbearable images; he shows only other condemned figures being transported away. [Zhanna Garmish is dead](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c14). Torkin leaves visibly aged. Days later, his wife, the singer Radon Torkin, shoots him for failing to save her daughter and threatens Amentola as the greater culprit. Zhanna’s fiancé, Richard Garmish, mourns her and honors her adoption of his surname. Public sympathy grows around Radon. She also discovers that her husband’s killing may qualify for the Terror company’s hundred-thousand-didan reward.

Meanwhile, the prisoner broadcasts draw immense Cortezian audiences. Semipalov watches hungry captives appeal to relatives or blame the leaders who sent them to defeat. These messages disrupt ordinary work as families wait to recognize loved ones. The Aid Administration gathers supplies rapidly; Amentola calls Latania’s confiscations robbery, but cannot stop deliveries. Bar requires food to arrive before delegations. Twenty women then come to meet Gamov.

[Norma Friz](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c14), a mathematics professor and mother of captive student Arman, leads their thanks. Gamov unexpectedly attacks their gratitude. Why praise the man who ordered their sons and husbands killed, captured, and starved? Using personal information prepared by Pavel, he turns each family’s history against its acceptance of war. He reminds Eliza Paur of nursing her son Kurt and hoping he would become a sculptor; war has taken his right arm. He accuses Norma of blessing her sons’ service: Pyotr is dead, Arman imprisoned. He even threatens to kill Arman before her as a lesson in the reality behind martial glory. The threat is not carried out, but Semipalov briefly fears Gamov could mean it.

The women break down, and the broadcast carries their distress into homes abroad. Fagusta calls the meeting mass psychosis; Georgiu calls it a profound union between ruler and listeners. Semipalov sees Gamov trying to turn women throughout the world against the wars that consume their families. His method sacrifices the immediate courtesy owed to the relief workers for what he considers a greater aim.

When Norma asks whether their aid is useless, Gamov advances an even harsher argument: relief makes war bearable, whereas unrestrained suffering might force humanity to reject it. He then refuses that conclusion in practice. He says he is human, with limits to his cruelty. [The aid plan remains in force](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c14), and the women will visit the prisoners and deliver their allotted supplies.

He rejects their claim of weakness and praises Radon’s violent revenge. Norma points out that he too is a state leader responsible for suffering. Gamov accepts the accusation and says an attack on him by such mothers would have a moral basis, although his guards have prevented that possibility. His colleagues later wonder whether his rhetoric carried him too far. The narrator says they will learn that political purpose governed even these apparent excesses. Almost at once, a calm Gamov calls Semipalov and Peano to present the completed invasion plan for Nordag. The emotional performance is over; the military decision resumes.

<a id="p4-c15"></a>
### Part 4, Chapter 15 — Nordag’s thirst and a daughter’s defiance

[Source](src/text/part0045.html) · [Chapter map](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p4-c15)

Nordag prepares strong defenses against the kind of war Latania has already fought. Semipalov and Peano instead land troops away from cities and fortresses to seize roads, bridges, power lines, waterworks, and gas mains. [The blockade cuts supplies and movement](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c15) without offering the enemy a conventional battle. Shtupa drives away rain clouds, while using energy-water for drinking would deprive Nordag’s weapons of power. Gamov threatens the cities with thirst but offers refuge to women and children.

After a week, President Franz Putrament accepts responsibility for the coming defeat. He urges the army to submit to the victor’s will and civilians to seek mercy, while planning to escape to Cortezia and continue the war. Aid cyclones diverted by Shtupa flood Korina and Klur instead. Klur threatens to leave the alliance, and Cortezia abandons the effort. Some thirsty Nordag units still attack rather than surrender unused weapons, but the blockade posts stand beyond their heavy guns’ range. Eventually the capital, Parko, capitulates and troops leave their fortifications.

Gonzalez proposes using [Luiza Putrament](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c15) to draw her father out of hiding. Gamov had secretly stayed her death sentence even though the government announced all the condemned dead. Now posters offer a million gold lats for the president’s capture, threaten death for hiding him, and set Luiza’s execution for noon on the first day of the month of leaf-fall unless he surrenders. Semipalov goes to Parko with authority to settle disputes between Terror and Mercy. He fears Gonzalez’s conduct and feels responsible for the country he conquered.

Luiza scorns the contradiction in announcing her death and then producing her alive. She refuses to beg her father to surrender and promises to denounce the occupiers on air. Her courage impresses Pavel and Semipalov. A second staged execution cannot readily be prepared, and Semipalov regrets having supported the hostage plan. He also fears that Putrament may lack news or may not believe the woman shown is really his daughter.

On the scaffold, Luiza tells her father to stay hidden and continue the struggle. Semipalov decides to stop the hanging by his own authority, whatever Gamov later says. Before he acts, Putrament rides into the square with two guards. He has come to exchange himself for his daughter. The crowd cheers his sacrifice, and Luiza breaks down in his arms.

Gonzalez hints at immediate execution, but [Pustovoyt intervenes decisively](docs/knowledge/parts/part-4-ledger.md#p4-c15). A court must decide the president’s fate; for now, he is to speak with Gamov. The crowd cheers again, this time because he will live. In the palace, Putrament says he would have arrived earlier if news and horses had reached him sooner. Semipalov wounds him by recalling the generals he once abandoned to execution and suggesting that he might also have valued himself above his daughter. Their meeting ends without an agreement about Nordag’s future.

As Semipalov leaves for the airfield, an unnamed woman passes the guards and fires a blue flash at him. He hears Pavel call for help and cry that he has been killed. Part Four ends at the shooting, before his condition or the attacker’s identity is established.

## Part 5 — The Devil of a God Not Ours

<a id="p5-c01"></a>
### Part 5, Chapter 1 — Anna Kursai's revenge for Floria

[Source](src/text/part0047.html); [chapter evidence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c01).

Semipalov survives the attack that ended the previous part: he moves aside in time, and the electric discharge only scorches his clothing. Pavel Prishchepa brings him back to Adan with the captured attacker, Anna Kursai, the wife of the spy Jean Voityuk. Gamov approves Semipalov's work in Nordag and lets him decide Anna's punishment. He also gives him an urgent new problem: the flooding of Korina has produced an epidemic that especially harms children and could spread through Nordag into Latania. Elena will supply the medical details. These are two separate consequences of the regime's policies, as the [chapter ledger](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c01) records.

Semipalov first interviews Anna alone. He assumes she wants revenge for her husband, whose present fate he says neither he nor Pavel knows. Anna rejects this explanation. She is a Florian, and she tried to kill the head of government for what his policy has done to her country. She describes closed borders, military checkpoints, stopped supplies of electricity and heat, failing factories, and worsening hunger. Latanian specialists have been withdrawn, while Florian soldiers and students have been sent home. She calls this collective terror and presents her attack as resistance to it.

Semipalov accepts that the suffering is the intended result, but disputes Anna's moral account. He argues that Floria lacks the resources needed for modern prosperity and previously received more from the larger state than it produced. In his view, this support created arrogant nationalism rather than gratitude. Restricting exchange to goods of equal value will force Florians to recognize their dependence. Anna instead calls Latanian rule conquest, denies his image of a voluntary family of peoples, and demands independence with foreign help. Semipalov predicts that foreign aid would make Floria dependent on Latania's enemies and that its loans would eventually come due. The text presents these as opposing political arguments, not as a neutral settlement of [Floria's status](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c01).

After dismissing Anna, Semipalov asks Gonzalez to add her to the coming public Black Court trial of Marutzian and Komlin. He declares her dangerous chiefly because she supports Marutzian's policy, rather than because she attacked him. His personal discretion thus turns directly into political prosecution.

<a id="p5-c02"></a>
### Part 5, Chapter 2 — A homecoming and an enemy beyond military plans

[Source](src/text/part0048.html); [chapter evidence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c02).

Semipalov treats his decision about Anna as a death sentence: he assumes that nobody handed to Gonzalez will survive. He cannot summon Elena to discuss public business in the room where he has just condemned another woman, so he goes to their apartment. Elena has recreated their old Zabon home after his apparent execution. Its furniture, his first inventor's certificate, and the photograph of his school friend Albert Loskin restore his connection to life before power. Loskin drowned trying to save a girl; Semipalov saved her but could not save his friend. Elena has excluded portraits of the celebrated ruler from the room. Semipalov understands that she loves the engineer he was, not his political importance. Their joyful reunion briefly repairs their long separation; these memories clarify his [earlier identity and marriage](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c02).

Politics returns over dinner. Elena questions Anna's sentence and insists that Semipalov has independent responsibility for the regime. He compares himself to the devil who carries out a god's purposes while the god retains public reverence. Elena assigns that role to Gonzalez and says Semipalov stands beside Gamov, rather than merely serving him. The argument gives the part's religious imagery a political meaning.

Elena then explains the Korina epidemic. Patients can drink ordinary water, but water vapor causes coughing, vomiting, fainting, and suffocation. Doctors describe it as water allergy. According to her report, a previously weak virus became dangerous during the artificial floods and continues to strengthen after humidity falls. Children are especially vulnerable. Klur's coastal towns are under suspicion, although no epidemic is yet established there; Klur, Nordag, and Latania may be next. Ending rain could check the virus but would destroy crops. Normal manufactured milk is plentiful but does not treat the disease. Human breast milk protects nursing mothers and infants and can save other patients, although treatment may take weeks or months. These limits define the [new medical crisis](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c02).

Later, with Elena asleep beside him, Semipalov decides that home, modest work, and love are his true life. He feels that power has made him an instrument of forces he does not control. Yet his next act is to call Gamov with two proposals. Gamov has expected the call and summons the Core for the morning. Semipalov's return to private life gives way at once to the demands of government.

<a id="p5-c03"></a>
### Part 5, Chapter 3 — Saving enemy children by force

[Source](src/text/part0049.html); [chapter evidence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c03).

At the Core meeting, Elena reports as Minister of Health. Isiro and Pavel add evidence of enemy panic: Korina has appealed to Cortezia, Amentola has held three secret meetings, and Klur has found cases during port inspections. Peano reports a similar illness among Latanian occupation troops in Patina and Nordag. Bar proposes watching what the affected governments do. Gamov refuses to wait for Latanian children to die. Semipalov goes further: borders cannot contain the disease, so Latania must attack it at its source, even at the expense of military advantage.

His first proposal is to use Shtupa's weather generators to impose dry conditions on the affected countries. Moving the populations to the deserts of Torbash, Sobrana, or Great Lepin is impossible; moving dry weather to them is feasible. Shtupa confirms that he can block ocean cyclones without strong opposition. Pustovoyt warns that the drought will destroy crops and may only suppress the disease for a season. Semipalov answers with his second proposal: make breast milk state property, require nursing women in Latania to supply hospitals, substitute artificial milk for their healthy infants, pay exceptionally high prices, and give the mothers preferential food supplies. A reserve will protect Latania, while most milk will go abroad. The [International Mercy organization](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c03) must also buy milk throughout the world.

Gonzalez objects to helping enemies. Semipalov replies that saving their children also saves Latanian children, and that promises of mercy are worthless without action. Woodworth predicts that compulsory rescue will overturn the enemy's political expectations. Gamov accepts the plan and orders diplomatic notice, rapid collection, and transport. The policy combines a real intention to save lives with coercive control of both weather and women's milk; neither element should disappear from its account.

The Core proposes an international women's rescue committee. Gamov names mathematics professor Norma Friz, a forceful woman from the overseas prisoner-aid organization; Radon Torkin, who killed her husband and threatened Amentola; and Lyudmila Miloshevskaya, now mediating between Ponsiy Markvard and Vilkomir Torba in Patina. Semipalov adds Luiza Putrament for Nordag. These are proposed appointments, as the [ledger](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c03) distinguishes from later execution of the plan.

After the meeting, Elena praises both Gamov's humanity and Semipalov's initiative. Her renewed admiration does not bring him home. He says the previous evening was one of his happiest, but he cannot return that night and cannot promise when he will. The task that expresses his humanity again displaces the life he has just chosen.

<a id="p5-c04"></a>
### Part 5, Chapter 4 — Mercy on the public stage

[Source](src/text/part0050.html); [chapter evidence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c04).

Semipalov reviews the uneven results of the Terror and Mercy companies. Foreign states initially declined to join; only Knurka IX sought a White Court seat. Then the Cortezian woman who killed her husband became a public heroine, and attacks began against politicians and officers on Gonzalez's list. Enemy police obstructed payments to the attackers. Fagusta condemned this as murder on credit. At the same time, women whom Gamov had publicly rebuked became his advocates. Norma Friz praised his understanding of women's role, while Miloshevskaya presented herself as the force that would restrain Patina's rival male leaders. Their public prominence concealed the real power of General Leonid Prishchepa's occupation command. Semipalov recognizes the importance of such [political theater](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c04), including his own handling of Luiza and her father in Nordag.

The epidemic campaign turns that publicity into action. Norma organizes milk collection, preservation, and transport; Amentola permits Mercy's purchasing funds into Cortezia. Korina's queen accepts aid because children stand outside war. Klur initially resists a drought but then agrees not to oppose Shtupa. Weather generators move west, Pustovoyt prepares foreign purchases, and Elena forms medical teams for Patina and Roder.

Gonzalez announces Marutzian's trial during this hopeful moment. Semipalov fears that another bloody spectacle will destroy the goodwill and asks Gamov to postpone it. Gamov refuses. Watching the broadcast, Semipalov sees the aged former president and Marshal Anton Komlin beside their former associates, Anna Kursai, and Captain Konrad Komlin, the marshal's nephew and surviving attacker. Fagusta acts as defense counsel after refusing Gonzalez's offer to prosecute. No Mercy representative attends. Konrad admits the attack but denies that the former leaders ordered it: their humiliation, compulsory confession forms, and public cruelty led the three young officers to act. Anna admits attacking Semipalov but calls it political struggle.

Fagusta argues that resentment is not proof of conspiracy. Called as a witness, Gamov says the former leaders bear responsibility because their prestige gave their words force. Fagusta changes the ground: these broken figures cannot threaten the future, so killing them serves no political purpose. Gamov then promises to cancel every death sentence. Gonzalez ends the case and releases everyone, expressly refusing to call them acquitted. Konrad, shaken by the reversal, is assigned to protect the man he tried to kill. Anna remains defiant; Gamov leaves her future to Semipalov. The exact [legal outcome and testimony](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c04) matter: guilt was disputed, and freedom came through the dictator's intervention.

Gamov later admits that he planned the release with Gonzalez. Fagusta had no prior briefing; Gamov predicted and used his honest defense. He excluded Semipalov because his deputy would have resisted turning the overthrow he had engineered into a theatrical ending. Semipalov is deeply hurt. For the first time, he wonders whether Gamov wants humanity's welfare or simply the starring role in its salvation.

Yet he immediately follows Gamov's example. He offers Anna a place on the rescue committee, working with Elena to collect milk in Floria. Anna enthusiastically accepts and promises that Florian dignity will show itself through generosity. Semipalov asks her someday to be glad she failed to kill him, echoing Gamov's words to Konrad. His growing opposition and his dependence on Gamov now coexist.

<a id="p5-c05"></a>
### Part 5, Chapter 5 — The first split in the Core

[Source](src/text/part0050.html#1FLS9E-e57e33ea454c44b1aa8a6263a5c5ad60); [chapter evidence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c05).

Several months pass without military operations. The epidemic never reaches Latania, but children and occupation soldiers die in Nordag, Klur's coastal cities empty in panic, Roder imposes strict quarantines, and Patina also suffers deaths. Shtupa prevents rain throughout the summer from Adan to the ocean. Gamov insists on continuing despite the cost, guided by daily reports of sick children. He visits hospitals in Roder and Nordag and presses Pustovoyt for more milk. War has reduced births, however, and mothers in neutral countries are reluctant to sell. Gamov and Pustovoyt see the women's committees as a possible foundation for world government. Semipalov doubts that existing states will tolerate them once conditions change.

Pavel, Bar, and Peano ask to meet Semipalov secretly from Gamov. Bar describes an approaching economic crisis. The weather operation has exhausted stored energy-water and reduced the harvest. Marutzian's accumulated wealth is gone. Mercy's high milk prices have drained its reserves, while prisoner exchanges have reduced the camp-remittance income. Only Torbash has joined the organization, and Gonzalez conceals Terror's costs. These are the [reported financial limits](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c05), not yet a collapse of the currency.

Pavel and Peano warn that Cortezia has used the pause to rebuild its forces while Latania spent its resources on rescue. They expect a new offensive from Klur through Roder and Lamaria into Patina, an expedition from Korina into Nordag, and attacks from former allies newly supplied by Amentola. Great Lepin's Lon Chudin seeks continental power; his brother Kir Kirun wants war. Peano urges an immediate surprise offensive while Latania still has a temporary advantage. Gamov has dismissed their separate warnings, believing that political and moral pressure now matters more than armies.

Semipalov brings their joint case to the Core after two days of thought. The vote divides exactly as predicted: six oppose renewed operations and four support them. Gamov argues that shared rescue has changed former enemies into potential friends and that women's organizations will stop their governments from attacking. To resume war would destroy the growing conviction that war is unnecessary. The [Core's division](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c05) is both strategic and personal: Semipalov's group retains its view but submits to the majority.

Afterward Gamov fears that Semipalov will resign. Semipalov refuses to leave voluntarily, saying that he too rejects conventional political behavior. Gamov repeats that he cannot imagine governing without him. Their partnership survives, but its former unity has ended.

<a id="p5-c06"></a>
### Part 5, Chapter 6 — Famine, revolt, and a rejected appeal

[Source](src/text/part0051.html); [chapter evidence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c06).

Natural autumn weather returns. The epidemic has been suppressed, and the women's rescue committees lose their purpose and influence. Radon Torkin, acquitted after killing her husband, continues to threaten Amentola without disturbing his security arrangements. Meanwhile, the enemy buildup grows. Semipalov plans another effort to persuade Gamov to attack, but Amentola changes the situation by stopping food exports, including exports to Klur and Korina.

Amentola blames Latania's policies. He says the dry summer halved his allies' harvests, while the displaced cyclones reduced Cortezia's grain crop by a third. He presents the embargo as a necessary sacrifice for victory. Gamov instead sees a political defeat: Cortezia has abandoned its prisoners and hungry allies. Latania can gain moral ground by feeding prisoners properly. Semipalov seeks exact crop figures. Bar now reports an average Latanian harvest, helped by moisture brought by northern winds; Pavel reports that Klur harvested only a quarter of normal and Korina about a third, with reserves nearly empty. These [reports and revisions](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c06) must remain distinct from Amentola's broadcast claims.

Near winter, Klur and Korina reduce rations, seize private grain, and appeal to the world for food. Latania asks whether they also offer peace. Both reject a separate settlement as disloyal to their coalition. Amentola demands Latanian admission of guilt, withdrawal from Roder, Lamaria, Patina, and Nordag, reparations, and removal of objectionable leaders. Gamov's hopes fail. His declining energy looks like depression to Semipalov, who later understands that physical illness is developing.

Before New Year, women's activism revives. Norma Friz leads a Cortezian peace committee. In Klur's capital, Fermor, a further one-third ration cut drives women into the palace and stereo station. Ministers flee; regular guards refuse to fight unarmed women. The women appeal specifically to Latania, and a hungry boy begs “Grandfather Gamov” for help. Gendarmes clear the station and use water cannon. General Arman Pliss then seizes power, bans protest, declares continued war, and announces that Amentola has restored food exports. Intelligence confirms the reversal, but Shtupa says winter ocean storms will prevent adequate deliveries unless Cortezia spends the energy reserves needed for its spring offensive.

Gamov asks the Core to feed Klur and Korina. A battlefield victory, he argues, leaves hatred alive; feeding armed enemies could remove the cause of future wars. He admits that the recipients might still attack. Semipalov replies that Pliss's military honor will treat aid as weakness, not friendship. Bar confirms that substantial exports require lower Latanian rations, and Shtupa cannot guarantee the next harvest under attack. If generosity fails, Latanian children may starve the following winter.

Only Pustovoyt and Gonzalez support Gamov. When mocked about atonement, Gonzalez says that good deeds cannot redeem his sins and that he will seek judgment and punishment from himself. The [seven-to-three rejection](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c06) does not end the matter: Gamov calls for a referendum. He lets Semipalov write the question, which explicitly asks citizens to reduce their own rations for enemies who may use the aid to defeat them. Gamov accepts this hostile framing as a true test of the people's moral resolve. He links his right to lead to their answer and refuses to delay for medical care, although Semipalov can now see how ill and exhausted he looks.

<a id="p5-c07"></a>
### Part 5, Chapter 7 — Gamov collapses before the assembled government

[Source](src/text/part0052.html); [chapter evidence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c07).

Gamov presents the food-aid proposal to a large meeting of Latanian and allied officials, military leaders, editors, and women's committees. The gathering includes Fagusta and Pimen Georgiu, rival editors, and Patina's opposing leaders Markvard and Torba. Anna and Luiza attend on the strength of their rescue work. Anna has fulfilled her promise: Floria supplied more milk per woman than any other region, and Nordag came second under Luiza's campaign. Florian notices still forbid Semipalov entry, but his respect for their effort has softened his hostility. These [measured contributions](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c07) qualify the earlier conflict without erasing it.

Gamov speaks weakly and without his usual command. Semipalov interprets the audience's silence as rejection and expects an easy political victory. As Gamov explains why he accepted Semipalov's referendum question, his words break apart. He grips the table, loses balance, and collapses into Semipalov's arms. The narrator connects the heart attack to despair at losing his closest supporters, although that emotional cause is his interpretation.

Before losing consciousness, Gamov orders Bar to prepare food from the stores and Peano to give him transport. The consignments must be ready to leave as soon as the referendum permits them. His last appeal is that children are dying in Klur. A doctor later says the immediate danger of death has passed, but recovery requires protection from further severe emotional shocks.

Semyon Serbin, Gamov's constant guard and attendant, accuses Semipalov of driving the dictator toward death in order to replace him. Semipalov threatens arrest; Serbin refuses to be intimidated. When Gamov regains consciousness, Semipalov promises to prepare the food and organize the vote. He privately still expects rejection and reassures Gamov to protect his health, a distinction preserved in the [ledger](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c07).

Returning to the meeting, Semipalov announces that he will exercise Gamov's governmental powers during the illness. Food and transport preparations will proceed, but crossing the military frontier still requires the people's approval. He also warns that the referendum result may endanger Gamov's recovery, while insisting that the country's future cannot be subordinated to one person's feelings. Twice-daily health bulletins will keep the public informed.

<a id="p5-c08"></a>
### Part 5, Chapter 8 — The sickroom becomes a political force

[Source](src/text/part0053.html), including [continuation](src/text/part0054.html); [chapter evidence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c08).

Bar says full food preparation needs at least a month, plus a week to move supplies to the frontier. Certain that voters will refuse aid, Semipalov grants only seven days. He intends to demonstrate compliance with Gamov's orders without completing an operation he expects to cancel.

The detained Franz Putrament asks to join the effort. He recalls working as a cowboy in Cortezia, but his main concern is Nordag. Watching Latania save enemy children and seeing Luiza free, respected, and leading that work has changed him. He requests a Nordag referendum: its harvest is good, and Klur and Korina are old friends. Semipalov warns that Latania may vote against aid. Putrament still accepts the risk that Latania will confiscate Nordag's collected food; at least his people will have tried to help. Semipalov frees him to organize the vote, while explicitly reserving the right to seize the supplies. This [conditional release and referendum](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c08) extend the campaign beyond Latania.

Meanwhile, Serbin begins intimate broadcasts about Gamov's sleep, food, weakness, and bodily needs. Semipalov finds them degrading and orders Isiro to stop them. Isiro resists because viewers demand these reports, and asks him to watch the audience first. The crowds are deeply concerned, not merely curious. Abroad, Gamov's collapse produces a similar response. Pliss dismisses aid to enemies as madness or sainthood and expects the referendum to fail. He praises the Latanian commanders' military skill while promising to execute them honorably if captured. Cortezian intelligence reports also predict a negative vote, but fear that Gamov's departure would bring the less flexible Semipalov to power.

Serbin's next accounts give political meaning to ordinary details. Gamov cannot eat while thinking about the starving boy; Serbin cries on screen and says the wait for the vote may be unbearable. Semipalov sees emotion displacing the arguments he knows how to answer. He visits Gamov, who denies suggesting topics or watching the broadcasts but trusts Serbin's devotion to carry his policy. Gamov is embarrassed when told about the toilet details. Semipalov nevertheless concludes that the dictator knowingly uses genuine illness as his strongest political appeal. The text preserves the difference between [Gamov's statements and Semipalov's interpretation](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c08).

A later broadcast supplies the decisive formula: giving up one quarter of each person's daily ration could feed millions, and Gamov is worth that quarter. Serbin says the guards will vote yes. Semipalov commands the state apparatus, yet feels unable to sever the emotional bond between the sick leader and the public.

Elena, Miloshevskaya, Anna, and Luiza visit to establish whether he will honor an adverse result. He promises to obey the people. Miloshevskaya insists Patina should participate despite uncertainty over its vote; Luiza predicts a firm Nordag yes; Anna reports Florian posters urging Latania to vote yes. Elena has medical staff and equipment ready. Semipalov argues that either result defeats Gamov's original philosophy: a no rejects sacrifice, while a yes won through love of one man does not prove universal generosity. Miloshevskaya challenges his assumption that a Latanian rejection would also judge the enemy's capacity for goodness. The chapter ends with his certainty broken. He still loves Gamov, but fears Serbin's influence and the political power of feelings he cannot control.

<a id="p5-c09"></a>
### Part 5, Chapter 9 — Semipalov obeys the vote he opposed

[Source](src/text/part0055.html); [chapter evidence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c09).

During the final two days, Semipalov and Pavel watch the broadcasts almost continuously. Semipalov now expects that Gamov may win. He believes the sickroom reports have replaced an immense national sacrifice with a small personal act: surrender a quarter of one's ration to save a beloved leader. His further belief that Gamov deliberately sustains his illness remains the narrator's interpretation, not a doctor's finding.

Isiro fills the screens with hungry crowds in Fermor, Florian demands for a yes vote, and the Putraments' campaign in Nordag. Franz has resumed his presidency. Cortezia receives less attention, although Radon Torkin, the acquitted former singer, continues her threats, and Norma Friz criticizes delayed food shipments. Amentola blames ocean storms; the images support Shtupa's warning that loaded ships cannot safely leave port. Semipalov objects to Isiro's selective presentation, but admits that his acting authority does not permit him to dismiss Gamov's minister. The [campaign record](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c09) separates truthful pictures from their partisan selection.

On the eve of voting, Klur's churches lead mass prayers for Latanian generosity. Women, children, and some men kneel before the cameras. Korina's people discuss the same crisis more calmly, accepting that neither the referendum nor ocean transport offers certainty. Two hours before voting begins in the east, Serbin gives a brief report of Gamov's weakness and breaks into tears. Semipalov feels powerless against this appeal, while still believing that state responsibility requires refusal.

Voting begins at six in the morning local time and moves westward. Early reports show more than two thirds in favor in the east. Pustovoyt demands immediate dispatch; Bar wants to wait for complete results. Semipalov tells Bar to move the supplies forward. The narrator describes a day's convoy movement through Patina, Lamaria, and Roder and a border wait of over half a day, without clearly aligning those durations with the voting clock. Crowds stand silently along the route.

At noon, Semipalov casts his own no vote and summons the Core. Isiro estimates about eighty percent support, with final counting expected by midnight. Semipalov orders the convoys across the border before the exact total is known and takes responsibility for announcing compliance. He has not changed his judgment of the risk; he accepts the people's decision. These [dispatch stages](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c09) distinguish preparation, border waiting, and actual entry.

Gonzalez is surprised and asks whether Gamov should first be consulted. Semipalov says his public statement will spare the patient anxiety. Gonzalez then repeats the nickname used against him: “the devil of a god not ours.” Their exchange reveals how supporters of the same ruler can cast one another as hostile servants, even when Semipalov has just carried out Gamov's wish.

<a id="p5-c10"></a>
### Part 5, Chapter 10 — Aid changes the alliances

[Source](src/text/part0056.html); [chapter evidence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c10).

After his broadcast, Semipalov visits Gamov, who is delighted but still visibly weak. Semipalov admits that he continues to oppose the policy, yet urges the dictator to recover before taking back his duties. Together they watch the first food convoys cross into Klur. Soldiers cheer and fire salutes; civilians welcome the drivers with flowers and embraces. Semipalov remains cautious because Pliss has said nothing and the troops retain their posts and weapons. As Minister of War, he will keep Latania ready until the political danger is resolved.

Nordag's contribution requires a sea passage. Semipalov has arranged for Shtupa to use improved mobile generators to create a brief, relatively calm corridor from Nordag to Korina. The machines rival or exceed the old fixed installations. Loaded fishing boats race out as the weather changes. Putrament celebrates beside Shtupa; the coastal gun salute worries Semipalov until he learns that Latanian crews still control the weapons. The [aid routes and military safeguards](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c10) show that cooperation has not yet removed occupation controls.

In Klur, civilian committees receive and distribute food. Peano reports no hostile troop movements or military seizures. Celebrations delay the return of emptied vehicles because residents draw their crews into festivities. Then Queen Agnessa of Korina announces peace. She recounts how Latanian weather attacks helped cause the earlier disaster and epidemic, but argues that Latania's costly rescue has made continued war morally impossible. Her ministers had insisted on obligations to Cortezia; she now overrides that position. Korina will keep its friendship with Cortezia while ending war with Latania. Supplies are already unloading, and Putrament has arrived aboard a fishing boat after exchanging clothes with a fisherman. He presents himself as an advocate for peace and promises to return home. Gamov gives a brief, deeply moving public thanks to the voters.

Pliss's silence finally ends with an account of two conversations with Amentola. The general demanded the promised food as a matter of military honor. Amentola asked for a day, then said storms prevented delivery for an unknown period. Pliss calls this betrayal. He admits that the referendum overturned his judgment of Latania: the supposed enemy fed his people while the supposed ally failed them. He cancels obligations to Cortezia, declares war against it, forms an alliance with Latania, and places Klur's army under Peano. This is a more extensive reversal than [Korina's separate peace](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c10).

Gamov welcomes the success but immediately turns to the remaining uncertainty: Cortezia may answer with peace or with newly equipped armies. In Fermor, Pliss leads a joint victory march with Colonel Korney Kaplin and the Latanian convoy guards. The formal parade dissolves into a popular celebration; the crowd carries Pliss and Kaplin through the streets, and the rigid general cannot stop crying. Semipalov reconsiders his contempt for political theater, seeing that the public emotion is itself part of history. The war with Klur has ended, and Gamov's attempt to defeat hostility through aid has succeeded there. Semipalov congratulates him and sincerely says he is glad to have been wrong.

<a id="p5-c11"></a>
### Part 5, Chapter 11 — Cortezia's crisis ends in peace

[Source](src/text/part0057.html), including [continuation](src/text/part0058.html); [chapter evidence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c11).

The loss of Korina and Klur shocks Cortezia's belief in its own greatness. Public confusion replaces the anger Semipalov expected. Woodworth predicts deep internal conflict, perhaps civil war. Gonzalez's international terror scheme now gains force after its earlier failure to produce lasting results. Assassins kill a weapons manufacturer and a shipowner and claim their rewards; other listed figures die. A vibration mine then strikes Amentola's vehicle. Its excessive force throws the vehicle beyond the worst zone, saving him, while bystanders are killed. Semipalov challenges the moral basis of terrorism that plainly serves Latania's national advantage. His remark that they do not direct terror against themselves draws an unusual response from Gamov, who calls the idea interesting. The [ledger](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c11) preserves this as a suggestive exchange, not a completed policy.

Norma Friz rebuilds her organization as a Peace Committee, keeps women in command, and calls an anti-war congress. Radon Torkin supplies its fiercest argument. She compares electing Amentola to a disastrous marriage, demands a referendum to remove him, proposes Norma as president, and urges women to use their influence over men to force political change. The conference endorses peace and an early presidential replacement vote on the first day off of winter's first month. Amentola's government treats it as a private movement and prepares for a spring offensive. Fewer than half the women participate, and very few men vote; many men regard the event as entertainment.

The next day, Norma leads twenty formally dressed women to deliver the declaration. Radon is excluded because the mission is meant to be peaceful. Palace officers refuse even to receive the document. One young delegate pushes forward; officers seize her, tear her dress, and strike her. Her fiancé rushes from the watching crowd. He and an officer shoot one another, and both palace officers are subsequently described as lifeless beneath the crowd. Men storm the grounds, then armored vehicles force them out without firing. The broadcast appeal to stop work and defend the women produces a nationwide general strike.

The strike initially centers on insult and apology more than peace or presidential removal. Woodworth explains that Cortezians understand public events through personalities. Supplies and household services begin to suffer, but Amentola does not yield. Terrorist attacks cease during the strike. Norma's headquarters becomes a center for both sexes, while Radon gathers an armed following and again threatens to kill the president. The [sequence of protest and violence](docs/knowledge/parts/part-5-ledger.md#p5-c11) matters: the failed private referendum becomes a national crisis through the palace confrontation.

Norma announces a second march, this time to remove Amentola. Radon leads an armed advance group; Norma follows with supporters who also carry concealed weapons. Thousands reach the palace, where roughly twenty modern combat vehicles await them. Semipalov expects a massacre and watches helplessly. Instead, the gates open. The crowd enters in silence, and Radon calls Amentola out to be killed.

He comes alone and stands within easy range. Radon raises and lowers her weapon three times but cannot fire. At his quiet command, she drops it and cries. Amentola then announces that his government has spent more than a week considering the situation and has unanimously decided to end war with Latania and its allies. He has already sent Gamov the message. The crowd's joy restores his popularity. Semipalov and Gamov call one another, laughing and crying. Semipalov shouts “Victory”; Gamov answers “Peace.” Their different words preserve the distinction that has governed their dispute, even as both celebrate the same result.

## Part 6 — Cleansing

<a id="p6-c01"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 1 — The condemned return alive

[Source: P6-C01](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c01). [Chapter facts and continuity](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c01).

Semipalov calls this the height of Gamov's political glory, but corrects the belief that he knew all the dictator's plans. Gamov, Gonzalez, and Pustovoyt have kept the purpose of the world conference secret even from him. Woodworth and Gonzalez prepare its invitations. Former enemies come to Adan in unexpected groups: rulers who distrust one another remain close enough to watch each other; Amentola walks with Norma Friz and Radon Torkin, although both women had publicly threatened him. The government leaves the day's rain, snow, and cold to nature without using weather generators.

The hall has no audience chairs. Guards hold about three hundred guests back from an empty space before the small platform. Semipalov initially reads this as needless fear of attack. Gamov, visibly ill, begins by announcing discussion of the postwar world. Radon Torkin interrupts and demands her daughter. Gamov reminds her of the execution notice, then orders the commandant to give the mother what remains of her child. The daughter runs into the hall alive. Semipalov believes Torkin's interruption was spontaneous, although Gamov immediately used it to intensify his planned revelation.

Next come the condemned Nordag officers, led by Lieutenant Generals Sumo Pariona and Kinza Vardant. President Franz Putrament joyfully greets men he had refused to rescue. Gonzalez and Pustovoyt embrace and signal their affectionate reproach to Semipalov: he had mistaken their coordinated deception for cruelty and helplessness. More people officially reported executed enter, until the hall is full of hundreds of survivors. Semipalov now understands that the technique used for his own false execution was an established practice, not an exception invented for him. The [earlier apparent deaths require revision](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c01), but he expressly rejects the comforting idea that all the war's deaths were fictitious. Thousands truly died; only a fraction of Gonzalez's prisoners received secret protection.

Overcome, Gamov weeps and needs help to leave. Despite Semipalov's efforts to turn away the cameras, his weakness is broadcast around the world the next day. Woodworth predicts further surprises. The conference has become a public revelation of mercy, while leaving the practical settlement of world government for later.

<a id="p6-c02"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 2 — The cost of a useful deception

[Source: P6-C02](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c02). [Chapter facts and continuity](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c02).

At the next morning's Core meeting, Gamov appears fully recovered. Semipalov demands to know why seven members of the ten-person leadership were denied the secret of the false executions. Gamov says that he devised the scheme and that Gonzalez and Pustovoyt accepted it. The seven were excluded to keep one man, Semipalov, ignorant. His genuine hatred of Gonzalez gave political credibility to the terror apparatus; knowledge of its hidden mercy would have changed his conduct. Gamov considers this inability to sustain deception an honorable part of Semipalov's character. He points to the strain of Semipalov's earlier dealings with the spy Jean Voityuk and to Semipalov's own decision to let Elena believe that his staged execution would be real. The argument makes his moral protest part of the machinery he had opposed.

Gonzalez says that many more prisoners are being released elsewhere than appeared at the conference. Yet he refuses complete exoneration: the Ministry of Terror and Black Court really killed people, and several hundred prevented executions do not erase his responsibility. Semipalov asks whether one innocent death could outweigh hundreds of rescued lives. Gonzalez accepts the possibility and admits that immediate demands had displaced fundamental questions of justice. Semipalov cannot tell whether Gonzalez himself has changed or whether the revelation has changed his own perception. The chapter therefore preserves both [the rescued lives and the real crimes](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c02).

Woodworth introduces the philosopher Orest Biber, captured with the writer Arnold Falk when Mart Tronshke's army collapsed. Captivity has converted Falk from a glorifier of war into an opponent of it, now planning an antiwar novel. General Arman Pliss, once hostile to Biber's arguments, now promises to study his books in retirement. Biber's plan matches the Core's own ideas: a world government headed by Gamov as chief president, a council of national presidents including existing monarchs, permanent peace, dissolved armies, and destruction of weapons except those required by national police. Borders, customs, visas, and passports would disappear; one currency and free choice of residence would follow.

Semipalov asks Biber to present the detailed proposal to the conference. When Biber is surprised that Semipalov makes the decision, Semipalov distinguishes Gamov's creative leadership from his own organizing role. Gamov explicitly supports the arrangement.

<a id="p6-c03"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 3 — The victor demands a trial

[Source: P6-C03](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c03). [Chapter facts and continuity](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c03).

Semipalov explains why his account now gives less space to the new world order: an unexpected personal crisis displaces public history in his attention. The conference receives Gamov's speech with a hymn and prolonged applause. Biber gives a three-hour report. The rulers approve union under Gamov, with reservations on secondary points, then return home to reorganize their authority. Adan becomes the temporary world capital. Semipalov expects a lifetime of work consolidating the settlement.

Gamov instead summons an extraordinary Core meeting and asks permission to put a criminal on trial: himself. Semipalov first treats the demand as illness. Gamov explains that every military benefit to his people also injured others, and that even lasting peace has been purchased through deaths and suffering. He wants the whole world to judge the relation between the good he achieved and the evil he used. A victorious leader's voluntary submission would reject the rule that victors are beyond judgment. He seeks an objective assessment, not punishment for its own sake, and offers to bear guilt alone while sharing credit with his colleagues.

The proposed court is already partly arranged. Gonzalez will judge. The former loyalist editor Pimen Georgiu will prosecute; the hostile editor Konstantin Fagusta will defend. Gamov says both promised to perform these reversed roles honestly. [The plan predates victory](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c03): Gonzalez first heard it when asked to become Minister of Terror, and the editors heard it when appointed to their opposing newspapers. Semipalov resents another long concealment and questions whether such arrangements can produce an impartial trial.

Gonzalez then demands to be tried too. As Gamov's executing hand, he shares responsibility; as a judge, whose decisions always prevail over the courtroom's competing sides, he is himself a victor who should face judgment. He intends to remain judge while also becoming a defendant. Semipalov attacks this contradiction of roles, but sees that Gonzalez is serious. Calling himself Gamov's right hand and Gonzalez the left, he accepts that their shared responsibility also requires his own trial.

Semipalov asks Gonzalez to design the procedure. His bitter jokes offend both men, especially Gamov, who can accept tragedy more readily than ridicule. After they leave, the other Core members remain alarmed and confused. Pavel Prishchepa rejects Semipalov's complaint that intelligence should have detected this crisis: his orders concerned enemies, not the private minds of their own leaders.

<a id="p6-c04"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 4 — Semipalov reviews the road to victory

[Source: P6-C04](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c04). [Chapter facts and continuity](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c04).

Semipalov walks home in fury, then tries to understand why Gamov trusted other men with plans concealed from his deputy. Looking back, he recognizes a pattern in actions he once treated as sudden inspirations. Gamov's apparent zigzags repeatedly served a fixed destination. His helpers could overcome the immediate obstacles, but might never have joined him if they had understood the whole route.

Semipalov reconsiders several earlier episodes. Gamov's savage street fight now looks like a preview of deliberate policy against violent gangs. Birker Shtok's interrogation established humiliation as a substitute for bodily torture. Giving captured money to distinguished soldiers anticipated a system of paying for battlefield achievement. Semipalov's own false execution seemed a unique act of heroic sacrifice; knowledge that it used a routine method would have made it morally repellent to him. These [retrospective judgments](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c04) reveal how differently he now understands his own participation.

The final example is Gamov's demand that food stores be opened to enemy populations. When his officials refused, he appealed directly to the people through concern for his health. A question about the sacrifice of millions of rations became, for each person, a choice between a little food and the leader's survival. Semipalov nevertheless believes the danger to Gamov was real: a public refusal would have killed him. He sees manipulation throughout their shared path, but also recognizes that its declared goal was achieved. They won the war and created the political union intended to prevent future wars. Why, then, demand a trial instead of celebrating?

Elena finds him struggling with whether they are heroes or criminals. She repeats Gamov's explanation that concealment respected Semipalov's honesty. More decisively, she suggests that Gamov could accept morally dangerous methods because he had already resolved to submit them to judgment if victorious. His trial would bring either acquittal or cleansing through punishment. Semipalov knows her admiration is not romantic love, yet still resents it. He points out that cleansing might mean execution for Gamov and for her husband. Elena hopes Gonzalez would never condemn himself, but Semipalov cannot promise this: Gonzalez might enter the fire even before his leader.

<a id="p6-c05"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 5 — An enemy warns against self-destruction

[Source: P6-C05](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c05). [Chapter facts and continuity](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c05).

Months of administrative work precede the trial. The new government must function while its two principal leaders are absent, and possibly after their permanent removal. Bar and Woodworth receive central authority for that period. Bar says that if he succeeds them, his first act will be to abolish the Black Court and everything resembling Gonzalez's system. Semipalov agrees. The court nevertheless remains in existence and announces that Gamov, Semipalov, and its own chairman will be tried for crimes committed in power. Shock produces worldwide protests and disputes.

The Cortezian senator Leonard Bernoulli visits Semipalov. Age, defeat, and imprisonment have altered his appearance, but not his forceful intelligence. He intends to return to politics against Amentola, whom he wants punished for losing the war, and also against the victors for their proposed self-trial. He regards their wartime campaign as brilliant. Even his own kidnapping, which he resisted fiercely, now appears to him a political masterpiece. Semipalov gives Gamov credit for inspiration, acknowledges his own part, and names Colonel Prishchepa as the organizer. Bernoulli also praises the convincing broadcast of Semipalov's false execution, while remaining hostile to his former friend Woodworth.

Bernoulli's warning is practical. By submitting to a real court, the victors may restore the strength of enemies who want revenge. Those enemies will find ample grounds to hang them, and the world could lose the benefits of their victory. His admiration therefore supports [the achievement while challenging the trial](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c05). Semipalov answers that courts can acquit as well as condemn and suggests that Bernoulli participate on the defense side. The senator leaves intending to offer himself as a defense witness.

The encounter unsettles Semipalov. He had assumed that this surprising maneuver would end as successfully as Gamov's earlier ones. Now he asks whether Gamov actually wants acquittal, or might regard condemnation as the proper outcome. He doubts the care with which he volunteered as a defendant. When his secretary presents a long list of visiting rulers, thinkers, and former opponents seeking audiences, he rejects them all and says he will choose whom to see.

<a id="p6-c06"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 6 — The prosecution exposes the price of policy

[Source: P6-C06](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c06). [Chapter facts and continuity](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c06).

The court meets in the oval hall where Gamov and Semipalov seized power from Marutzian and Marshal Komlin. Gonzalez presides with four black-collared judicial officers. Gamov and Semipalov occupy the same small table from which they announced the old leaders' removal. After a day of procedure, Georgiu delivers a long indictment. His former anonymity as the government's spokesman gives way to a distinct, commanding presence.

Georgiu does not attempt to try war itself. He concentrates on evils he considers avoidable: humiliating punishments, renewed prosecution of already sentenced prisoners, cancellation of commutations, destructive northern labor, and punishment of relatives and associates. He cites a young woman exiled because a robber paid for their outings, and Police Lieutenant Colonel Anton Karmanyuk, hanged for releasing his wife's brother after arresting him. Karmanyuk's wife and three children were exiled and their property seized. Georgiu places direct responsibility on Gonzalez's court and Semipalov's approval.

Semipalov concedes that many charges are undeniable. He remembers Maravan-khor's execution and reprisals in Konduk, and the revenge inflicted by liberated prisoners on their former tormentors. Georgiu also attacks money paid for enemy casualties and compensation to bereaved families: even a useful allowance, he argues, could make a relative's death financially desirable. This is a claim about moral danger, not evidence that every family wanted such a death. He condemns Sacred Terror and the Black Court Joint-Stock Company for rewarding abroad the same predatory acts suppressed at home. National advantage, presented as universal justice, has corrupted moral standards.

Gonzalez listens with a sadness that makes Semipalov fear he may truly condemn himself. Gamov records the charges without visible surprise. When the opposing editors begin their exchange, Fagusta challenges Georgiu's change from praise to accusation. Georgiu explains that the official paper expressed the government's wishes, not necessarily his beliefs. Its unsigned editorials were written by a government member and could not be edited. Pressed to name the author, he identifies Gamov.

Semipalov finds this plausible, having often seen Georgiu visit Gamov with papers. Fagusta, however, is visibly stunned. He drops his questions, and the court adjourns. [The undisclosed authorship](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c06) resolves one apparent inconsistency in Georgiu's conduct while creating a new question about Fagusta's reaction.

<a id="p6-c07"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 7 — Gamov wrote both sides of the dispute

[Source: P6-C07](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c07). [Chapter facts and continuity](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c07).

After a sleepless night discussing the proceedings with Elena, Semipalov hears Fagusta's full defense. Fagusta assembles the regime's acts of mercy: rescuing enemy children from water allergy, sharing scarce food with enemy populations, and secretly preserving people announced as executed. Together, he argues, these show a coherent policy of beneficence. Omar Isiro's cameras carry every word and gesture to the world.

Fagusta also challenges one-sided descriptions of harsh policies. Georgiu condemns reduced prisoner rations and confiscation of more than half the food sent by Cortezia. Fagusta replies that the same system allowed enemy families to supply and visit prisoners, while diverted food sustained hospitals and children's institutions. Norma Friz's Relief Administration could inspect camps, hospitals, and children's facilities to verify where it went. The defense does not deny the seizure; it disputes whether its moral meaning can be judged apart from these uses and new forms of contact.

When Georgiu accuses Fagusta of abandoning his earlier opposition, Fagusta says he had supported Gamov's general direction while criticizing particular acts. He then reveals that Gamov also wrote the hostile unsigned editorials in the *Tribune*. Thus [one author sustained both newspaper positions](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c07). Neither editor knew of the other's arrangement. This explains Fagusta's earlier shock and now astonishes Georgiu, Gonzalez, and especially Semipalov. Gamov openly enjoys the revelation.

Fagusta recounts the secret agreement. Gamov feared unpopular policies would create an internal front vulnerable to foreign support. Public criticism could provide a channel for resentment and let readers either find their objections expressed or formulate arguments in the government's defense. Because Fagusta supported Gamov and doubted he could attack him sincerely, Gamov offered to write the criticism himself. He also claimed a deeper purpose: policies generate resistance, and a government that suppresses outside criticism must investigate that resistance itself. Semipalov and Gonzalez were deliberately kept ignorant.

In private afterward, Semipalov asks what Gamov intends to say in court. Gamov will argue for the necessity of the trial through self-accusation and expects Semipalov to defend them. Semipalov still regards their victory as sufficient vindication and calls the proceedings extravagance without historical purpose. He promises a defense that will sound to Gamov like an accusation. Gamov welcomes it.

<a id="p6-c08"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 8 — Voityuk's accusation and the shadows of good

[Source: P6-C08](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c08). [Chapter facts and continuity](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c08).

Jean Voityuk, whom Semipalov had presumed dead after his disappearance, appears as a prosecution witness. Hiding and deprivation have left the former polished diplomatic aide emaciated and embittered. He admits espionage but denies treason. Born in Latania to a line of Cortezian men, he says he served Cortezia as his true homeland and took no payment. In his account, Semipalov is the traitor.

Voityuk offers two arguments. First, Semipalov knowingly passed a genuine secret invasion plan through an enemy spy. The information induced Putrament to withdraw from Zabon, but, Voityuk argues, if that trick had failed, Nordag would have been warned against the actual operation. Gonzalez rejects the hypothetical reasoning; Voityuk insists that a useful result cannot change the act's treasonous nature. Second, he accuses Gamov and Semipalov of betraying their public ideals. Their apparent self-sacrifice won enemy trust, but every concession served their own ultimate advantage. He calls that concealed calculation a betrayal of those who believed in their disinterested goodness.

Semipalov takes the evening session to answer, although his main opponent is Gamov. He now reads the two newspapers as an expression of history's own opposing aspects. Gamov recognized this duality but, Semipalov argues, misunderstood it by treating the harmful consequences of beneficial action as personal crimes. Saving children from water allergy by creating drought also reduced harvests and exposed those children to later hunger. Evil can accompany good as a shadow accompanies a body. Trying to remove every such shadow would amount to trying the laws of the universe.

His defense is not simply that victory proves virtue. He explicitly allows that evil forces can win. Justice must weigh the total good and harm, rather than condemn an action merely because someone suffered. The relevant question is which result serves the common good. Against Georgiu's inventory of crimes, he argues that their achievement weighs far more heavily. The narrator declines to reproduce every example, directing the reader back to the events already told. His image of [“cutters of shadows”](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c08) thus challenges both Voityuk's private grievance and Gamov's public self-accusation.

Afterward, Gamov praises the speech and says it has changed his understanding of their practice. Gonzalez also praises its force, but announces that he will oppose it the next day. Elena is delighted by her husband's performance. Semipalov remains cautious: neither Gonzalez nor Gamov has yet made his case, and the trial is still unresolved.

<a id="p6-c09"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 9 — Gonzalez asks for his own death

[Source: P6-C09](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c09). [Chapter facts and continuity](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c09).

Gonzalez formally calls himself as a prosecution witness, then abandons the absurdity of questioning himself and gives a confession. During the early encirclement, he and Albert Peano concluded that conventional war offered no escape. They recognized Gamov's inventive leadership and Semipalov's capacity to execute it. Peano persuaded Gonzalez to accept the first distribution of money for battlefield success. When government envoy Danilo Mordasov tried to recover that money, Gonzalez obeyed Gamov's order to kill him. It was his first use of a weapon against a person. At the time, he and Peano thought Gamov had bound him through shared bloodshed; he now sees a larger design.

The day after the seizure of power, Gamov asked Gonzalez to lead the Ministry of Terror precisely because he hated cruelty. That aversion should prevent indiscriminate punishment. Gamov also arranged a secret partnership with Pustovoyt. Executions would be real only when Pustovoyt could find no substantial objection. Where mercy objected but political necessity still demanded punishment, the sentence would be simulated. Pustovoyt created the technical group and hidden shelters that made [the false executions possible](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c09).

To bear his new office, Gonzalez privately resolved that each death sentence also condemned him. He initially believed his eventual death would balance the life he took. Repeated executions destroyed that consolation: one head could not balance hundreds. Now, combining defendant, prosecutor, and judge, he demands death for himself and promises to follow justice when sentencing. He leaves the room for about an hour. Semipalov fears that Gonzalez's new openness makes him more dangerous, since the same reasoning may condemn Gamov and him as well.

After the recess, Biber testifies for the defense. He argues that each preceding speaker has captured part of the truth while treating events too separately or too statically. History must be judged in motion. Gamov accelerated the growth of good, so that even accompanying evil became smaller relative to the result. Biber recalls Gamov thanking officers for educating soldiers capable of surpassing them. He points to planetary union, the practical prevention of interstate war, and international generosity replacing hatred as the decisive outcome. Semipalov is impressed by the concrete achievements gathered in the philosopher's account, although he withholds judgment on the philosophy itself.

<a id="p6-c10"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 10 — The army prepares to stop an execution

[Source: P6-C10](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c10). [Chapter facts and continuity](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c10).

During the free evening, Semipalov receives an unusual delegation: General Peano, Colonel Kaplin, and the soldier Serbin. Serbin has brought them. The guards and their army friends fear that Gamov's trial reflects a breakdown and will end in his death. Serbin reports that Gamov now paces and talks aloud, even wondering with apparent amusement how Gonzalez could execute a sentence against them with only his judicial officers. The soldier asks Semipalov to order Gonzalez's arrest.

Peano believes his old friend Gonzalez has long intended to pay for his crimes with his life, and will also condemn Gamov and Semipalov. His personal loyalty is strongest toward Semipalov, his actual military commander. He offers to mobilize the army and, if necessary, arrest Gamov under a declaration of illness. Kaplin says a palace seizure is already planned: his division can isolate the Black Court's guards within minutes. The troops, he says, will not accept the execution of the leaders who brought them victory.

Semipalov declines an immediate coup. The trial claims to restore justice, so force against it would be premature. He proposes a higher appeal: if Gonzalez imposes death, Semipalov will announce a worldwide referendum on the executions. The army will intervene only if the court tries to carry the sentence out before humanity can answer. He accepts a concrete plan for Kaplin's officers to disarm the guards and isolate Gonzalez, and Gamov if necessary, on his signal. Continuous stereo coverage must carry that signal. Peano reveals that Omar Isiro has already offered help.

This [contingent intervention plan](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c10) gives Semipalov his first sense of calm since the trial began. He also feels a new sympathy for Serbin, whose devotion had previously brought them into conflict. As he weighs the forces for and against him like a military campaign, the narrator warns that an unforeseen force will enter the struggle the next day, defeating the completeness of his calculations.

<a id="p6-c11"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 11 — The individual victim and the unknown origin

[Source: P6-C11](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c11). [Chapter facts and continuity](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c11).

Gamov rejects the defense's assumption that a beneficial historical result can settle the moral account. Its calculation views events from outside. General benefits are distributed across millions, while injury falls on particular people and can be absolute for them. He recalls a girl of about six to eight killed in Amentola's air attack after the defeats of Ferdinand Vaksel and Mart Tronshke. Stereo showed her dead with her arms raised toward the sky. For her, death destroyed the whole universe she could experience. No future paradise for survivors could compensate her.

Conscience, Gamov argues, registers precisely this suffering that abstract totals miss. It produces compassion and responsibility, distinguishing human beings from animals and thinking machines. He regards the girl as having been killed in a war directed at him: the attackers could not reach the leaders, so they struck civilians to reach the leaders' consciences. Their later punishment did not remove his responsibility. He demands his own execution. The distinction between [general progress and irreparable personal loss](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c11) gives the trial its deepest challenge to Semipalov's defense.

Instead of pronouncing sentence, Gonzalez calls the last defense witness, Tarkhun-khor, Konduk's high priest and claimed living embodiment of the prophet Mamun. The priest says he is unworthy either to defend or accuse Gamov: the court has no jurisdiction over him. He questions Gamov about his origin. Gamov knows neither parents nor birthplace and remembers nothing before observatory workers rescued him from the desert near Sorbas. His rescuers inferred that he survived a caravan buried in a sandstorm. Gamov treats this as plausible, not proved.

Tarkhun-khor reports that searches of trade records and instrument-assisted surveys around the observatory found no trace of such a caravan. He concludes that Gamov came from another world. When asked for firm proof, he admits that he has testimony instead, then sings three ancient songs. Mamun describes a beautiful homeland divided by many languages, a catastrophic transfer of a people into another universe, and the hope that a future leader will reunite the peoples who now share one language. Tarkhun-khor identifies Gamov with that promised leader. Gamov rejects prophecy as evidence, and Gonzalez refuses to accept this interpretation as legal proof.

Semipalov separates the priest's religious conclusion from the possible factual core. He remembers seeing another world through Berthold Shvurts's and Berthold Kozyura's apparatus, including a child destroyed in a nuclear attack. That sight caused unbearable shame, he says, without making him feel personally responsible for the death. He also realizes that Gamov has never been told about the physicists' work. After the hearing he orders Prishchepa to bring them. Their shared reason for withholding unfinished research can no longer stand. Peano remains ready to act on Semipalov's signal if the following day's expected sentence is death. Gamov's origin, and the relevance of the neighboring world, are now central unresolved questions.

<a id="p6-c12"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 12 — Sentence, intervention, and the desire to disappear

[Source: P6-C12](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c12). [Chapter facts and continuity](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c12).

Gonzalez sentences Gamov, Semipalov, and himself to death, declaring the judgment final and beyond appeal. When he calls the court guards to arrest them, Isiro turns every camera toward Semipalov. At his signal, Colonel Korney Kaplin's water-aircraft troops enter instead. The Black Court's soldiers have already been arrested and removed. Semipalov orders Gamov and Gonzalez into custody. Gonzalez throws several soldiers aside before Jean Vilta and the others restrain him; Gamov remains calmly curious.

Gamov observes that Semipalov is repeating the operation that removed Marutzian, this time against him. Semipalov replies that the first seizure gave Gamov power and this one is intended to preserve it. Before the cameras, he distinguishes suspension from cancellation of the sentence. He accepts that Gonzalez was their lawful judge and admits that he is temporarily taking all governmental authority. But the collective judgment of humanity, he argues, stands above a decision made by individual leaders. He announces preparations for a worldwide referendum on whether the sentence is just. He refuses both to sacrifice himself and to permit Gamov and Gonzalez to destroy themselves before that appeal.

The other Core members largely welcome the intervention, although Woodworth dislikes its departure from diplomatic forms. Prishchepa is absent because he has gone to fetch the physicists. Fagusta celebrates the operation, while Georgiu maintains that both his official advocacy and his prosecution fulfilled his duty. Tarkhun-khor still insists that Gamov lies beyond the court's authority; Semipalov tells him that he takes his origin claim more seriously than the other listeners do.

That night, Semipalov visits Gamov, who remains guarded in his own rooms. Both expect the referendum to reject the death sentence. Gamov admits that this prospect troubles him. He wants an impressive ending: to appear, win, and disappear, leaving humanity to confront the boundary between good and evil. Ordinary administration of a peaceful world would not make the same philosophical contribution. This admission adds [the desire for a dramatic conclusion](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c12) to the earlier arguments from conscience.

Semipalov promises a further discussion and warns the guards to keep Gamov safe. Semyon Serbin and Grigory Varella have already searched his clothes and rooms and removed a pocket impulsator. Semipalov fears that Gamov may take his own life; he holds Serbin personally responsible for preventing harm. The soldier kisses his hand in distress, and Semipalov leaves equally troubled.

<a id="p6-c13"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 13 — A living departure replaces martyrdom

[Source: P6-C13](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c13). [Chapter facts and continuity](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c13).

Two weeks pass before the referendum. Its result is an overwhelming rejection of the death sentence, although Semipalov gives no reliable numerical tally. Tarkhun-khor has meanwhile proclaimed Gamov a returned prophet, bringing religious authority among roughly a quarter of humanity to his support. Semipalov releases Gamov and reports that the Black Court has been abolished. Gonzalez must find a new occupation. Meetings with national leaders, army demobilization, and conversion of military factories are turning the settlement into practical reality.

Gamov nevertheless feels that Semipalov has spoiled his intended conclusion. He explains that he has become a symbol, not merely a person holding successive offices. His ambition developed during the war: first to overcome incompetent commanders, then to replace the government, then to defeat war itself. Conventional military methods could not expose war as a crime without weakening their own army. His two newspaper voices let him uphold the struggle while making its suffering visible, with one mind controlling the balance between praise and criticism.

His final purpose emerged when water allergy and the resulting drought brought hunger to Klur and Korina. Saving enemy children could still look strategically useful; sharing scarce food required harm to oneself for another's good. Gamov describes the conflict between military advantage and conscience, then his use of his own failing health, conveyed through Serbin, as a public test of moral solidarity. He believes the people's response made him a symbol of universal conscience. A judicial execution would have completed that symbol: his life would create good, his death condemn the evil accompanying it. He fears that ordinary presidential work will gradually reduce this moral image to an efficient administrator.

Semipalov offers an alternative to martyrdom. He explains the secret laboratory and shows photographs from the “other-world viewer.” He visited it again with Prishchepa the day before the referendum. The physicists believe Gamov is an emigrant from the neighboring world; Kozyura claims a similar origin, while Shvurts once failed in an attempted crossing. They now assure Semipalov that their improved transfer apparatus is safe. These remain [their origin and safety claims](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c13), distinct from the images he has actually seen.

Gamov accepts that the photographed world exists, but recognizes nothing. Childhood amnesia could explain the absence of memory, yet it does not prove where he came from. Semipalov proposes departure alive into that world as a more powerful ending than execution. He openly acknowledges his own ambition: to remain as the practical builder of Gamov's peaceful world state. Gamov accepts the bargain, shakes his hand, and orders preparation for the journey.

<a id="p6-c14"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 14 — Gonzalez changes the passenger

[Source: P6-C14](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c14). [Chapter facts and continuity](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c14).

The Core and guards accompany Gamov to the laboratory. Prishchepa wonders why they have accepted the departure so easily. Semipalov says they are still following Gamov's decisions, although he himself supplied this particular idea. Their conversation also exposes the personal cost of his work: Elena has complained that he has not returned home since the trial, and Pavel admits that he still thinks he would have been the better husband. Both had once courted her, but she chose Semipalov.

Shvurts and Kozyura welcome their first intended passenger. They claim that repeated calculations guarantee a safe crossing with a spread of only two microseconds. The room-sized cabin combines nuclear power with chronological transfer machinery. It also carries weapons, water-powered equipment, a one-person aircraft, and about two weeks of food against unforeseen circumstances. Semipalov suspects that almost everything in the destination will be unforeseen.

The farewell exposes doubts among Gamov's colleagues. Gonzalez calls the journey exile and asks whether Semipalov can truly replace Gamov. Semipalov answers that he can consolidate the path already opened, though he cannot create an equivalent new one. Pustovoyt wishes Gamov would stay. Bar, physically restored by peace and resuming his prewar Thursday gatherings, also sees useful work that needs no spectacular departure. He fears that their own significance depends on the man who is leaving. His verse about a heavenly visitor who inspires mortals and then flies away gives Semipalov an opening to urge the ceremony forward.

Before the cameras, Gamov publicly accepts that he came from another world, thanks fate for his role in this one, and expresses confidence in his successors. This declaration does not itself settle the earlier evidentiary doubts about his origin. He says farewell and approaches the cabin with Semipalov and the others close behind.

At the last moment, Gonzalez overturns [the planned departure](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c14). He reaches past Semipalov, seizes Gamov, and flings him backward. At the same time he drives Semipalov into the cabin with his chest, knocks Shvurts away from the controls, and pulls the starting lever. Semipalov falls inside and loses consciousness. The man sent away is the narrator, not the intended traveler; Gonzalez's motive remains unstated at the moment of the act.

<a id="p6-c15"></a>
### Part 6, Chapter 15 — The peace-maker faces another divided world

[Source: P6-C15](docs/knowledge/source-map.md#p6-c15). [Chapter facts, final resolutions, and open ending](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c15).

Semipalov wakes in a military medical room in the other world. Major Leon Segovia, a linguistics professor, becomes his interpreter. A computer identified his feverish words among records of about four thousand languages: the universal language of his homeland corresponds to a small, long-extinct language here. Through the window he sees a far brighter sun and buildings of roughly 180 to 200 floors. He learns that the cabin appeared abruptly on a football field. Forward Mike Dixon struck its metal wall instead of the ball and broke his leg; he can walk again but will not return to football. Military police, initially fearing an enemy bomb, found Semipalov unconscious inside.

The authorities want his origin, purpose, and unfamiliar weapons explained. He agrees to describe the events that brought him here before negotiating. Segovia supplies a recorder. Each morning Semipalov dictates the story beginning with his first meeting with Gamov, then corrects the transcript. This reveals [the narrative's place and purpose](docs/knowledge/parts/part-6-ledger.md#p6-c15): the book is his account for another world's rulers.

In isolation, he cannot determine why Gonzalez sent him away. Was it planned, impulsive, meant to save Gamov, or meant to remove him? No answer comes. He regrets subordinating Elena to public duty and imagines that Pavel's lifelong love may finally bring her happiness. This is hope, not knowledge of their fate. Rescue also seems remote. The first cabin took decades to build, and he recalls Kozyura saying that one year at home corresponds to about twenty here. He fears that help could arrive too late for his remaining life.

Segovia's account of an entire people vanishing after ancient disasters suggests an explanation for the shared language. Semipalov hypothesizes that a close encounter between the coupled universes transferred that people and their land, founding his own world's humanity. He hopes to investigate natural crossings and establish regular contact. The theory remains unproved.

He then discovers that newspapers falsely report him still unconscious. Segovia admits that the host state conceals his recovery to monopolize the energy-water, weather control, vehicles, and weapons described in his manuscript. Such knowledge could give it world dominance. Semipalov sees the danger: a peace-maker at home may become the cause of a new destructive war. He resolves to withhold military knowledge, even under imagined threats or torture, while still hoping for a negotiated exchange based on friendship. His projected suffering and refusal are internal preparations; no torture has occurred.

The government finally invites him to present his proposals. Four guards escort him from his 128th-floor confinement. In the meeting room he inspects the displayed cabin equipment and notices that its weather accumulator lacks the generator needed to control weather. Officials stand as he enters, then wait for him to speak. Surrounded by an unfamiliar government, he suddenly feels himself back in command of the Core. He opens the meeting and asks who wants the floor. The novel ends before any answer. His renewed confidence leaves both possibilities alive: another effort to make peace, and another struggle over power, methods, and responsibility.
