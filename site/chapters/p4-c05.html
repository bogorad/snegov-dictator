Part IV · Chapter 5

A storm opens the prison camps

Source · Chapter map

Shtupa’s weather offensive begins without the old restrictions on energy-water. Mobile generators move close to the fighting, rear stations intensify their work, and a vast cloud mass rolls west. Marshal Ferdinand Vaksel initially refuses to believe reports of clouds ten leagues thick. He assumes Gamov has spent his last reserves on a short display for the Fermor conference. When counter-cyclones fail, Vaksel conserves his remaining energy and orders troops, civilians, vehicles, and aircraft to stay under cover until the storm passes.

That apparently rational order leaves the sky open to Latania’s hidden air army. Its leading divisions cross above the clouds toward Roder and Klur. Landing forces attack barracks and headquarters before the enemy grasps the scale of the offensive. Gamov and Semipalov watch Pavel’s live feeds as scouts seize Vaksel. The marshal fires at the first attacker and repeatedly fights free, while his generals remain passive. Orders require his capture alive. His captors refuse to let him dress, intending to display him barefoot in his shirt. Vaksel denounces this humiliation and resists until they bind his legs and carry him out.

Semipalov admires his courage and questions the treatment. Gamov admires it too, but approves the humiliation. The narrator explains Gamov’s hostility to military honor: he regards shame as war-makers’ proper reward. Semipalov does not yet understand how fully Gamov will later impose that view on his own followers.

Roder’s air scouts detect the forces heading for Fermor where cloud cover is thinner. Roder and Klur launch aircraft, but Peano’s escorts clear the route while Kaplin’s division keeps flying toward its targets. More than 250 escort waterplanes overwhelm about forty Klur machines. Kordobin’s troops enter the five-story Svetoch hotel, which houses the conference delegates. Familiar rulers and diplomats emerge as terrified prisoners. Amentola is not yet found.

Semipalov insists that Pavel turn to the prisoner camps, the operation’s main objective. Freed soldiers are to be rearmed into a force that will strike Vaksel from behind while the existing army attacks from the front. The plan depends on this combined pressure, not on storms alone or on capturing particular leaders. In a Lamaria camp, guards fight briefly before surrendering. In a smaller Patina camp, they surrender without resistance and occupy the prisoners’ empty bunks. Starved men embrace and dance with their rescuers, collapsing as stronger soldiers support them. The joy reveals such suffering that Semipalov cannot bear to watch another camp.
