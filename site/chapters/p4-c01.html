Part IV · Chapter 1

A secret return to life

Source · Chapter map

Andrei Semipalov wakes from a terrifying state in which his whole body seems to break apart through vibration. Pavel Prishchepa and Nikolai Pustovoyt have watched over him for two days. His execution was a deception, but the apparatus could still have harmed him, and his recovery took longer than expected. Pavel reports that half of Floria has fallen and that local rebels fired on the retreating Latanian troops. Amentola is drawing Latania’s former allies toward his own conference. Yet this military and diplomatic danger also serves the hidden plan: Semipalov’s apparent death has strengthened the enemy’s confidence in its strategy.

Pustovoyt’s orders require Semipalov to remain in a guarded house until Gamov calls him. He can contact Pavel, but direct contact with the dictator would risk exposure. Grigory Varella guards him chiefly against being seen. His receiver also lets him hear Semipalov’s voice outdoors; only the screened rooms offer privacy. The concealment that keeps Semipalov alive also makes him a closely monitored prisoner of the plan.

Pustovoyt describes two scientists in Pavel’s nearby secret laboratory. One seeks the energy hidden in matter. The other seeks access to other worlds and claims that he belongs to one of them. Pavel values both, although Pustovoyt considers them mad. The conversation also forces Semipalov to confront his own public character: Pustovoyt says that ministers can argue with Gamov but dare only report to Semipalov, whose refusal is final.

Alone, Semipalov thinks about the unfinished waterplane program and Shtupa’s shrinking energy-water reserves. He hopes production can soon shift back to weather defense. He also fears that the southern states may attack before Gamov can strike Vaksel. His private thoughts return to Elena’s hatred during their last prison meeting. To escape political and emotional isolation, he asks Varella to invite the two scientists to his screened room.
