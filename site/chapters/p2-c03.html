Part II · Chapter 3

Rewards, Executions, and International Courts

The cyclone lasts more than a week, flooding western lands but failing to destroy the grain-growing steppes. Floods also stop the enemy advance. Pavel reports that the enemy has spent over two-thirds of its stored energy-water, giving Latania a respite until winter. Bar takes Semipalov to a new currency shop. Its abundant reserve goods contrast with the poor ration department. Workers can already buy with certificates for extra output, though some prefer to wait for gold coins. Government members receive none of the new currency. (Source map; weather and incentives.)

Semipalov then shows Bar the hanging body of police chief Anton Karmanyuk. He concealed serious corruption in his confession and later arranged two bandits' escape. Gonzalez ordered his death without a trial; Pustovoyt protested, but Semipalov upheld the sentence. Karmanyuk's parents and wife have been exiled and his three sons sent to military school. Privately, Semipalov remembers that one escapee was Karmanyuk's brother-in-law, whom he released after his wife's pleas. Semipalov struggled to sign and reduced Gonzalez's proposed drowning in excrement to hanging. Yet he knows he will hesitate less next time. Another recalled dispute shows Mercy's limited restraint: Pustovoyt defeats Gonzalez's demand to intern everyone ever imprisoned by demonstrating the prohibitive cost of guards. (Terror and its checks.)

At the palace, Gamov permits opposition leader Konstantin Fagusta to revive The Tribune, despite Fagusta's promise of relentless criticism. The government's Herald of Terror and Mercy will be edited by Pimen Georgiu, a former Marutzian loyalist and Fagusta's bitter enemy. Gamov then proposes international Black and White Courts, initially funded with ten billion lats divided equally. The Black Court will identify those who organize, finance, or glorify war, warn them, judge proven guilt, and pay agents to execute its sentences abroad. Pavel offers intelligence channels to deliver gold; Woodworth expects paid executioners to be available in Cortezia. The White Court will fund aid and redress. Gamov insists that mercy must prevail over terror, but Semipalov points to Karmanyuk as a contrary example. Gamov's further plan for equal enemy participation and funding meets Semipalov's disbelief. (Press and court proposals.)

In a restricted meeting, Pavel suspects foreign-ministry expert Jean Voityuk of spying through Torbash's ambassador, Shirbai Shar. Voityuk's wife wears a valuable necklace missing from Shar's family collection. Semipalov observes that a private sexual affair could explain the gift. Gamov therefore orders a test: feed Voityuk news of a northern offensive while the real attack is planned in the south. Semipalov objects that enemy preparations might become an offensive against Zabon; Gamov accepts the risk and orders Woodworth to plant the information. (Espionage evidence and military risk.)

Finally, Gamov asks to make Elena Bar's deputy, ostensibly to represent the women who form two-thirds of the home-front population. Semipalov recognizes a concealed purpose involving his jealousy. Gamov promises an explanation after Voityuk is exposed but requires Elena's appointment first. Semipalov agrees while suspecting that Gamov's real plan predates the spy case. (Elena's appointment and unresolved purpose.)
