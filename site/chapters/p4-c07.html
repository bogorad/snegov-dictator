Part IV · Chapter 7

Six strongholds close around the enemy

Source · Chapter map

The enemy recovers faster than expected under Mart Tronshke, a Roder corps general. He recognizes that the liberated prisoners are becoming a new army and turns his main forces against them, leaving a defensive front in Latania. Unlike Vaksel, he takes the threat seriously from the start. Peano openly condemns the failures of intelligence and command, including his own. Evacuating the prisoners would save them but abandon the whole strategy. Instead, he concentrates them in six towns, orders all-round defenses, and uses Latania’s air superiority to deliver troops, weapons, food, and portable weather generators.

Meanwhile, Pavel asks Semipalov to see Elena, who is happy that he lives and desperate to reach him. She regretted her prison outburst even before learning the truth. Pavel admits that her genuine grief and anger were planned parts of the deception. Semipalov still refuses to meet her. He recalls Pavel’s former attraction to Elena and their enduring friendship, but gives him the task of comforting her. Pavel tells him that he has changed. Semipalov’s joke about returning damaged from death does not ease the rebuke.

Bar shows that industrial output has risen despite the strain of producing the air fleet and weather reserves. The old stores are exhausted and luxury goods are scarce, but arms and supplies are ready. Semipalov delays the frontal offensive until Tronshke is deeply engaged against the six fortified towns. Tronshke takes the first town’s outer defenses and nearly wins. Repeated Latanian air landings retake lost positions and force his units to fight in several directions. This buys the time Peano needs.

The western front then breaks in two places. Territory gained by Vaksel over a year falls back in days. Tronshke abandons the sieges to rescue his front, but all six strongholds open at once. Rearmed prisoner divisions form corps and attack his returning troops from behind. Amentola sends Klur and Korina into the fighting. Their intervention cannot reverse the encirclement; Klur’s tired columns are driven back.

Gamov writes an ultimatum in Peano’s name. Surrender within twenty-four hours brings decent captivity. Continued fighting brings punitive camps, while resistance for empty military glory brings degrading execution without appeal to Mercy. Tronshke accepts the military facts but rejects the choice between rewarded dishonor and punished courage. He releases his generals to decide for themselves and kills himself with an impulsator. Peano orders a burial with music supplied by surrendered Roder soldiers.

Capitulation follows unevenly. Some commanders fight for another two weeks. Less than a month after the Fermor raid, Latanian troops control Patina, Lamaria, and Roder and stop at Klur’s frontier. Gamov refuses another invasion. With winter approaching, the leaders expect a pause until spring; the narrator warns that events will defeat that expectation.
