P4-C01 — Secret recovery after the staged execution

Catalog consulted: characters, places, world rules, and continuity. The central catalog currently covers early Part One; its absence of later facts is not a contradiction.

  • Observed recovery; reported duration: Semipalov wakes after the staged execution with a whole-body vibration experience. Pavel and Pustovoyt say his unconsciousness lasted longer than expected; Pustovoyt says they have stayed awake beside him for two days. The two days modify the vigil, not the entire period since arrest. Evidence: P0002–P0016.
  • Reported front and diplomacy: Pavel says half of Floria is lost; rebels shot retreating Latanian troops in the back. Amentola is convening allies and increasing aid to Latania’s former allies. Konduk’s example frightens those states. Evidence: P0011. Cross-part dependency: Floria, Konduk, and the prior deception need their earlier context.
  • Deception and purpose: The execution was camouflage. Semipalov explicitly recalls the joint ruse of naming Bernoulli and himself traitors to their respective countries. Pavel reports that Amentola accepts his own strategy more confidently. Semipalov infers that increased foreign aid shows the deception worked; its duration is uncertain. Evidence: P0030, P0057.
  • Characters and roles: Grigory Varella, a favored operative of Pavel, guards Semipalov against recognition. Pustovoyt is Minister of Mercy. Gamov remains dictator. Pustovoyt reports that Semipalov is less accessible and less open to argument than Gamov; this is his assessment, not an objective measure. Evidence: P0021–P0027, P0043–P0049.
  • Place and communication: Semipalov occupies a one-story house in a heavily guarded park. He must remain there until Gamov orders otherwise. He has direct access to Pavel but not Gamov, to preserve secrecy. Varella’s voice-tuned receiver hears outdoor speech; Semipalov’s study is screened and has call buttons. Evidence: P0021, P0028–P0036, P0054–P0056, P0061–P0075.
  • Reported research: Two scientists with the same first name work in Pavel’s secret nearby laboratory. One studies energy within matter; the other seeks routes to other worlds and claims an origin there. Their identities and the truth of the second claim remain unstated here. Pustovoyt’s diagnosis of madness is opinion. Evidence: P0037–P0051.
  • Strategic expectation: Semipalov expects completion of waterplane construction to free energy factories for Shtupa. He fears a southern attack before the offensive against Vaksel. These are expectations, not completed outcomes. Evidence: P0057.
  • Relationship continuity: Semipalov remembers Elena saying she hated him in prison. This chapter does not establish whether she knew the execution was staged. Evidence: P0058–P0060. Cross-part dependency: reconcile with the prison meeting.

Chapter review: The apparent execution and present survival are a deliberate deception, not a continuity error. The location of the park is not named. No new absolute date is supplied. No confirmed contradiction found within this chapter.

P4-C02 — Shvurts, Kozyura, and the viewed world

Catalog consulted: P4-C01; central characters, world, places, and continuity. Catalog search found no existing Shvurts, Kozyura, Sorbas, or nuclear-world entry.

  • People and patronage: Berthold Shvurts is the short, stout nuclear physicist; Berthold Kozyura is the tall, thin chronophysicist. Both are completely bald. They say Marshal Komlin founded the laboratory and Colonel Pavel Prishchepa funds it. Evidence: P0003–P0021, P0026, P0055. Their shared first name does not make them one person.
  • Energy-water explanation: Shvurts says ordinary water becomes energy-water through molecular compression and yields stored energy when released. He contrasts this with splitting heavy nuclei and joining light nuclei, the latter harder but more productive. Semipalov observes automated equipment; Shvurts says its output charges basement accumulators that supply both laboratories and Semipalov’s house. The mechanism is the scientist’s explanation; power supply is reported rather than independently measured. Evidence: P0022–P0025, P0061–P0065.
  • Kozyura’s origin claim: Kozyura says a nuclear-laboratory accident threw him into this world’s time line near Sorbas. He infers his parents had taken him to their laboratory; his memory was damaged. Baldness is attributed to passage between time streams. Shvurts says his own attempted departure failed and also cost his hair; a better apparatus is under construction. Evidence: P0032–P0052, P0073. This is an unresolved extraordinary explanation, not an established contradiction or fully witnessed journey.
  • Observed viewing process: A large screen initially shows blurred images; Kozyura says these combine a century’s events. Adjustments produce coherent aircraft, landscape, city, and people. He can change spatial position and time focus. Semipalov accepts that he is seeing another universe. Evidence: P0066–P0087. No journey occurs in this chapter.
  • Viewed technology and comparative world rules: The aircraft appears about ten times larger than Semipalov’s largest waterplanes. He counts ninety floors in one building and sees a neighboring building about ten floors higher; he says buildings in Adan, Zabon, and rich Cortezia do not exceed five floors. He observes many road vehicles and colored exhaust. Kozyura reports no condensed-water technology or artificial cyclones there, and combustion using wood, coal, and oil. Evidence: P0071–P0085. These figures describe the viewed city and Semipalov’s comparison, not population counts.
  • Viewed nuclear attack: After Kozyura changes time and place, Semipalov sees a city destroyed by a nuclear flash, a mushroom cloud, melting buildings, a vaporized child, and burned victims. No city, country, or calendar date is named. The other sun is orange-gold; Semipalov describes his own as pale greenish. The sun’s apparent blackness in the flash is explicitly his visual experience. Evidence: P0091–P0101.
  • Ethics and cosmology: The scientists distinguish beneficial nuclear applications from human misuse and compare them with energy-water’s military uses. Kozyura says his world is the original on the main stream of time and theirs a diminished copy on a side stream. This hierarchy is his claim; the chapter demonstrates images, not the whole theory. Evidence: P0102–P0113.

Chapter review: The unnamed scientists from P4-C01 now have identities. The promised ten-minute walk takes at least half an hour (P0054–P0057); the narration explicitly notes this discrepancy, so it is a character estimate, not an unacknowledged error. Nuclear images strongly evoke historical Earth, but this chapter does not name Earth or identify a particular bombing. Do not add that identification as fact. Ordered review also compares the five-floor houses in P0083 with P2-C07 P0011's ninth-floor headquarters laboratory: residential houses versus an institutional building may explain the scope, but this limit is not explicit. Central C013 retains the qualified tension rather than treating five floors as a universal physical cap.

P4-C03 — Moral comparison and the scientists’ altered bodies

Catalog consulted: P4-C01, P4-C02, central Gamov, Knurka IX, Torbash, and the world and continuity registers. Search found no central Klur or chronophysics entry.

  • Moral self-questioning: Semipalov compares the viewed nuclear massacre with Konduk, Gonzalez’s terror, and artificial rain that deprives civilians of food. He does not reach a defensible numerical threshold for permitted killing. Varella recalls Gamov condemning the murder of children after Sorbas. Evidence: P0002–P0010. Cross-part dependency: earlier Konduk and Sorbas events must be reconciled; the chapter supplies references, not a fresh description of them.
  • Title continuity: Soldiers call Gamov “the colonel” because that is how they first knew him. Semipalov and Peano are now generals. “Colonel” can therefore refer to Gamov in soldiers’ speech, not automatically Pavel. Evidence: P0009–P0011.
  • Kozyura’s biographical qualification: Kozyura cannot prove he came from another universe. He was found as an adolescent, nearly dead, without memory, identity, or the local language. He learned quickly, created chronotransformers while at university, and later recognized screened landscapes as childhood memories. He and Shvurts were student friends. Komlin supported their work for military advantage. Evidence: P0020–P0024.
  • Research and authority: Semipalov accepts the other world after a second viewing of peaceful life. Pavel has not yet informed Gamov; Semipalov does not prohibit the laboratory. Pavel proposes a secret reconnaissance expedition if a usable passage is found. These are acceptance and future intent, not completed travel. Evidence: P0021–P0027, P0045–P0049.
  • Timeline and front: Pavel arrives one week after Semipalov contacts him. Floria has fully fallen, and enemy forces are entering ethnic Latanian territory. The narration limits pro-Cortezian defectors to no more than one third of Floria’s people, emphasizing youth and advocates of change. Evidence: P0028–P0035. This updates the half-loss report in P4-C01.
  • Diplomacy: Former allies mass forces at the borders and await Amentola’s signal at the Klur conference in ten days. Torbash does not attend. Knurka IX invokes the Highest Judge to justify joining the eventual victor and bargains to pay Terror and Mercy company entry costs in dried fruit, cured mutton, and sheepskin coats instead of gold. Bar delays the negotiations. Evidence: P0036–P0042.
  • Concealment decision: Gamov’s message says the false treason has met all aims, but Pavel reserves judgment. Semipalov chooses to return after Amentola’s speech; Pavel says Gamov expected that answer. Evidence: P0032, P0042–P0044. The claims and decision do not independently prove every aim was met.
  • Medical and behavioral mystery: Pavel reports altered blood composition, abnormal vision and hearing, and weakened ordinary appetites in both scientists. Shvurts left his partner; neither seeks a family. Kozyura’s past remains unexplained; Shvurts’s past is traceable. Semipalov later regrets missing the meaning, but does not disclose it here. Evidence: P0050–P0054.

Chapter review: Kozyura’s P4-C02 accident account is qualified by recovered-memory inference rather than independent proof. His childhood/adolescent wording is clarified: he was found as an adolescent. The scientists’ changes are a deliberate unresolved clue. No confirmed contradiction found. The week and ten-day interval refer to different events and must not be combined into one stated date.

P4-C04 — Return and offensive orders

Catalog consulted: P4-C01, P4-C03, central Peano, weather generators, and Roder. Search found no central Fermor or Kaplin entries.

  • Geography and chronology: Fermor is Klur’s capital and hosts the allied conference. After Amentola’s speech, Semipalov orders transport and returns to the palace the next morning. Evidence: P0002–P0005.
  • Secret return and witnesses: The Core, ministers, and newspaper editors Pimen Georgiu and Konstantin Fagusta welcome him. Stereo staff are excluded because his return is not yet for broadcast. Most attendees learn only that day that he was not executed. Peano had advance warning but doubted the ruse during the execution; Semipalov did too. Evidence: P0005–P0012.
  • Office and marriage: Semipalov resumes the functions of war minister and deputy dictator, chairs the Core at Gamov’s invitation, and proceeds to headquarters in the palace wing. Elena still believes him dead. He refuses Pavel’s offer to notify her before the general announcement. Evidence: P0013–P0024. This resolves the P4-C01 uncertainty about her present knowledge: she has not been told he is alive.
  • Air forces: All waterplane divisions are at starting bases. Hundreds of new machines are concealed under forest cover, in artificial caves, and in hangars; the enemy’s aerial view reveals no infrastructure. Semipalov describes the fleet as his concept and main strategic stake. Evidence: P0023–P0026.
  • Enemy disposition: Vaksel’s Cortezian divisions lead, Patina’s forces support, and Roder’s troops guard the rear, ready either to complete victory or defeat a breakthrough. Peano assesses a Latanian counterattack as something Vaksel allows for but considers unlikely. Evidence: P0027–P0029. The assessment is not direct access to Vaksel’s thoughts.
  • Orders: Most air divisions will land behind Vaksel and seize Latanian prisoner camps. One division will capture the Klur conference; two supporting divisions will engage enemy aircraft. Colonel Korney Kaplin leads the capture force. His brigade commanders are Alfred Palman, Ivan Kordobin, Sergei Skripnik, and Jean Vilta, described as recent rebels. Evidence: P0031–P0035. Cross-part dependency: reconcile their earlier rebellion without inferring it from their current loyalty.
  • Weather and start: Shtupa promises cloud position and height, hurricane cover for routes, and prepared energy-water reserves. Semipalov orders the offensive for the next day. Evidence: P0036–P0040. These remain readiness claims and orders until execution is shown.

Chapter review: This chapter begins at its explicit internal anchor in the same HTML file as P4-C03; its events are distinct. The full capture plan is prospective, not an accomplished victory. No confirmed contradiction found.

P4-C05 — Weather attack, Vaksel’s capture, and prisoner liberation

Catalog consulted: P4-C04, central weather limits, weapons, war doctrine, and Roder.

  • Weather operation and mistaken forecast: Shtupa uses forward mobile generators, intensified rear stations, and stored mountain cloud masses thousands of leagues wide and about ten leagues high. Vaksel says three leagues was the prior practical limit and predicts the attack will last only hours. His counter-cyclones fail. He then orders energy conservation and universal sheltering, including grounding aircraft. Evidence: P0002–P0007. The three-league figure is his rejected prior expectation, not a conflicting narrator measurement.
  • Identity and evidence channels: The enemy commander is Marshal Ferdinand Vaksel. Semipalov distinguishes later-captured enemy recordings from live Latanian feeds and narrates both. Evidence: P0004–P0010. Do not treat every quoted headquarters exchange as something Semipalov heard at that instant.
  • Capture: Pavel’s scouts take Vaksel alive after a prolonged physical struggle. Vaksel uses an impulsator, disarms an attacker, and continues resisting after his arms are tied. His generals do not intervene. Captors refuse his request for uniform and footwear; he addresses Gamov and Peano through the cameras and condemns deliberate humiliation. Evidence: P0010–P0020. He still names Peano rather than the supposedly dead Semipalov.
  • Weapons and policy: A vibrator is less immediately lethal than an impulsator but may disable or paralyze at close range. The assault team is specifically ordered to take headquarters personnel alive. Gamov approves the humiliation despite admiring Vaksel’s courage; the narrator connects it to his hatred of war and soldiers. Evidence: P0010–P0011, P0021–P0024. Future conversion of Gamov’s aides to this doctrine is retrospective foreshadowing.
  • Fermor operation: Thinner clouds permit Roder reconnaissance to detect the force. Escorts engage opposing aircraft while Kaplin’s division proceeds directly to its landings. The two escort divisions have more than 250 waterplanes; Klur launches about forty. Ivan Kordobin enters the five-story Svetoch hotel, lodging for the delegates including Amentola. Many delegates are captured, but Amentola’s fate is unknown at chapter end. Evidence: P0025–P0032.
  • Strategic hierarchy: The central aim is to capture all prisoner camps in Patina, Lamaria, and Roder, free and rapidly rearm prisoners, then attack the enemy from front and rear. Capturing Vaksel and the conference are valuable but expendable additions. Semipalov explicitly says the plan allows for rational enemy action and does not depend on the shelter order. Evidence: P0035–P0037. This clarifies P4-C04 without reversing its plan.
  • Observed camp scenes: A large Lamaria camp has double wire, eight towers, and about twenty barracks; three Latanian waterplanes secure it. Some guards fight, one resisting officer is killed, and others surrender. In a smaller Patina camp, two waterplanes receive surrender without combat. Guards become prisoners in former prisoner barracks. Emaciated freed men dance, sometimes collapsing into rescuers’ arms. Evidence: P0040–P0047.

Chapter review: The initial statement that no enemy aircraft opposed the leading forces applies to the first overflight, not the later fighting over Roder and Klur. The later encounters are explicitly explained by thinner cloud cover. No numerical casualty total is given; Semipalov’s general claim of very low losses is not proof of zero casualties everywhere. No confirmed contradiction found.

P4-C06 — Camp justice and the end of the public deception

Catalog consulted: P4-C04, P4-C05, central Gonzalez, Pustovoyt, world currency and reward entries, and the continuity register. No prior central Khota, Samino, or Barvell entry was found.

  • Raid outcome and time: By the next day, commanders know successes are large and losses small; exact accounting takes longer. Amentola escaped before Latanian aircraft crossed into Roder and Klur. Dozens of rulers and hundreds of observers/journalists are captured, but some delegates left early, escaped, or never arrived. Peano evacuates captives rather than holding Fermor. Within a week, Latanian troops and aircraft leave Klur. Gamov prohibits destruction, including fortresses. Evidence: P0002.
  • Plan revision: On the second day Semipalov recognizes that emaciated prisoners cannot immediately fight. Peano redirects landings to enemy supply depots to feed them and deny supplies to enemy forces. Gonzalez sends Black Courts to the camps. Pustovoyt requests White judges with power to overturn injustice; Gamov agrees to their presence, without explicitly repeating the full veto demand. Evidence: P0003–P0012.
  • Trial setting and sources: A narrative flash-forward describes Omar Isiro’s broadcast from a Roder camp holding about 3,000 prisoners with more than 200 guards. Black and White judges sit with six former-prisoner assistants. Commandant Major Ishim Samino holds seized Latanian kalons, Cortezian didans, and Roder dons. The prosecution says guards stole food and sold two thirds of supplied medicines. Evidence: P0013–P0022. The trial is not necessarily on the second day.
  • Medical counts: For a six-month hospital period, 120 patients entered, 45 survived, and 75 died. Gabl Khota treated 48: 32 survived, 16 died. Pop Barvell treated 72: 13 survived, so 59 died by subtraction. Records show comparable illnesses, not Barvell’s claimed excess of severe cases. Barvell recorded unused medicine and blood as spent; Khota secretly supplied about two dinas of his own blood. Evidence: P0023–P0050. The counts add consistently.
  • Sentences: Samino is to be killed by forced ingestion of stolen money; deputy Purp Gorgon by his own whip; Barvell by overdose of the withheld medicines. Guards must execute the sentences under threat of death. The White judge does not mitigate these three sentences but frees Khota and grants a vast monetary award. Evidence: P0051–P0054. The narration gives sentences and compliance; it does not describe each execution taking place on screen.
  • Confirmed numerical discrepancy — blood reward: P0054 states two dinas = 2,000 koras, twenty drops per kora, one gold lat per drop, then a total award of 2,000,000 lats. The listed volume yields 40,000 drops and hence 40,000 lats. Cross-part source review of P2-C02 P0029–P0030 confirms that gold coins and convertible notes use the same lat; P0054 itself offers the award in either form. No denomination conversion resolves the factor of fifty. Preserve the narrated two-million award; the error’s cause is unknown. The donation is also called one third of Khota’s total blood volume, not necessarily a single withdrawal.
  • Terror policy: Gamov approves both the punishments and the extraordinary reward. He credits Gonzalez with inventing the particular executions; Semipalov sees ministers of Terror and Mercy alike as instruments of Gamov’s policy. Evidence: P0055–P0061.
  • Voityuk and Anna Kursai: Voityuk has escaped Pavel’s search. Semipalov proposes using his wife Anna Kursai as a hostage to force surrender. Gamov says she has not been arrested and will not be used for this minor target, but reserves the method for a greater purpose. Evidence: P0065–P0069. This is a proposed coercion, not an executed arrest.
  • Public disclosure: Gamov broadcasts that Senator Leonard Bernoulli was an active enemy, abducted and falsely branded an agent to discredit his analysis. Semipalov proposed the matching false treason and staged hanging because some still doubted Bernoulli’s guilt. Gamov publicly restores Semipalov and credits his air fleet. Semipalov is too moved to appear for about five minutes, then speaks briefly. Evidence: P0062–P0064, P0070–P0075. Cross-part dependency: compare earlier secret actions and public accusations, preserving the distinction.

Chapter review: Amentola’s absence from P4-C05 is resolved as escape, not capture. The prisoner-army schedule changes because their physical condition is worse than planned. The reward has a confirmed numerical discrepancy; all hospital subtotals agree. The broadcast resolves the public false-treason narrative but remains Gamov’s own account of the plan’s authorship and success.

P4-C07 — Tronshke’s counterstroke and defeat

Catalog consulted: P4-C05, P4-C06, central Elena, Pavel, Bar, Nordag, and timeline/continuity entries. No central Tronshke entry exists at this point.

  • Enemy succession: Mart Tronshke, a Roder corps general, commands the combined Cortezian, Roder, Lamaria, and Patina forces after Vaksel. He attacks the liberated prisoners in his own rear before they can form a field army, leaving defensive forces at the front. Evidence: P0002–P0003. His fear is presented as prudent recognition of danger, not cowardice.
  • Six defensive centers: Peano acknowledges failures by intelligence, staff, and command. He concentrates former prisoners in six towns, creates circular defenses, and uses absolute air superiority to send weapons, reinforcements, food, and portable weather generators. Pustovoyt’s proposed evacuation is rejected because it would abandon the rear-army strategy. Evidence: P0004–P0017, P0035–P0039.
  • Elena, Pavel, and Semipalov: Elena now knows the truth and seeks a meeting. Pavel says she regretted her prison words after the apparent execution; he withheld the truth because her grief and indignation were calculated parts of the plan. Semipalov refuses to meet and asks Pavel to comfort her. He recalls that Pavel once seemed in love with Elena, who chose him instead. Evidence: P0018–P0034. This attraction is Semipalov’s perception, not Pavel’s confession.
  • Marriage duration: Semipalov says Elena has been his wife for nearly twelve years (P0024). The central P1-C01 entry states they first met twelve years earlier, with no marriage length given there. Keep both claims for ordered chronological review; rounding and unknown interval prevent an immediate contradiction verdict.
  • Industry and timing: Bar, Minister of Organization, learned the Voityuk ruse from Gamov’s public speech. He has maintained and increased war production despite exhausted Marutzian-era reserves and declining luxury stock. Semipalov proposes an offensive in a week, waiting until Tronshke is engaged in the rear rather than attacking the next day as Bar suggests. Evidence: P0041–P0049.
  • Battle sequence: Tronshke takes the first town’s outer defenses but is slowed by street fighting and Latanian air landings that retake positions behind him. Latania then breaches the main front in two places on the first day. Tronshke lifts the sieges; all six centers release divisions, which combine into corps and attack his forces from behind. Amentola reappears and brings Klur and Korina into the fight, but their aid fails to prevent encirclement. Evidence: P0050–P0052.
  • Ultimatum: Gamov writes the text issued under Peano’s name. It asserts three quarters of enemy ammunition spent and nearly equal numbers of fit and sick/wounded troops. It offers good camp conditions for surrender within twenty-four hours, worse-than-enemy punitive camps for later captures, and degrading execution without Mercy appeal for obstinate military-glory resistance. Evidence: P0053–P0061. Tronshke agrees the military description is accurate in the acquired recording (P0063).
  • Death and surrender: Tronshke frees his generals to choose, writes a will, then kills himself with a hand impulsator. Peano orders burial with music played by surrendered Roder soldiers. No other general follows him in suicide; some resist for two more weeks. Evidence: P0062–P0065.
  • Territorial result: Klur’s troops halt for about a week in Roder and Lamaria, then retreat. Less than one month after the Fermor raid, Latania reaches the Roder–Klur border. Gamov declines to invade Klur. Patina, Lamaria, and Roder are conquered; Klur remains to the west, Korina and Nordag to the north. Winter approaches; a pause until spring is planned, not guaranteed. Evidence: P0066–P0068.

Chapter review: The six towns are not named here. The force that attacked the enemy rear becomes the force that closes the encirclement; no unexplained reversal of sides occurs. The one-week preparation, two-week residual fighting, and less-than-one-month overall claim are approximate or nested intervals, not independently additive dates. The two twelve-year relationship statements are compatible with rounding and a different start event; preserve both without declaring an error. No confirmed contradiction found.

P4-C08 — Returning prisoners and the unresolved marriage

Catalog consulted: P4-C06, P4-C07, central Elena, Pavel, weather generators, and the world/continuity registers.

  • Season and environment: Autumn becomes exceptionally calm when both sides halt meteorological attacks to rebuild reserves. Semipalov says even natural storms cease and interprets this as nature’s exhaustion, an outcome meteorologists had not predicted. The harvests are already practically destroyed in Latania and the neighboring combatant states. Evidence: P0002. Calm weather is narrated; “exhaustion” is the narrator’s explanation, not a tested mechanism.
  • Prisoner movement: Freed Latanian prisoners receive special uniforms, one hundred gold lats each, and thirty days’ military leave; routes are assigned before departure. Captured Cortezians, Roders, Lamarians, and Patinians travel to prebuilt rear camps. Bar controls transport; Mercy staff take over at the camps. Evidence: P0003–P0004. The narration withholds the policy’s larger purpose.
  • Meeting and knowledge: Pavel brings Elena after the front eases, invoking Semipalov’s promise. Elena knows officials learned of his survival before she did. Semipalov calls her his former wife, but no divorce procedure or legal termination is stated; he explains this through his sense of having died. Evidence: P0005–P0038.
  • Private feeling versus conduct: Semipalov admits he still loves Elena, even more intensely, despite his earlier emotional withdrawal and planned separation. He cannot say he no longer loves her. He fears that yielding would be weakness the changed man he has become would later resent. Evidence: P0039–P0041, P0059–P0068. This is explicit internal conflict, not inconsistent narration requiring correction.
  • Elena’s explanation: She accepts that the words of hatred were spoken but says they addressed the convincing traitor-mask. She argues that his successful deception required her belief and that he unfairly blames her for that belief. She reports grief, self-hatred, thoughts of suicide, and later ecstatic relief. She refuses to recant hatred of the supposed traitor. Evidence: P0042–P0065. Her initial “I did not say it” is deliberately clarified, not denial of the physical utterance.
  • Gamov and fidelity: Elena says she considered revenge through an affair with Gamov, but he did not want her and treated her as a political piece. She admits enjoying Semipalov’s jealousy. She says she would not have wanted other men even for revenge and could remain alone but not choose to leave him. Evidence: P0069–P0080. No affair is reported as having occurred.

P4-C11 — The reprisal carried out

Catalog consulted: P4-C04, P4-C05, P4-C10, central character/place/world entries for Cortezia and the air forces. The event-specific names and rules are retained in this ledger.

  • Capture and sentencing: Peano attacks Cortezia’s island air bases; no enemy pilot escapes according to the narrator. Klur aircraft attempt to intercept the returning force. Isiro repeatedly shows about three hundred captured commanders, pilots, and mechanics. Gonzalez sentences the raiders to death over their home cities at announced times. Evidence: P0002–P0005. “About three hundred publicized” is not automatically the number later flown.
  • Mission and hostage meeting: Kaplin’s division is assigned the execution raid. Alfred Palman, Ivan Kordobin, Sergei Skripnik, and Jean Vilta are decorated brigade commanders. Nearly two hundred captured conference participants attend the palace briefing; Gonzalez threatens possible future death sentences, while Pustovoyt explains the broadcast appeals and conditional rescue. Evidence: P0007–P0017. Their threatened guilt is Gonzalez’s assertion, not a completed individual trial.
  • Name form: Russian accusative “Zhana Viltu” at P0007 and instrumental form at P0010 establish the surname Vilta, not the earlier provisional English Vilt. This is transliteration correction, not a story alias.
  • Reasoning: Semipalov argues that shooting down the convoy might serve rational long-term political aims despite higher immediate deaths. Gamov says Amentola has no time to persuade the public to accept that cost. Cortezian opinion demands rescue and divides over whether the original raids were criminal. Evidence: P0018–P0021. Enemy noninterception follows at P0033; motives remain inferred.
  • Prisoner loading and count limits: Ten transport/execution aircraft carry about fifty hostages in each upper cabin and twenty condemned men in each lower cabin (P0023–P0024): approximately five hundred hostages and two hundred condemned by multiplication. This exceeds the nearly two hundred at the palace briefing and is fewer than the approximately three hundred publicized earlier. Additional hostage selection or a condemned subset could explain those differences; neither is expressly described. Preserve each stage’s count without equating the groups.

The same paragraph later gives twenty crossing onto the ocean: ten with prisoners plus ten escorts. P0044 returns twelve armed aircraft plus ten transports, totaling twenty-two. No losses, detachments, or reinforcements explain the changes. This is an unresolved numerical inconsistency in convoy description; do not silently choose a total.

  • Execution outcome: Condemned men are dropped; the narrator says more than one quarter die immediately and more than half of survivors become permanent asylum inmates. These are proportions, with no reliable total casualty count given. Evidence: P0042.
  • Release and interpretation: Ten hostage aircraft land in Klur on return; hostages are released to cheering crowds. Armed escorts pass over. The journalist is carried on a wooden shield. Crowds also wave farewell to departing Latanian crews. Gamov calls this a turning point in minds; Semipalov doubts that conclusion. Evidence: P0043–P0049.

Chapter review: The source distinguishes the first base-capture operation, opposed by Klur, from the later execution flight, not opposed there. Convoy totals are inconsistent; prisoner totals may describe different subsets. The capital spelling is a source variant, not a second city. The reprisal and hostage release are now accomplished events; Gamov’s broader psychological victory remains his interpretation.

Cross-part scientific continuity

The first laboratory visits in P4-C02 and P4-C03 do not state a relative rate of time between worlds. P4-C02 P0070 explains temporal image focus, from a century of superimposed images down to shorter intervals; it does not equate one year here to twenty years elsewhere. No later chapter in Part Four supplies that ratio. A later attribution of such a statement to the first visit therefore needs to be recorded as retrospective added information or an attribution gap, not silently inserted into these scenes. Likewise, no Part Four passage connects Gamov’s original next-day war prediction to Kozyura’s research. P4-C03 P0025–P0027 instead says Gamov had not yet been informed of the laboratory.
