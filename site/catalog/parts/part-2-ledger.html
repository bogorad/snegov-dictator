P2-C01 — Program of the new government

Source: P2-C01, paragraphs P0001–P0033, in part0011.html. The source part title translates as Sacred Terror.

  • People and offices: Alexei Gamov leads the six-person governing nucleus and chooses the title Dictator (P0002–P0005, P0032–P0033). Andrei Semipalov presses for a philosophy and consents first to degrading punishments (P0003, P0028–P0030). Albert Peano calls Marutzian his uncle (P0014). John Woodworth helps design financial and military incentives (P0018–P0022). Marutzian is under arrest and called his captors “six usurpers” (P0003). Marshal Komlin is credited with stockpiling weapons, despite failed military leadership (P0010). Semyon Serbin is the previously punished mutineer (P0028); the earlier incident remains a Part 1 dependency.
  • Time and place: The announcement of the government change came one week before the meeting. Cortezian and Roderan weather attacks have continued since then. Adan floods; on the meeting day, military meteorologists push the rain 100 leagues from the capital, leaving a dry electrical storm (P0007). The chapter does not supply a calendar date.
  • Military and economic proposals: Use reserves sufficient for three years of war in an immediate counterattack; increase military production by roughly 50 percent; release premium consumer goods; create gold pay for production above quota and military exploits (P0009–P0023). Gamov reports national gold reserves of 5,000 chuds (the source's unit; no modern conversion supplied), formerly spent on allies (P0017). Woodworth's predicted hoarding is accepted as compatible with production incentives (P0019–P0020).
  • External and internal policy: Abandon all alliances, hoping Cortezia will expend resources on the abandoned allies (P0024–P0027). Degrade criminals publicly rather than rely on prison and execution (P0028–P0030). Retain experienced administrators through confidential “Repentance Sheets”: amnesty for confessed offenses, full punishment and publicity for concealed ones (P0031). These are agreed proposals, not yet proof of implementation.
  • Narrative qualification and continuity: Gamov announces seven points, but the narration presents a connected program without a complete numbered list; do not invent a numbering scheme (P0009–P0031). Semipalov says the anti-crime method itself looks criminal, yet agrees (P0029–P0030): an ethical conflict, not a textual contradiction. Gold rewards behind Roderan lines and Serbin's cesspit punishment refer back to Part 1 (P0021, P0028). The central catalog's early civilian and military offices require later-state updates, not replacement of earlier facts. Review: all 33 paragraphs read; proposals distinguished from outcomes; no confirmed source contradiction identified in this chapter.

P2-C02 — Public program and the ten-person Core

Source: P2-C02, P0001–P0066, part0012.html.

  • People and relationships: Semipalov and Elena temporarily occupy an Adan hotel; their Zabon home is locked. Elena visits a hospital testing her factory's new medicine (P0002). She doubts Gamov's grasp of public life and later Semipalov's ability to remain a faithful subordinate (P0012–P0015, P0057–P0062). Gamov requires surnames and formal address among colleagues; Semipalov retains “Pavel” to distinguish son from father (P0019). Semipalov describes Elena as unable to lie; this is his personal characterization (P0060).
  • Government: The Core consists of ten unelected, irremovable members: Alexei Gamov, Dictator; Andrei Semipalov, deputy dictator and war minister; Gottlieb Bar, organization; John Woodworth, foreign relations; Albert Peano, army commander-in-chief; Kazimir Shtupa, weather; Pavel Prishchepa, state protection; Arkady Gonzalez, Terror; Nikolai Pustovoyt, Mercy; Omar Isiro, information. A second layer of elected, removable, accountable ordinary ministers is planned (P0044–P0055). This updates rather than contradicts their earlier jobs.
  • Speech, currency, and production: The broadcast lasts two hours (P0021). Gamov attributes defeat and threatened harvests to equipment and condensed-water shortages, and poor output to material incentives and public apathy (P0023–P0029). Quotas are frozen until the war ends. Normal output earns kalons; excess output earns lats, gold or convertible notes. Coins are 5, 10, and 20 lats; one lat has the value of one kor of gold. Reserve goods enter special shops; army reserves are released at once (P0029–P0030). A commission will set rewards for military achievements, extending the Steel Ram and Golden Wings practice to all troops (P0034). Clear skies through late autumn are a conditional meteorological promise if reserve energy-water is supplied (P0028).
  • Terror orders: Shoot imprisoned repeated murderers and publish names and guilt; send other inmates to northern hard labor or penal battalions; abolish prisons (P0037). Gamov estimates about 200,000 bandits at large. Voluntary surrender offers a route to pardon. Otherwise leaders face public drowning in excrement; parents witness executions, lose property, and endure forced labor until war ends. Proven beneficiaries of robbery also lose property and face labor. Police who continue shielding bandits are to be hanged at their stations; their property is seized and families expelled (P0038). Earlier confessed police offenses receive amnesty. Gamov explicitly calls his method barbarism and crime, justified by deterrence; he promises safe streets this winter (P0041–P0042).
  • Time, narration, and continuity: Streets are initially skeptical; Semipalov's images have not yet been widely broadcast (P0004–P0011). The evening ends with renewed western weather attack (P0063–P0066). Semipalov's feeling that enemies paused to hear the speech is an impression, not confirmed strategy (P0020). The mass emotional response and “devilish magic” are retrospective narrator judgments (P0021–P0022, P0039–P0043). Public terror now exceeds P2-C01's initial discussion: execution and family penalties are policy development, not an authorial inconsistency. Review: all 66 paragraphs read; orders, conditional promises, and narrator interpretations separated. Cross-part check needed for the earlier captured money trucks and the formation of the Core.

P2-C03 — Institutions and the Voityuk test

Source: P2-C03, P0001–P0113, part0013.html.

  • Weather and sequence: The cyclone continues more than one week; roughly ten cubic leagues of water flood the west. The grain steppes are protected. Floods impede the enemy more than Latania's defenders. Pavel reports enemy expenditure of over two-thirds of its accumulated condensed water, grounding the expectation of no new major weather offensive before winter (P0002). This is a report and forecast, not independently measured data.
  • Economy and Bar: Bar oversees industry, trade, finance, and 24 ministries. Gold coins and notes are still being produced. Defense workers use above-quota certificates at the first premium shop; one worker has 40 lats of credit and wants to save gold. Cabinet members receive no new currency (P0009–P0018). Consumption and hoarding coexist, as discussed in P2-C01.
  • Anton Karmanyuk: District police chief, lieutenant colonel, decorated public figure, approximately 45, father of three boys. His posted offenses include bribes, incomplete confession, and a paid escape for two bandits after reconfirmation in office. One escapee is his wife's brother; he yielded to her pleas after having arrested him himself. Gonzalez signs the sentence; Pustovoyt protests; Semipalov approves hanging in place of the proposed filthy drowning. Parents and wife go north, property is confiscated, children enter military school (P0021–P0032). No trial or appeal occurs; Mercy's protest does not prevail. Semipalov's hesitation and his anticipated future hardness are explicit self-observation.
  • Mercy and Terror: Pustovoyt previously defeated Gonzalez's proposal to intern all former prisoners by calculating that guards would require about ten frontline divisions, or closure of about twenty factories, or abandonment of effective anti-bandit work (P0036). These are alternative estimated costs, not actual deployments or closures.
  • Press and people: Pimen Georgiu, a former Maximalist close to Marutzian, changes sides early and is designated editor of the government Herald of Terror and Mercy (P0034). Konstantin Fagusta, leader of the Optimates, receives permission to revive the formerly banned Tribune and promises fierce criticism (P0035–P0047). They are bitter enemies. Semipalov foreshadows Fagusta's decisive role in Gamov's eventual fate without revealing its result (P0038). The palace's smaller semicircular council room adjoins Gamov's living rooms and the private “confessional” (P0033, P0037).
  • International courts: Proposed Black and White Court joint-stock companies receive 10 billion lats, equivalent in Bar's answer to 1,000 chuds of gold, divided equally (P0048–P0050, P0061–P0062). Foreign reserves include Cortezian didans, Lepin yulans, and Konduk dons; these country spellings require later nominative confirmation where only inflected forms occur (P0050). The Black Court is to warn holders of potentially war-making power, investigate actual guilt, and fund punishment abroad; the White Court funds relief and redress. Gamov says Mercy must prevail. Equal enemy funding and jurisdiction over Latanian offenders are intended, not accepted international arrangements (P0052–P0070). Woodworth's account of Cortezian money values is his characterization. Pavel offers overseas agents and clandestine gold delivery (P0058–P0060).
  • Spy evidence and plan: Jean Voityuk, expert on southern neighbors in Woodworth's ministry, confesses corruption and displacing his predecessor but omits ties to Shirbai Shar, ambassador of Torbash's Knurka IX. Pavel identifies Shar as a paid Cortezian informant. A genuine emerald necklace worn by Voityuk's unnamed wife matches a Shar heirloom now absent from that family's collection, without a theft report (P0073–P0087). These facts support suspicion; Semipalov proposes an intimate rather than political explanation (P0091). Gamov orders Woodworth to leak a false northern offensive to test transmission, while Peano's real southern Western Front offensive aims at lost areas of Lamaria and Patina. Semipalov warns of an enemy counterstroke against Zabon; Pavel promises early warning (P0089–P0100).
  • Elena and unresolved purpose: Gamov requests Elena as Bar's deputy, citing women as two-thirds of the home-front population. He acknowledges Semipalov's jealousy and promises disclosure after Voityuk's exposure, but wants Elena present first. Semipalov agrees she will attend the next day and infers a pre-existing concealed plan (P0101–P0113). Preserve this as unresolved explanation, not proof of an affair.
  • Continuity review: The ideal that Mercy outranks Terror is immediately challenged by Karmanyuk's execution; the text itself recognizes the gap (P0062–P0065). Possible attribution slip: P0083 describes contempt in Peano's smile, while P0088 says Prishchepa's eyes now show surprise “rather than contempt”; both names are present in the source, but the shift can also be independent observation. Retain as a possible editorial issue, not a confirmed contradiction. “Minister of intelligence” in P0059 functionally describes Pavel, whose formal title in P2-C02 is state protection. Review: all 113 paragraphs read; evidence, hypotheses, plans, and future foreshadowing separated. Earlier train-coup discussion is a Part 1 dependency (P0057).

P2-C04 — Court notices and Fagusta's indictment

Source: P2-C04, P0001–P0051, part0014.html.

  • Publication and institutions: First issues of the government Herald of Terror and Mercy and opposition Tribune are printed at the same press, with Gamov's approval (P0002–P0007). “Herald of Terror” and “Herald” are shortened references to the full title, which recurs in P0016; no evidence of a separate newspaper. Latania publicly assigns 5 billion lats in gold to each court and invites all countries to subscribe (P0008). Gonzalez chairs the Black Court company; Pustovoyt signs the White Court appeal (P0009, P0016).
  • Targets and judgments: A first summons list contains 100 names, headed by Amin Amentola and ending with Eduard Konveizer, described as the world's richest banker and a military-corporation chief. Court hearings are in Adan; only death excuses absence (P0011). Warnings name about ten people, including four journalists and two bishops. 84 people receive death sentences in absentia: 38 airmen, approximately ten punitive officers, three priests, and a camp commandant and soldiers who shot prisoners (P0013–P0014). Do not force the approximate component counts into an exact breakdown. Semipalov infers Pavel's investigative help; the chapter does not directly confirm it.
  • Rewards and appeals: Minimum bounty 100,000 lats, compared with 100 years of an average worker's earnings; 300,000 for an airman; 500,000 for the camp commandant. Payment may be gold, lats, or didans. Only execution of a Black Court sentence is sanctioned; other killing is called banditry (P0015). The White Court accepts all appeals and can suspend execution briefly; its advocates examine every sentence. A citizen of a subscribing state can nominate a personal advocate (P0016).
  • Fagusta's position: He initially welcomed Marutzian's fall and Gamov's military ability, but condemns dictatorship above law and profit from killing, wounds, or a soldier's death. He questions trial without hearing the accused, practical access through enemy battle lines, the recruitment of criminals as executioners, and danger to bystanders (P0017–P0021). This is an argument, not a neutral authorial verdict. His tolerance of the gang leader's degrading execution is explicit and limits any claim that he rejects all Terror (P0022).
  • Unnamed gang and collective punishment: Fagusta reports an Adan-outskirts gang leader who worked by day loading groceries and murdered a woman, her husband, and two children. His filmed drowning in excrement is followed by deportation of both elderly parents. His young girlfriend, who had known him one week, says she only wanted to dance and eat, took no money, and knew nothing of his criminal activity; she too is forced to watch and sent north (P0022). Semipalov subsequently concedes that Fagusta uses real facts (P0040), but the girl's knowledge remains her reported claim.
  • Gamov's rationale and unresolved clue: Both Semipalov and Gonzalez demand Fagusta's arrest; Gamov rejects it. He says publicity creates deterrence and that Fagusta's eloquence serves this purpose. Gamov asserts a sharp drop in robbery and murder; Semipalov notes the lack of voluntary bandit surrenders (P0036–P0046). Gamov denies precisely that Fagusta showed him this article before sending it to print. The narrator says the deliberately repeated wording matters, but supplies no explanation yet (P0048–P0051). Classification: deliberate withheld explanation. Review: all 51 paragraphs read; public claims and critical interpretations distinguished. The Mercy/Terror conflict continues; no new confirmed continuity error.

P2-C05 — Allied conference preparations and the northern danger

Source: P2-C05, P0001–P0091, part0015.html.

  • Visitors and geography: King Knurka IX of Torbash arrives first with Shirbai Shar, described here as ambassador for special missions. Visitors occupy Adan's luxurious Podnebesnaya hotel (literally “Under Heaven”; P0005–P0011). Lon Chudin is president of Great Lepin; Kir Kirun is his brother. Woodworth's conspicuous friendliness to Kirun deliberately provokes Chudin after a discourteous greeting (P0012–P0028). Great Lepin, Sobrana, Torbash, and Nordag are the allies Woodworth fears losing (P0051). Korina is currently neutral, across a narrow strait from northern Roder (P0077). The source's explicit nominative confirms Roder, not the earlier inherited “Rodera.”
  • Technology and offices: Knurka's waterplane carries 15 passengers and two pilots; cold vapor streams provide propulsion and downward braking during landing (P0006). Army formations formally lose the “volunteer” label as reserve equipment reaches them (P0029). Elena makes her public debut as deputy minister and the government's only woman at the dinner and ball (P0030). Omar Isiro is described as quiet, modest, competent, and unobtrusive; Semipalov does not know why Gamov chose him for the Core (P0071–P0073).
  • Industrial results: Bar reports voluntary overtime, hoarded gold coins, circulating convertible notes, rapid reserve-goods sales, and industrial output nearing 30 percent above baseline (P0036). The paragraph recalls a Gamov promise of 20 percent. P2-C01 P0012 proposed a 50-percent rise in military production: scopes differ, so this is a target-comparison question, not a confirmed numerical contradiction. Energy-water remains short. Shtupa calls for double supply, fearing autumn rains and winter snow; Gamov prioritizes new factories and increased wages paid wholly in new currency (P0037–P0039). This autumn concern is less confident than P2-C03 P0002's no-attack-before-winter forecast; preserve the changed assessment.
  • Allied demands and strategy: Allies request weapons, food, didans or lats; Kirun rejects his own country's yulans, which it can print. Knurka also requests Latanian soldiers; Great Lepin asks for 2,000 premium wool carpets (P0041–P0043). Gamov plans to withhold unearned aid, encourage defection to Cortezia, and drain Cortezian resources. New declarations of war will trigger Black Court death sentences and large bounties against leaders and supporters. Gamov expects personal fear to divert their military resources into self-protection and explicitly accepts criminal executioners (P0047–P0069). Woodworth objects; Semipalov supports; Pustovoyt visibly suffers. This is a plan, not yet demonstrated international success.
  • Voityuk test results: Woodworth planted the coastal offensive story one week earlier. Voityuk has since met a food seller and Shar, in company, with no observed private conversation. Ferdinand Vaksel, four-star general and deputy to supreme commander Amin Amentola, arrives on the Western Front, meets commanders, and the enemy strengthens its north (P0074–P0084). Pavel calls indirect evidence conclusive; direct transmission remains unproved. Semipalov warns that defenses designed for three or four divisions cannot safely meet several corps, threatening Zabon, the country's second center (P0085–P0091).
  • Continuity concerns: Pavel says Semipalov agreed to the deception (P0087), whereas P2-C03 P0096–P0099 records explicit opposition and Gamov's override. Classification: conflicting character assertion versus narrated prior objection; no unrecorded later assent is established. The intended real offensive was on the southern sector in P2-C03 P0094, but is called an offensive in the center here (P0089–P0090). This may be revised operational framing; the text does not explain it. Mark as a location/plan continuity question, not a proven contradiction. Shar's glance at Semipalov's departure is retrospective foreshadowing (P0011). Review: all 91 paragraphs read; military risks remain predicted within this chapter despite the narrator's later judgment of error.

P2-C06 — The alliance break and the false love triangle

Source: P2-C06, P0001–P0069, part0016.html.

  • Conference: Gamov announces suspension of all material, weapons, and financial aid, explaining Latania's crisis and leaving possible later aid conditional on military recovery (P0002–P0007). Mgobo Mardoba, president of Sobrana, is about 40; his apparent silent agreement can conceal strong disagreement, according to Woodworth (P0008). Knurka IX raises disputed border territories and admits Torbash has neither fought Cortezia nor assembled a sufficient army. His plan was to declare war, induce a landing, then attack with Latanian assistance (P0009–P0015). This is his reported intended strategy, not a military event.
  • Northern war: Enemy forces see no serious Latanian northern concentration and attack Zabon (P0017). Semipalov proposes a deliberate withdrawal: preserve central reserves, draw further enemy forces north, break the weakened center, and encircle the Zabon force from the west. Defending the city would consume the reserves and jeopardize recovery of western lands, punishment of Patina, conquest of Lamaria, and driving Roder's armies behind their natural borders (P0020–P0023). Gamov rejects the planned sacrifice, orders defense of Zabon, and accelerates the central offensive (P0024–P0034). Woodworth warns of Nordag's possible defection if enemy corps reach its border; no actual defection yet occurs. Semipalov plans inspection with Peano and Pavel the next day (P0035–P0038).
  • Espionage plan and relationships: Gamov interprets the enemy's rapid response as proof that Voityuk is a trusted source. He speculates that Woodworth overdid the planted information; that blame is not independently established (P0048). He transfers Voityuk toward Semipalov as an international adviser. Semipalov is to appear a political rival who leaks secrets; Elena is to appear Gamov's mistress, giving her jealous husband a convincing personal motive (P0049–P0061). Gamov explicitly proposes a pretended affair. Semipalov agrees; Elena starts adopting the performance (P0062–P0069). The intended false hostility produces genuine emotional coldness in Semipalov, while his loyalty and service persist.
  • Continuity resolution: The purpose behind Elena's government appointment in P2-C03 P0104–P0113 is now revealed as access for the staged triangle, alongside the claimed administrative purpose. Semipalov connects it to Gamov's earlier inquiries about jealousy, before discovery of Voityuk (P0062); this cross-part recollection requires Part 1 alignment. The northern threat predicted in P2-C03/P2-C05 is now an actual attack. Gamov's initial wrong judgment and reluctance to accept a strategic sacrifice are part of the narrated conflict, not an authorial contradiction.
  • Open rank question: P2-C05 P0083 calls Ferdinand Vaksel a four-star general; Gamov calls him Marshal Vaksel here (P0048), without an explicit promotion. Record as possible rank drift or an unreported promotion, pending later evidence. Review: all 69 paragraphs read; no affair asserted as fact; the source's retrospective interpretation is kept distinct from events and character explanations.

P2-C07 — Weather defense and encirclement

Source: P2-C07, P0001–P0103, part0017.html.

  • People and offices: Semipalov commands Zabon's defense and is addressed as general (P0025, P0042). Pavel is colonel, contrasted retrospectively with his earlier captaincy (P0010). Vaksel is called marshal, approximately 50, and wears four gold stars (P0040), sustaining the unresolved general/marshal terminology question. Franz Putrament, president of Nordag, declares war after the flood (P0098). Shtupa temporarily assumes command when Semipalov and Pavel fly to Adan (P0103).
  • Press and opposition: Omar Isiro's news does not disguise the crisis; Zabon's street screens update troop maps hourly. Fagusta attacks intelligence competence. Pavel says Isiro briefs Fagusta daily and Gamov summons him after especially scandalous articles. Pavel suspects secrets behind the editor's conduct and vows to expose them; these are his suspicions (P0002–P0006).
  • Geography and forces: Enemy divisions outnumber Zabon's defenders five to one (P0007). Hills arc around the city from the east toward the northern sea; the southwest sector is lowest, with wooded marshy lowlands before it, where Vaksel concentrates (P0043). Nordag lies northeast, sends a minister rather than its president to the conference, and is said here not to have requested goods or money (P0021). This distinguishes Nordag from the generalized allied demands and from P2-C06 P0029's claim that it resented denied aid; preserve this tension for reconciliation.
  • Intelligence technology: Nail-like sensors hidden in posts, ground, and bridges measure total iron mass, without separating tanks, guns, vehicles, and small metal objects. Personal sensors tune to an officer's individual biofield or receiver and connect him with agents. The observation center divides coverage into south, southwest, west, northwest, and northeast sectors (P0011–P0020). Northeast readings show a heavy Nordag border buildup; Semipalov reports it but takes no adequate precaution (P0021–P0026, P0098).
  • Timing and resources: Shtupa arrives the same night; equipment arrives by morning. Setup initially needs two days. First reinforcements need one week, complete concentration two weeks; Shtupa can guarantee three, at most four days of flood, not a week (P0027–P0038). National stations feed clouds to local field generators; enemy local capacity is lower but removes surprise (P0045–P0068). During the actual battle Semipalov estimates ordinary cloud water for a week, but energy-water for only two days of this intensity (P0090). These quantities concern different resources and conditions, not an automatic numerical contradiction.
  • Battle sequence: Semipalov expects a night assault after rest. Vaksel instead secretly withdraws troops, leaving fixed guns and camouflage, to limit losses from the expected storm (P0069–P0074). Semipalov orders immediate weather attack and then rain despite Shtupa's warning of friendly flooding. Two opposing storms collide over the heights; Shtupa and an operator rescue Semipalov from the wind (P0075–P0087). Sealed enemy vehicles and amphibians escape by swimming or moving along the bottom. Equipment is damaged, attack delayed, but army not destroyed (P0089–P0092). The rain lasts two nights and two days. Zabon floods; Shtupa declines to expend safety reserves chasing the enemy and releases cloud control (P0093–P0095). Semipalov's final forecast is at least one week before a new attack over the mud, revising Pavel's earlier minimum three days to dry (P0091, P0095).
  • Nordag's attack: Flooding of its border regions gives Putrament the stated cause of meteorological aggression. Nordag declares war, overwhelms border defenses, and encircles Zabon; troops appear on its northeast outskirts. Pavel expects them at the current weather command post the next day (P0098–P0103). Semipalov blames himself and interprets Vaksel's maneuver as a trap; enemy planning is inferred from events, not directly narrated from its headquarters.
  • Source-level concerns: The intelligence laboratory is placed on the ninth floor of headquarters (P0011), but P0039 calls the laboratory whose displays Semipalov duplicates underground. No move or separate laboratory is explained: apparent spatial continuity issue. P0055 calls the season midsummer and then evokes late-summer days in the same paragraph; possibly loose seasonal language, not a date suitable for exact chronology. Review: all 103 paragraphs read; forecasts, resource quantities, and actual durations kept separate. No unqualified claim that the flood destroyed Vaksel's army.

P2-C08 — Airborne capture and the manufactured threat

Source: P2-C08, P0001–P0097, part0018.html.

  • Siege: Nordag captures the hills and outlying food depots, completing Zabon's encirclement. Flooded lowlands still separate Nordag's troops from Vaksel's army. Putrament broadcasts his intent to starve the city and parade among its dead (P0007). Food lasts two weeks at old rations, four at half rations; starvation is expected in week five (P0010, P0018). Government members accept the same reduced ration. Their exclusion from gold pay is explained by Gamov's distinction between inherited national reserves and current government service paid in kalons (P0011).
  • Production: Four new energy-water factories are operating, twelve nearing readiness, 31 under construction; projected total about sixty in a year (P0013). These are pipeline categories and a future total, not a claim that 4+12+31 equals60. Pre-coup Latania had about five government waterplanes and no combat fleet; about twenty have now been produced, with several hundred projected by spring (P0014). Only Cortezia had accumulated production expertise and a military waterplane fleet according to Bar's report. Earlier small-scale use/availability requires Part 1 reconciliation.
  • Relief plan and raid: Gamov cancels western offensive operations, leaves firm defenses, and orders freed troops north to defeat the besiegers within three or four weeks; Peano gives six weeks as the minimum transfer period (P0016–P0019). Pustovoyt's proposed airlift is rejected as too small. Aircraft instead raid Nordag's forest headquarters near its capital, inaccessible by defended roads (P0020–P0028). Semipalov objects to revealing secret production, then consents after Gamov's personal appeal to shared responsibility.
