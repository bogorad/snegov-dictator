Characters and relationships

Catalog index. Entries are added in reading order. Each claim names its source chapter. Later revelations must preserve the earlier evidence and distinguish aliases from separate people.

Andrei Semipalov

First-person narrator and witness at the Thursday gathering. He works in a laboratory; his exact profession is not stated in this chapter. He is initially skeptical of Gamov's warning, then recognizes that Gamov's speech has made him psychologically ready to follow. He fights several gang members and later reports Gamov's conduct with retrospective uncertainty. He first met Elena twelve years before this chapter; their marriage is strained but emotionally binding, and its duration is not stated. Source: P1-C01.

Alexei Gamov

Astrophysicist, philosopher by inclination, and physically explosive speaker. He shifts rapidly between anger, mockery, calm, and compassion; the narrator identifies these changes as a method used against opponents. He predicts that Marutzian's support for Patina will trigger world war the next day, calls the coming war purposeless and criminal, and argues that Cortezia is waiting for a pretext while Latania's leaders are foolish. In a street fight he uses extreme violence, then releases the injured attackers after ordering them to fear his name. He treats political words as material force. Source: P1-C01.

Pavel Prishchepa

Engineer in Semipalov's laboratory. He brings Semipalov to Bar's Thursday gathering and is a friend of Kazimir Shtupa. His father, General Leonid Prishchepa, has official contact with the meteorological service. Source: P1-C01.

Gottlieb Bar

Host of the Thursday meetings, literature expert, ironist, and cynic. At this point he is chief engineer of a motor-building factory, but the narrator later recognizes a deeper nature as a state organizer. He enjoys presenting two convincing opposite opinions and values his role in the group. He argues that no large war can begin without visible preparations and that modern war is a meteorological contest. Source: P1-C01.

John Woodworth

Cortezian who moved to Latania about ten years earlier and claims to have found his ideal homeland. He criticizes Latanian institutions while praising Cortezia, and supports party leader Arthur Marutzian during the Thursday dispute. He later helps Semipalov and Gamov against the gang and remains cool toward Gamov's theatrical threats. Source: P1-C01.

Arkady Gonzalez

Teacher of art history and specialist in ancient painting. His delicate, beautiful appearance contrasts with the narrator's retrospective description of him as a fanatic of cruelty and a strongman. The later description is reported knowledge, not an action witnessed in this chapter. Source: P1-C01.

Nikolai Pustovoyt

At the gathering, an accountant in a construction office with an oversized, asymmetrical appearance and a quiet, gentle voice. He questions why a border dispute should become war. The narrator later identifies his kindness and soft voice as genuine and names his future office as Minister of Mercy. Source: P1-C01.

Kazimir Shtupa

Military meteorologist and friend of Pavel Prishchepa. He reports planned climate stability, no dangerous weather disturbance, and an excellent forecast for the next two days. Source: P1-C01.

Elena

Semipalov's wife. They first met twelve years before this chapter; the duration of their marriage is not stated. She has spent a month away at her father's home and returns despite believing that life with Semipalov is difficult, because life without him is impossible. Their conversation shows affection, jealousy, resentment, and fear of another confrontation. Source: P1-C01.

Arthur Marutzian

Chairman of the Latanian government, defended by Woodworth. His promise to support Patina is the immediate trigger Gamov cites for war. A news broadcast reports that he has signed a decree to mobilize volunteers for Patina, ages eighteen to fifty-five. Source: P1-C01.

Amin Amentola

President of Cortezia. The news broadcast reports an alarming speech by him after Lamaria requests help. Source: P1-C01.

Vilkomir Torba

Patina's leader as described by Gamov. Gamov calls him a bombastic figure who may drag Latania into a fight for narrow interests. The chapter provides no independent confirmation of his office or motives. Source: P1-C01.

Leonid Prishchepa

General and father of Pavel Prishchepa. His official work brings him into contact with the military meteorological service; he favors the young scientist Shtupa. Source: P1-C01.

Bar's unnamed wife

An unnamed woman who serves tea at Bar's gathering and leaves. Semipalov says he never remembers her appearance, treating her repeated appearance and disappearance as her only narrative significance at this stage. Source: P1-C01.

P1-C02 state updates

  • Andrei Semipalov: Receives a volunteer summons, is appointed captain, and is assigned as a radio saboteur in the Steel Ram division. His rank reflects a military-science course and field training completed three years earlier. He departs for Patina's wooded western mountains. Source: P1-C02.
  • Elena: Is mobilized to synthesize medicines at a pharmaceutical factory in Adan. She leaves the city the morning after her final evening with Semipalov. Source: P1-C02.
  • Pavel Prishchepa: Has formed two volunteer battalions and will lead a third. He recruits Semipalov into his father's Steel Ram division and explains its radio, resonance, and electro-artillery equipment. Source: P1-C02.
  • Leonid Prishchepa: Commands the Steel Ram division. Pavel says Leonid considers Gamov a born soldier. Source: P1-C02.
  • Alexei Gamov: Has become a major and deputy technical officer in Leonid's division. His military role confirms that his earlier violence and certainty were not only theatrical, while the chapter does not explain how he advanced so quickly. Source: P1-C02.
  • Arthur Marutzian: Delays ordering an invasion of Roder while speaking publicly in favor of the alliance. The chapter contrasts his cautious “volunteer” language with Amentola's open deployment of regular troops. Source: P1-C02.
  • Amin Amentola: Sends regular Cortezian soldiers and weapons into Roder and maintains an active intervention while Latanian leaders hesitate. Source: P1-C02.

Knurka IX

Hereditary king of Torbash, a small state described as having no army or industry. He attends the allied conference in Adan himself. Semipalov's retrospective narration says that this apparently weak ruler later proves more intelligent than all allied leaders combined; this is a forward-looking narrator claim, not yet an action witnessed in the present timeline. Source: P1-C02.

Albert Peano

Twenty-two-year-old operator and planner in the Steel Ram division. He is Arthur Marutzian's nephew. Rumors say he is estranged from his uncle, but Semipalov has heard warm conversations between them. Peano says that people do not tell his uncle what he dislikes, and Gamov suspects Marutzian placed him in the front line to expose him to a fatal resonance strike. Peano is lively, technically capable, and able to plan fighting withdrawals. Source: P1-C03.

P1-C03 state updates

  • Andrei Semipalov: Is now a major and commands a mobile sabotage detachment formed to strike into the enemy's encirclement. Source: P1-C03.
  • Pavel Prishchepa: Is now a captain and commander of divisional reconnaissance. He supplies a deliberately favorable report on Steel Ram at Marshal Komlin's request. Source: P1-C03.
  • Leonid Prishchepa: Suffers severe vibration after a nearby resonance shell explodes but refuses hospital care and keeps command. He orders circular defense and planned withdrawals. Source: P1-C03.
  • Alexei Gamov: Is promoted from major and deputy technical officer to colonel and deputy division commander three days before the chapter's main action. Leonid and Pavel now present him as a future army leader. Source: P1-C03.
  • Arkady Gonzalez: Serves as an operator who performs his orders but dislikes war and does not seek combat. He is physically powerful and technically reliable; the narrator calls his later role grave but does not yet disclose it. Source: P1-C03.
  • Arthur Marutzian: Remains at the center of the alliance's public story while his nephew serves at the exposed front. Gamov suspects deliberate family betrayal; this is not confirmed. Source: P1-C03.
  • Vilkomir Torba: Orders Patina to stop fighting and asks for a separate peace after Patina's defeat. He says Marutzian misled him by fielding volunteers rather than a professional army. Source: P1-C03.

Birker Shtok

Roderian captain and deputy to Lieutenant Colonel Peter Parp. Captured after scouts destroy his vehicle, he resists death and torture threats but yields to Gamov's threat of filmed humiliation with a pig. His name is genuine despite Gamov's initial suspicion. He gives information while undrugged, then requests execution to atone for betraying his oath. Gamov keeps him alive. Source: P1-C04, P0007–P0071.

Peter Parp

Roderian lieutenant colonel commanding the guards regiment that escorts three hundred million captured kalons toward Roder. Shtok is his deputy. This initial information comes from Shtok's interrogation. Source: P1-C04, P0055–P0059.

P1-C04 state updates

  • Gamov and Leonid: Leonid's injury prevents sustained command work. Gamov issues orders independently (P0003). His interrogation uses threatened humiliation, not physical torture; he orders a false heroic broadcast before the fighting occurs (P0026–P0045, P0079–P0084).
  • Gonzalez: Eagerly assists the humiliation threat; his pleasure is observed (P0027, P0039). Semipalov's claim that he deliberately dirtied the pig is inference (P0040).
  • Semipalov and Pavel: Will lead the night convoy raid with Gamov; Semipalov supplies the sabotage force and Pavel the scouts (P0074–P0078).

Source: P1-C04.

Filipp Korkin

General commanding Golden Wings. Leonid says he exaggerates his ability to resist; Komlin's later report confirms that Korkin's favorable messages conceal a division broken into isolated groups. Korkin requests urgent help as his last defense is threatened. Source: P1-C05, P0006, P0100–P0106.

Anton Komlin

Marshal called Commander-in-Chief in P1-C03 P0004. His P1-C05 P0106 dispatch is signed commander of the Western Front. He orders Steel Ram to hold the Barta despite Golden Wings' collapse; Leonid requests permission to rescue it. The titles are recorded as supplied, with no demonstrated incompatibility. Source: P1-C03, P1-C05.

P1-C05 state updates

Gamov distributes captured money as combat rewards without authorization. Pavel gives unconditional support. Semipalov objects, refuses endorsement, but will not openly oppose him; he explicitly says Pavel has the stronger claim to being Gamov's first follower (P0040–P0072). Leonid neither approves nor cancels the payments (P0097). Gamov explains that public fame protects commanders from superior interference (P0021–P0023). Source: P1-C05.

Semyon Serbin

Private in the second battalion. Leads demands for equal distribution of the captured cash and tries to force access to the vehicles. Gamov has him thrown into the latrine instead of executed, then orders washing and leaves open a chance to earn distinction in battle. Source: P1-C06, P0022–P0054.

Varella

Soldier in the sabotage detachment. Challenges Serbin's lack of wounds while others in his section have been wounded, then helps subdue him. No first name is supplied. Source: P1-C06, P0035–P0044.

P1-C06 state updates

Semipalov prevents a volley into the mutinous crowd, proposes the westward rescue, and withdraws his objections to continued rewards for immediate practical reasons. Leonid approves the breakout despite Komlin's order. Gonzalez argues for rewarding kills over captures; Gamov rejects this, defending spared lives and induced surrender. By chapter end, the narrator describes himself, Peano, Gonzalez, and Pavel as Gamov's followers, without full knowledge of his plans. Source: P1-C06, P0039, P0058–P0082, P0105.

P1-C07 command changes

Leonid refuses to sign the continuing reward scheme; Gamov signs it (P0003–P0005). Peano designs the rescue deployment; Semipalov and Pavel lead blocking regiments, Gamov the heavy force, Leonid coordinates (P0015–P0016). After the rescue, Leonid makes Gamov corps commander, retains Steel Ram, takes the injured Korkin as deputy, and proposes Semipalov for Golden Wings. These appointments await higher approval (P0075–P0095). Peano's present reaction to the implied challenge to his uncle is unknown; later loyalty is retrospective (P0099). Source: P1-C07.

Danilo Mordasov

Marutzian's emissary and Komlin's personal representative. Introduced as a general, he explicitly corrects the title to state councillor. He reports national losses and disorder, then demands recovery of the cash and soldiers' rewards. He reveals Marutzian's conditional order to arrest Peano. Gamov sentences him to death; Gonzalez kills him when he draws a resonator. Pavel records the meeting. Source: P1-C08, P0017–P0100. His losses/corruption discussion is reported speech, not independent verification.

P1-C08 state updates

Serbin earns rewards for two resonators, an officer, and a wound, then publicly reconciles with Gamov. The narrator's account of his later sinister influence is a future warning, not a present office (P0008–P0013). Peano openly accuses high officials' families of potential embezzlement; Mordasov quotes Marutzian's distrust and arrest instruction (P0072–P0077). Leonid declares complete support for Gamov after the killing and public statement (P0101–P0103). Source: P1-C08.

Sergeant Serov

Guard sergeant identified retrospectively as the man who nearly fired on Serbin during the cash mutiny. He joins the delegation promising to defend the commanders. No first name is supplied. Source: P1-C09, P0006.

P1-C09 identity and allegiance updates

  • Varella: First name Grigory; Pavel's trusted scout and spokesman for the secretly organized delegation (P0008, P0020). Earlier entry's absent first name is now supplied.
  • Woodworth: Now Marutzian's international-affairs adviser. Defects politically, preserves the corps' weapons and unit structure against orders, and urges Gamov to take supreme power (P0040, P0060–P0097).
  • Pavel, Peano, Gonzalez: Secretly organized soldiers and isolated Mordasov's guards before the meeting, without informing Gamov, Semipalov, or the generals (P0018–P0032, P0072–P0073). Their initiative must not be attributed to a previously disclosed Gamov order.
  • Elena/Semipalov/Gamov: Elena reunites with her husband and denies romantic interest in Woodworth. Gamov says he is unmarried and later asks about Semipalov's jealousy; romantic intent is denied and a later strategic purpose is foreshadowed (P0036–P0054, P0109–P0115).

Source: P1-C09.

Colonel Morokhov

Head of government security. Reports the failure of city and then palace communications during the coup. His attempts to summon protection fail before Varella's soldiers occupy the chamber. No first name is supplied. Source: P1-C10, P0053–P0084.

P1-C10 final Part 1 states

  • Gamov: Takes supreme power through a military coup and a coerced broadcast transfer. Described in the broadcast as a nonparty colonel; he accepts office but postpones the government list and program (P0118–P0119).
  • Semipalov: Owns the coup's operational plan and orders removal of the former ministers. Gamov explicitly thanks him for the success (P0006–P0009, P0120–P0125).
  • Pavel/Woodworth: Pavel coordinates captured sites; Woodworth supplies the prepared resignation and camera crew (P0039–P0044, P0106–P0116).
  • Peano/Marutzian: Peano threatens to kill his uncle unless he accepts the transfer. Marutzian regrets sending him to the front instead of prison, confirming hostility; he yields and reads the statement (P0097–P0118).
  • Komlin/Korkin: Korkin's demotion is now announced by Marutzian (P0051). Varella strips Komlin's insignia. Semipalov's impression of Komlin's mental collapse is explicitly disproved by his later knowledge (P0084–P0088, P0120–P0122).
  • Shtupa: Reports to the government on enemy weather-station construction and harvest threats (P0028–P0030).

Source: P1-C10.

Part 2 — Offices and relationships in source order

P2-C01 — From six coup leaders to a governing program

Gamov leads the six-person nucleus and chooses “Dictator.” Semipalov accepts humiliating punishments despite recognizing their criminal character. Marutzian remains under arrest; the former commanders' current offices follow the coup, not their earlier civilian jobs. P2-C01, P0002–P0005, P0028–P0033.

The Core is unelected and irremovable; a subordinate layer of accountable elected ministers is planned. Elena questions whether Semipalov can remain a subordinate. His statement that she cannot lie is his judgment, not an omniscient guarantee. P2-C02, P0044–P0062.

P2-C03 — Anton Karmanyuk

Police lieutenant colonel, about forty-five, father of three boys. He concealed bribes and a paid escape after reconfirmation in office; one escapee was his wife's brother. Gonzalez sentences him, Pustovoyt objects, and Semipalov approves hanging instead of drowning in filth. Family penalties accompany the sentence. P2-C03, P0021–P0032.

P2-C03 — The two editors

Pimen Georgiu, former Maximalist close to Marutzian, receives the government Herald of Terror and Mercy. Optimate leader Konstantin Fagusta receives permission to revive the Tribune and promises fierce criticism. Their political hostility is explicit; Semipalov's future assessment of Fagusta does not yet disclose his final role. P2-C03, P0034–P0047.

P2-C03 — Jean Voityuk and Shirbai Shar

Voityuk is Woodworth's expert on southern neighbors. His Repentance Sheet omits his connection with Torbash envoy Shirbai Shar. Pavel calls Shar a Cortezian informant and links an emerald necklace worn by Voityuk's wife to Shar's family collection. This supports suspicion; Semipalov still considers a sexual rather than political payment. Gamov orders a false northern-offensive leak despite Semipalov's warning. P2-C03, P0073–P0100. Voityuk/Voytyuk and Shar/Shirbay are English transliteration variants, not separate identities.

Elena is asked to become Bar's deputy. Gamov offers an administrative reason but withholds another purpose until Voityuk's exposure; Semipalov agrees to bring her next day. This extends C008, without establishing an affair. P0101–P0113.

P2-C04 — Public targets and critics

Eduard Konveizer, described as the richest banker and a military-corporation chief, ends the first list of one hundred summoned people, headed by Amentola. Fagusta attacks unlawful dictatorship, paid killing, inaccessible trials, and collective punishment. He nevertheless accepts one gang leader's degrading execution; he does not reject every use of Terror. Gamov protects him from the arrest demanded by Semipalov and Gonzalez. P2-C04, P0011–P0046.

P2-C05 — Allies and the enemy commander

Lon Chudin is president of Great Lepin; Kir Kirun is his brother. Knurka arrives with Shar, an ambassador for special missions. Elena appears publicly as a deputy minister. Omar Isiro is the quiet, competent information minister; Semipalov does not yet know why Gamov selected him. Ferdinand Vaksel is introduced as a four-star general and deputy to supreme commander Amentola. His arrival and northern troop movement are indirect evidence used against Voityuk. P2-C05, P0005–P0030, P0071–P0091.

P2-C06 — Alliance rupture and the staged triangle

Mgobo Mardoba, Sobrana's president, is about forty; Woodworth warns that his silent agreement can conceal opposition. Gamov suspends aid and rejects Semipalov's proposed sacrifice of Zabon. He assigns Voityuk to Semipalov and asks Elena to pretend to be his mistress, giving her husband a plausible personal motive for political rivalry. Semipalov agrees, but develops real emotional coldness during the performance. No actual affair is established. P2-C06, P0002–P0034, P0048–P0069.

P2-C07 — Northern commanders

Franz Putrament, president of Nordag, declares war after its border districts flood. Semipalov is addressed as general and commands Zabon's defense; Pavel is now colonel. Vaksel, about fifty, is called marshal while still wearing four gold stars. Shtupa takes temporary command when Semipalov and Pavel leave for Adan. P2-C07, P0010, P0025, P0040–P0042, P0098–P0103.

P2-C08 — Anna Kursai and the staged threat

Anna Kursai is Voityuk's wife. Semipalov still considers both an affair and an intelligence payment as explanations of the necklace. He knowingly invents a plan for mass extermination and deportation of Nordag's people, presenting himself as its helpless opponent. Gamov adopts specific coercive measures against captured officers, but this does not turn the whole invented genocide story into policy. P2-C08, P0035, P0049–P0095. Kursai/Kursay is an English transliteration variation.

P2-C09 — Captive officers and returning guards

Sumo Pariona introduces himself as colonel general and deputy commander-in-chief; Kinza Vardant is lieutenant general and chief of the main staff. Six other major generals complete the eight. Grigory Varella commands their guard; Ivan Serov and Semyon Serbin led the raid's entry. Semipalov interprets Varella's smile as mutual forgiveness after the earlier mutiny; the friendship is not independently proved. P2-C09, P0006–P0017.

Putrament first refuses concessions, then orders a ceasefire and withdrawal. Semipalov rejoices, but Gonzalez brings an order to shoot the captive officers. Pustovoyt obtains only treatment of their bodies. The chapter ends with the sentence still pending, not a witnessed execution. P0033–P0038, P0069–P0113.

Part 3 — Turning point

P3-C01–P3-C04 — Complicity, coercion, and debate

  • P3-C01: Semipalov threatens to resign over the reported prisoner executions, then accepts moral complicity after Gamov connects them to his threats. This is Gamov's political argument and Semipalov's response, not proof that Semipalov ordered the killings. Elena is in her second week of inspections. Evidence, P0002–P0023, P0067.
  • P3-C02: Gamov values Fagusta's criticism but says it cannot measure the silent population. Semipalov asks Gonzalez to arrest an unnamed person; the next chapter identifies her. Evidence, P0023–P0032.
  • P3-C03: Voityuk admits spying and names Semipalov as his source. Semipalov detains Anna without a stated offense, coerces her husband, then releases her. Voityuk says she knows nothing of the espionage. The secret audience of Gamov, Pavel, and Woodworth confirms that Semipalov's offer to replace Gamov is an operation. Evidence, P0023–P0067, P0108–P0128.
  • P3-C04: Orest Biber, philosophy professor, and Arnold Falk, novelist, visit from neutral Klur. Falk glorifies war; Biber disputes Gamov's view of human nature. Semipalov reconstructs their interview from memory and guarantees its meaning, not verbatim dialogue. Evidence, P0025–P0033, P0046–P0052, P0143–P0163.

P3-C05–P3-C08 — Opposition, loyalty, and religion

  • P3-C05: Leonard Bernoulli is a former farmer, professor, leader of Cortezian independents, Amentola's enemy, and Woodworth's university contemporary. He opposes burdening Cortezia with Latania's former allies, not the war itself. Elena receives a technology award but complains that her promised larger role is delayed. Evidence, P0007–P0017, P0061–P0075.
  • P3-C06: Gamov rejects Pavel's proposed ballot manipulation, promises to resign if defeated, and names Semipalov as successor. Semipalov says Gamov can be removed but cannot be replaced in their common work. Evidence, P0025–P0059.
  • P3-C07: Fagusta explains that his criticism has accompanied support for the best available government. Amentola orders Vaksel to await the vote; Bernoulli urges immediate attack. Serbin saves Gamov from a knife and is wounded. Varella saves one attacker; Pavel suspects Komlin and arrests him, Marutzian, and associates. The plot's leadership is not yet proved. Evidence, P0002–P0017, P0022–P0031, P0055–P0069, P0089–P0092.
  • P3-C08: Tarkhun-khor is Konduk's chief religious leader and Mamun's seventy-fourth embodiment. Maravan-khor controls parliament and ordered the Sorbas raid without a parliamentary vote. Gamov reveals that he grew up among Mamun's followers. Isiro heads the occupation commission; Kir Kirun is now explicitly a commander-in-chief. Evidence, P0004–P0007, P0028–P0046.

P3-C09–P3-C13 — Frames, mutiny, and a false execution

  • P3-C09: Pavel and Semipalov plan to frame Bernoulli with Latania's own enemy-funded accounts. Semipalov's convincing performance includes real doubts about Gamov and a false assertion that the Konduk deployment used every serviceable waterplane. Evidence, P0006–P0070.
  • P3-C10: Alfred Palman, Ivan Kordobin, Sergei Skripnik, and Jean Vilta lead the aircrew mutiny. Colonel Korney Kaplin is identified as Mordasov's former pilot. Gamov pardons the crews and commanders and promotes the four proven performers to brigade commands. He nearly loses consciousness when the celebrating crowd carries him; no actual faint is established. The liaison remains under arrest for professional failure. Evidence, P0021–P0081.
  • P3-C11: Bernoulli is abducted through an unnamed neutral state, then brought to Latania on the promised third day. He refuses cooperation and correctly analyzes the enemy-resource strategy. Woodworth reluctantly accepts a false account of their youthful pact. Fourteen years since their last meeting is distinct from roughly ten years of Woodworth's residence in Latania. Evidence, P0009–P0068.
  • P3-C12: Semipalov voluntarily proposes his false treason and simulated execution. Gamov, Pavel, and Woodworth approve; Pustovoyt will protect him. Elena is deliberately kept ignorant. His staged arrest and Serbin's hatred produce real distress; his final false confession makes Elena leave in anger and contempt. Evidence, P0002–P0028, P0062–P0158.
  • P3-C13: Semipalov sees Peano crying at the gallows. Inside the sack he switches from believing Peano uninformed to believing his own execution real. These are successive frightened interpretations; the part does not confirm death. Evidence, P0002–P0005.

Part 4 — Offensive

P4-C01–P4-C05 — Survival, scientists, and return

  • P4-C01: Semipalov wakes alive. Pavel and Pustovoyt report a two-day bedside vigil after unexpectedly prolonged unconsciousness. Varella guards him against recognition. Elena's knowledge is not yet established. Evidence, P0002–P0016, P0021–P0036, P0058–P0060.
  • P4-C02: Berthold Shvurts is a nuclear physicist; Berthold Kozyura is a chronophysicist. Both are bald, but they are separate people. Komlin founded their laboratory and Pavel funds it. Kozyura attributes his arrival to a nuclear accident; Shvurts reports an unsuccessful crossing attempt. Evidence, P0003–P0055.
  • P4-C03: Kozyura's story is qualified: he was found as an amnesiac adolescent near Sorbas and later recognized screened landscapes as childhood memories. His origin remains unproved. Shvurts has a traceable local past. Both now have reported bodily and appetite changes. Gamov has not yet been told of the laboratory. Evidence, P0020–P0027, P0050–P0054.
  • P4-C04: Semipalov resumes his duties. Most ministers learn of his survival that day; Peano had advance warning but doubted the ruse at the scaffold. Elena still believes him dead, and Semipalov refuses early notification. Kaplin and the four former mutiny leaders receive the conference-capture mission. Evidence, P0005–P0035.
  • P4-C05: Vaksel resists capture but is taken alive and humiliated under orders. He still believes Semipalov dead. Amentola's fate is not yet known; captured conference delegates do not include every invited leader by definition. Evidence, P0010–P0032.

P4-C06–P4-C10 — Camp judgments and political settlement

  • P4-C06: Camp commandant Ishim Samino, deputy Purp Gorgon, and doctor Pop Barvell receive punishments mirroring their crimes. Doctor Gabl Khota is freed and rewarded for secretly donating blood. Voityuk has escaped; Gamov rejects Semipalov's proposed use of Anna as hostage. Amentola's absence is resolved as escape. Gamov publicly exposes the Bernoulli/Semipalov deception. Evidence, P0002, P0013–P0075.
  • P4-C07: Roder corps general Mart Tronshke succeeds Vaksel. After encirclement he releases his officers from loyalty and kills himself; Peano grants military burial. Pavel says Elena's real grief served the ruse. Semipalov's memory that Pavel once loved her is his perception. Evidence, P0002–P0003, P0018–P0034, P0062–P0065.
  • P4-C08: Elena explains that her hatred addressed the traitor-mask she believed. She considered revenge through Gamov, but says he did not want an affair. Semipalov still loves her, asks for time, and promises to return home. His “former wife” label is emotional; no legal divorce is stated. Evidence, P0005–P0086.
  • P4-C09: Torba leads Patina's Maximalists; Ponsiy Markvard and pianist Lyudmila (Lyuda) Miloshevskaya lead its Optimates. Gamov forces Torba and Markvard into a four-man Core, under wartime administrator Leonid Prishchepa. Miloshevskaya chairs the separate Women's Control Committee. Torba's account of his political motives remains his defense. Evidence, P0027–P0037, P0121–P0166, P0192–P0210.
  • P4-C10: Bar, Pustovoyt, and Gonzalez design the prisoner-relief scheme under Gamov's objective; Semipalov disclaims its authorship. After civilian raids, Semipalov proposes the execution flight with uncondemned hostages. Gamov approves. Evidence, P0009–P0034, P0035–P0054.

P4-C11–P4-C15 — Hostages, mothers, and surrender

  • P4-C11: An unnamed young journalist aboard Vilta's aircraft appeals to her father, an aviation general. She is an uncondemned hostage, released on return. She must not be silently merged with a later named captive. Evidence, P0025–P0032, P0043–P0049.
  • P4-C12: Tom Torkin is Amentola's special envoy. Luiza Putrament is Nordag's president's daughter. Zhanna Torkin, Tom's stepdaughter, is a separate captive; both women concealed themselves as nurses. Knurka's paid White Court membership lets Shar seek a stay. Evidence, P0035–P0056.
  • P4-C13: Shar says Anna rejected his advances, that he loves her, and that he secured membership to protect her. This disputes Semipalov's affair inference but remains Shar's testimony. Anna believes Voityuk dead; his death is unproved. Zhanna uses her fiancé's surname Garmish. Torkin fails to obtain the prisoners' release, and her execution is announced for next morning. Evidence, P0002–P0028, P0050–P0075.
  • P4-C14: The narration reports Zhanna executed. News then names her mother, singer Radon Torkin, as Tom's killer. Richard Garmish is Zhanna's fiancé. Norma Friz, mathematics professor, has captive son Arman and dead son Pyotr; Eliza Paur's son Kurt lost his right arm. Gamov threatens Arman but does not execute him. His personal knowledge comes from Pavel's dossiers. Evidence, P0002–P0044.
  • P4-C15: Franz Putrament, forty-three and a cavalry general, ultimately surrenders to save Luiza. Her sentence had secretly been stayed; her survival was concealed by a blanket announcement. Semipalov and Pavel intended to prevent her hanging if necessary. An unnamed woman then shoots at Semipalov; Pavel's immediate death cry is not a medical outcome. Evidence, P0028–P0032, P0041–P0058, P0098–P0136.

Part 5 — The Devil of a God Not Ours

P5-C01–P5-C06 — Rescue and disagreement

  • P5-C01: Semipalov survives by moving aside. Anna Kursai is the captured attacker, a Florian; she says she acts for Floria, not her husband. Semipalov and Pavel say they do not know Voityuk's fate. Anna is sent to the public conspiracy trial. Evidence, P0002–P0011, P0024, P0038–P0071.
  • P5-C02: Semipalov reunites with Elena in their reconstructed Zabon room in Adan. His former work is specified as engineer and head of an automation laboratory. School friend Albert Loskin died rescuing a girl whom Semipalov saved; he is not Albert Peano. The marriage heals, but public duties immediately draw Semipalov away. Evidence, P0004–P0037, P0069–P0074.
  • P5-C03: Elena is now Minister of Health. Semipalov proposes international epidemic rescue; Norma, already-known Radon, Miloshevskaya, and Luiza are proposed organizers. The source does not introduce Radon's identity anew. Evidence, P0003–P0035.
  • P5-C04: Captain Konrad Komlin is Anton's nephew and the surviving attacker. He admits the attempt but denies his uncle's or Marutzian's orders. Fagusta defends political opponents. All defendants are released, explicitly not acquitted; Konrad joins the guard and Anna joins Elena's rescue work. Gamov confirms his plan with Gonzalez while denying that Fagusta knew it. Evidence, P0029–P0135, P0154–P0168.
  • P5-C05: Semipalov, Pavel, Bar, and Peano favor renewed military action; the other six Core members reject it. Semipalov submits but refuses to resign. His strategic and personal separation from Gamov is now real. Evidence, P0004–P0034.
  • P5-C06: Radon has been acquitted by jury. Corps commander Arman Pliss takes power in Klur after the women's revolt is crushed. Gamov's condition worsens; the narrator later interprets the apparent depression as illness. Gonzalez speaks of his own irredeemable guilt and highest court. Evidence, P0002, P0045–P0063, P0096.

P5-C07–P5-C11 — Acting authority, referendum, and peace

  • P5-C07: Gamov suffers a heart attack and loses consciousness. Semipalov assumes his governmental functions and promises the referendum and conditional supply preparations. Serbin accuses him of seeking power; the doctor's warning is conditional, and the narrator's despair-based cause remains interpretation. Evidence, P0006–P0041.
  • P5-C08: Putrament is freed to organize Nordag's vote. He credits seeing mercy and Luiza's work with changing his attitude. Serbin broadcasts genuine sickroom details with nurses Sonya (Sonechka) and Matilda. Gamov says he does not script them. Pliss reports Biber in Gonzalez's dungeons; that is not confirmed death. Evidence, P0005–P0099, P0133–P0148.
  • P5-C09: Putrament resumes Nordag's presidency; Luiza rallies support. Semipalov votes no personally, then implements the emerging public yes. The eighty-percent figure is Isiro's estimate pending the final count. Evidence, P0011, P0034–P0047.
  • P5-C10: Queen Agnessa declares peace with Latania while retaining friendship with Cortezia. Pliss goes further: he breaks obligations to Cortezia, declares war on it, allies with Latania, and submits Klur's army to Peano. Shtupa also commands Latanian forces in Nordag. Gamov remains weak; Semipalov keeps acting duties. Evidence, P0002–P0013, P0036–P0038, P0049–P0069.
  • P5-C11: Amentola survives the vehicle mine and the final confrontation with Radon. Norma leads the Peace Committee and Radon the more militant wing. An unnamed delegate's fiancé dies, and two officers are described lifeless after the first palace confrontation. At the second march Amentola announces peace and regains public affection. “Last president” is retrospective language, not his death in this scene. Evidence, P0012–P0017, P0028–P0047, P0065–P0086, P0118–P0133.

Part 6 — Cleansing

P6-C01–P6-C05 — Survivors, settlement, and judgment

  • P6-C01: Zhanna Torkin and Nordag officers Sumo Pariona and Kinza Vardant appear alive, among hundreds previously announced executed. Gonzalez and Pustovoyt openly cooperate. The gathering does not prove all prior victims survived. Amentola arrives with Norma and Radon; their presence does not erase earlier hostility. Evidence, P0007–P0008, P0019–P0039.
  • P6-C02: Gamov says only he, Gonzalez, and Pustovoyt knew the full system. Keeping Semipalov ignorant preserved his sincere hatred as part of the performance. Gonzalez admits real killings despite the saved hundreds. Biber lives and proposes world union; Falk returns home planning an anti-war novel after captivity and factory work. Evidence, P0006–P0053.
  • P6-C03: Gamov demands judgment, not immunity as victor. Gonzalez reveals the trial commitment made at his appointment; Gamov reveals the editors' assigned future roles. Georgiu will prosecute, Fagusta defend. Semipalov joins Gamov and Gonzalez as a defendant; Gonzalez also chairs the court. Evidence, P0011–P0085.
  • P6-C04: Semipalov rereads earlier acts as a coherent policy; this is retrospective inference, not proof that every detail was foreseen. Elena treats the trial as moral cleansing and admires Gamov without romantic love. Evidence, P0004–P0049.
  • P6-C05: After months of government building, Bar and Woodworth hold authority during the trial. Bernoulli has survived imprisonment, returns to politics, and warns against the victors' self-destruction. His later praise of the abduction is not retroactive consent. Evidence, P0002–P0037.

P6-C06–P6-C10 — Authorship, confession, and military support

  • P6-C06: Georgiu prosecutes avoidable state wrongs and reveals that Gamov wrote the unsigned Herald editorials. Semipalov accepts many charges, including Konduk executions and camp revenge. The prosecutor's Karmanyuk account differs from the earlier family disposition; see C029. Evidence, P0010–P0038.
  • P6-C07: Fagusta reveals that Gamov also wrote the hostile unsigned Tribune articles. Neither editor knew the other's arrangement; Gonzalez and Semipalov were also uninformed. Fagusta's support did not require his knowing every secret. Evidence, P0020–P0046.
  • P6-C08: Voityuk appears alive, saying he hid in slums. He describes Latanian birth and mixed ancestry but chosen Cortezian allegiance, and says his espionage was unpaid. His accusations about treason and calculated altruism are arguments, not new proof that the earlier rescue was unreal. Evidence, P0003–P0035.
  • P6-C09: Gonzalez calls Mordasov his first actual killing. He says Peano secured his acceptance of rewards and Gamov selected him for aversion to cruelty. Pustovoyt secretly reviewed sentences and organized simulations/shelters. Gonzalez demands his own death; no verdict yet follows. Biber defends historical results. Evidence, P0006–P0026.
  • P6-C10: At Serbin's initiative, Peano and Kaplin offer military intervention. Semipalov refuses immediate arrests but permits action to stop immediate execution and obtain a world referendum. He now sympathizes with Serbin, changing their earlier hostility. Evidence, P0002–P0039.

P6-C11–P6-C15 — Judgment, transfer, and the final witness

  • P6-C11: Gamov remembers rescue near Sorbas but no parents or birthplace. The caravan story was rescuers' conjecture. Tarkhun-khor reports no caravan evidence and proposes interworld origin; the court rejects this as conclusive proof. Semipalov and Pavel had withheld the unfinished scientific work. Evidence, P0045–P0136.
  • P6-C12: All three defendants receive death sentences. At Semipalov's signal, Kaplin's troops arrest Gamov and Gonzalez, stop enforcement, and enable a world referendum. Gamov privately wants a dramatic philosophical ending rather than ordinary administration. Guards remove an impulsator; no suicide attempt occurs. Evidence, P0003–P0074.
  • P6-C13: After the vote rejects death, Gamov is released. He says conscience and symbolic immortality shaped his intended martyrdom. He accepts a living departure to the other world instead; his childhood origin remains unproved despite accepting the images. Gonzalez has no new assigned office after abolition of the court. Evidence, P0002–P0011, P0031–P0088.
  • P6-C14: Pavel confirms that both men courted Elena; no affair follows from this. At the cabin Gonzalez throws Gamov back, forces Semipalov inside, and launches him. Semipalov travels; Gamov does not enter the departing cabin. Gonzalez's motive is unstated. Evidence, P0009–P0016, P0047–P0049.
  • P6-C15: Major Leon Segovia, linguistics professor, interprets for the surviving Semipalov. Segovia reports that footballer Mike Dixon broke his leg kicking the appeared cabin. Semipalov dictates his account, learns of his hosts' plan for technical supremacy, and prepares to resist military exploitation. He has no later news of Gamov, Gonzalez, the Core, Elena, or Pavel. His hoped-for Elena–Pavel future is not a witnessed marriage. Evidence, P0002–P0042, P0049–P0061.
