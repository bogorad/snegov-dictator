Continuity and inconsistency register

Catalog index.

Use stable IDs C001, C002, and so on. For each item record its classification, status, first evidence, conflicting or clarifying evidence, impact, and resolution if supplied.

Classification: confirmed contradiction; possible continuity error; numerical or chronological tension; character claim or deception; deliberate mystery; unresolved explanation; source or editorial artifact. Status: open, resolved, or retained ambiguity. Do not count a resolved mystery as a confirmed error.

The leading status of each item gives its final disposition. Dated updates retain what the reader knew earlier. Confirmed text discrepancies are C020 (reward calculation), C021 (party attribution), C023 (convoy totals), C024 (speaker attribution), and C028 (trial ordinals). C016 confirms different quoted estimates, with uncertain cause. Other retained items are qualified claims, terminology, chronology, or interpretation; they are not all counted as authorial errors.

C001 — Gamov's precise war warning and information source

  • Classification: unresolved explanation; accurate political prediction. Hidden information is only a possible interpretation.
  • Status: open.
  • First evidence: During the Thursday gathering Gamov predicts that war will begin the next day because Marutzian promises support for Patina. Bar and Shtupa report no visible preparation. Source: P1-C01, paragraphs P0011–P0013 and P0031–P0039.
  • Clarifying evidence: Before dawn, Patina attacks Lamaria, Marutzian announces mobilization, and the war expands toward Cortezia. Gamov's prediction is accurate in outcome, but the chapter does not state how he knows.
  • Impact: Shows that Gamov reads the political danger more accurately than the gathering. The source does not establish hidden access to information.
  • Resolution: The precision of the original warning remains unexplained. Later scientific/origin hypotheses do not establish its information source. Preserve skepticism followed by confirmation of the prediction.

C002 — Unforecast hurricane after a stable weather report

  • Classification: initially unexplained event, later identified as a military weather attack.
  • Status: partly resolved in P1-C02: a surprise weather attack is identified; operational details are unstated.
  • First evidence: Shtupa reports planned climate stability, no dangerous disturbance, and excellent weather for the next two days. Gamov insists that Cortezian stations are being forced into action. Source: P1-C01, paragraphs P0035–P0039 and P0053–P0057.
  • Conflicting evidence: Before dawn an extreme hurricane strikes, although Semipalov says no forecast or ordinary station accident could produce it. The broadcast then reports enhanced operation at Cortezia's overseas weather stations.
  • Impact: Connects the personal warning to demonstrated environmental change and raises the possibility of deliberate meteorological attack.
  • Resolution: P1-C02 P0014–P0017 identifies the surprise attack and its defeat. Specific operators and station remain unstated; the forecast gap is not a confirmed continuity error.

C003 — Official victory reports versus retreat evidence

  • Classification: character claim or deception; deliberate wartime propaganda.
  • Status: resolved as intentional information conflict, with later direct admissions of fabrication.
  • First evidence: Elena hears stereo reports of Patina's advances while food queues report that Latanian forces are abandoning captured cities. Semipalov says broadcasts and rumors each distort the truth, then admits that political leaders treat truth as an ineffective weapon. Source: P1-C02, paragraphs P0005–P0010.
  • Clarifying evidence: The narrator confirms that the celebrated victory becomes a real defeat after the allied conference and that the front changes hands. The chapter presents the conflict as manipulation and incomplete information, not as a contradiction in the war's physical events.
  • Impact: Shows how civilians judge the war through competing channels and establishes the political use of false confidence.
  • Resolution: P1-C03–P1-C10 supplies explicit invented battlefield reports and a coerced transfer described publicly as voluntary. These are deliberate representations, not incompatible physical histories.

C002 update — First meteorological attack

P1-C02 P0014–P0017 describes the first storm as a surprise meteorological attack, repelled after one night because distant posts detected its excessive speed. This partly resolves the P1-C01 forecast gap: planned good weather did not exclude an enemy attack. Cyclone steering is unstable and depends on intuition. The immediate context points to Amentola's forces, but the passage does not identify individual operators or a specific station. Status: partly resolved; remaining operational detail unstated. This is no demonstrated continuity error.

C004 — Albert Peano's relationship with Marutzian

  • Classification: character claim or deception; retained ambiguity.
  • Status: partly resolved by P1-C10: hostility is confirmed; suspected intent to cause Peano's death at the front is unproved.
  • First evidence: Rumors say Albert Peano is out of favor with his uncle Arthur Marutzian. Semipalov has personally heard warm conversations between them, which does not confirm hostility. Gamov suspects that Marutzian placed Peano in the exposed division so a resonance strike might kill him. Source: P1-C03, paragraphs P0052–P0059.
  • Clarifying evidence: Peano says that people do not tell his uncle what he dislikes; his smile may conceal his true mood. The chapter does not establish whether the assignment is punishment, protection, ordinary duty, or a deliberate threat.
  • Impact: Makes family loyalty and wartime assignment part of the political mystery around Marutzian.
  • Resolution: Mordasov's order, Woodworth's disclosure, and Marutzian's own P1-C10 P0099 statement confirm hostility. The suspected plan to kill Peano remains unproved; apparent warmth never established genuine loyalty.

C001 update — Patina's betrayal prediction

Torba's separate-peace order confirms its outcome in P1-C03 P0042–P0045. Gamov now makes a further prediction: Patina will join Cortezia and turn its weapons against Latania. The separate peace alone does not yet prove this additional claim.

The report followed Marshal Komlin's request for good news and General Leonid Prishchepa's demand that Gamov be highlighted. This confirms intentional morale propaganda rather than a physical contradiction. Source: P1-C03.

P1-C04 updates — C001, C003, and Shtok's identity

C005 — Making war profitable while making it repulsive

  • Classification/status: Intentional ideological tension remains; the promised accountability is enacted in P6-C12–P6-C13. This is not an authorial continuity error.
  • Evidence: Gamov pays combat rewards, then states that he wants to abolish war by humiliating it. Semipalov directly objects that rewards make participation profitable, resembling piracy. Gamov distinguishes earned rewards from robbery, stresses motivation, and admits he is still seeking the larger method. Source: P1-C05, P0040–P0072.
  • Impact: The reader must preserve the unresolved conflict between the anti-war end and the incentives used to win. Soldiers' threats to resist confiscation show the immediate political effect (P0073–P0093).

C003 update — Publicity as command protection

Gamov's invented Barta report has been broadcast. He explains that fame gives commanders freedom from superior interference (P0021–P0023). Korkin's reassuring reports also conflict with Komlin's admission of collapse (P0100–P0106). Both are identifiable reporting distortions. Source: P1-C05.

P1-C06 — Reward revision and C005

His initial guess that Gamov feels shame is corrected by Gamov's stated concern about encirclement (P0052–P0056). Source: P1-C06.
