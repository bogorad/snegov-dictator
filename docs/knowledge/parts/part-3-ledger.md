## P3-C01 — Terror, complicity, and a channel to the enemy

**Source:** [Chapter map](../source-map.md#p3-c01); [Russian text](../../../src/text/part0021.html). Complete reading: P0001–P0069.

**Catalog consulted:** Central characters (Semipalov, Gamov, Woodworth, Pavel Prishchepa, Bar, Peano, Gonzalez, Pustovoyt, Elena); places (Latania, Nordag, Patina, Lamaria, Zabon); world (war doctrine, propaganda, weather control); continuity C001–C004. Earlier-part changes to these entries await ordered reconciliation.

- **Characters and relations:** Semipalov chairs the Core, threatens resignation over executed prisoners, and stays after Gamov connects the executions to Semipalov's own threats to Voityuk (P0002–P0023). Gamov's claim that Semipalov bears responsibility is a political argument; Semipalov accepts moral complicity, but did not order this execution here. Pavel is a colonel and minister; Gonzalez is Minister of Terror; Pustovoyt is Minister of Mercy (P0017–P0018, P0038–P0046). Omar Isiro supplies press intelligence. Konstantin Fagusta writes in the *Tribune*. Putrament is president of Nordag. The four informed officials agree that Voityuk is a spy; this is their shared conclusion, with its earlier evidence outside this chapter (P0047–P0057).
- **Places and control:** Peano reports Zabon relieved and the Cortezian–Roderian attack checked, but two-thirds of Patina abandoned and the enemy not driven back into Lamaria (P0031–P0035). Pavel reports Nordag withdrawing from its border to a line thirty leagues inland. Do not convert leagues into an unsupported modern distance (P0018).
- **Events and durations:** Pavel's intelligence report places the execution broadcast six hours after the executions: broadcast 11:00; ministers summoned 11:05; cabinet assembled 11:20; withdrawal orders sent 11:40. This precise sequence is reported intelligence, not Semipalov's direct observation (P0018). Peano expects front-line quiet until spring but says victory within the current year has failed. Autumn rains and approaching winter set the season; no calendar year is supplied. Elena is in her second week of factory inspections, not away for two completed weeks (P0067).
- **Institutions and world rules:** Gamov defines Sacred Terror as punishment of leaders who start wars, distinct in his argument from ordinary military operations. The Black Court's paid-execution program is criticized by Fagusta for producing no visible attacks on rulers or arms industrialists while prisoners are killed (P0068). Bar reports improved production and a completed harvest, but depleted reserves and worsening winter supply. Pavel estimates 180,000–190,000 bandits; this is explicitly an estimate, not a count. Gonzalez receives roughly ten regiments and police control for their destruction before frost; Pustovoyt receives authority to devise leniency for those who disarm and restrain Gonzalez (P0037–P0046).
- **Continuity and open questions:** Semipalov plans to supply Voityuk with truth until one decisive falsehood can redirect the war. The one-use deception is a future plan, not yet executed. He delays the public anti-bandit announcement to give Voityuk a verifiable advance disclosure (P0051–P0066). Fagusta's judgment and Gamov's justification conflict morally, not factually. Semipalov's hatred of Fagusta comes from recognizing that Fagusta is right about the prisoner killings (P0068–P0069). Cross-part dependencies: the Voityuk operation, Zabon retreat, prisoner executions, gold reform, and Black Court must be reconciled against Part 2. No confirmed continuity error is established here.

## P3-C02 — Amnesty and the limits of state knowledge

**Source:** [Chapter map](../source-map.md#p3-c02); [Russian text](../../../src/text/part0021.html#K0RSA-e57e33ea454c44b1aa8a6263a5c5ad60). Complete reading: P0001–P0032.

**Catalog consulted:** P3-C01 above; central Gonzalez, Pustovoyt, Gamov, Pavel, and Bar entries; central urban-order and propaganda entries; continuity register. No earlier central entry yet covers this amnesty. Geographic normalization: the source auditor confirms **Roder** as the country nominative; **Rodera** is a Russian case form, not a second state.

- **Characters and relations:** Gonzalez directs the campaign. Pustovoyt reviews his address; Gamov approves it; Semipalov declines to help write it (P0003–P0007). Omar Isiro is explicitly Minister of Information (P0008). Gamov values Fagusta as a permitted critic but says one extreme voice cannot reveal the silent population's views (P0023–P0026). Semipalov obtains Gonzalez's agreement to imprison an unnamed person on demand, choosing the Black Court's frightening reputation over Pavel's intelligence service (P0027–P0032).
- **Places and control:** Operation Cleansing targets gangs throughout Latania, including urban shelters, mountain refuges, and forests. No new named location or border change appears. Peace on the external front leaves the ocean and weather temporarily free of military manipulation (P0002–P0006).
- **Events and durations:** In the autumn months, the campaign initially advances slowly. At the traditional first-snow festival, Gonzalez announces a Week of Silence and Reflection, then an Amnesty/Mercy Week, followed by extermination for as long as needed. The first snowfall is natural, not generated (P0003–P0005). Surrenders rise for the first three amnesty days, fall on day four, and dwindle to a few people on day seven. Gamov announces 273,565 people disarmed (P0010–P0015).
- **Institutions and world rules:** Surrender promises army service for fit men, treatment for the sick, and suitable work for others. Gonzalez urges members to deliver their leaders alive or dead; refusal after the deadline will bring indiscriminate punishment and artificial freezing or snow burial. Gamov says organized banditry is ended, while explicitly allowing that isolated crimes can persist. Ten divisions can be released from internal security, with at least as many expected from amnestied men (P0003–P0015). This is announced manpower potential, not proof all those divisions have formed. Pavel explains that field sensors and agents work better against armies than transient gangs. Gamov recognizes that dictatorship suppresses the disagreement needed to measure popular opinion and proposes provoking disclosure (P0017–P0026).
- **Continuity and open questions:** **Numerical discrepancy, open:** Pavel's estimate in P3-C01-P0040 is 180,000–190,000. Gamov quotes it as 170,000–180,000 in P3-C02-P0017. Both wordings are present in original `part0021.html`; no extractor artifact explains the difference. It is a confirmed difference between the two statements, but whether it is Gamov's inaccurate recollection or a continuity error remains uncertain. Both estimates are lower than the surrender total. Do not silently replace either number. The proposed arrest's target is withheld in this chapter. The title “Week of Extermination” is a threat; this chapter does not narrate that final phase taking place.

## P3-C03 — Voityuk's coercion and the invented peace initiative

**Source:** [Chapter map](../source-map.md#p3-c03); [Russian text](../../../src/text/part0022.html). Complete reading: P0001–P0132.

**Catalog consulted:** P3-C01–C02, central Semipalov/Gamov/Woodworth/Pavel/Knurka IX entries, and searches for Voityuk, Anna Kursai, Shirbai Shar, and Klur. Their earlier histories await Part 2 reconciliation.

- **Characters and relations:** Voityuk is a diplomatic expert formerly under Woodworth and now working for Semipalov. He admits spying and names Semipalov as his source (P0023, P0038–P0050). Anna Kursai is Voityuk's wife; he says she knows nothing of his espionage. Semipalov has her arrested without a stated offense, threatens both spouses, and releases her during the same interview after obtaining compliance (P0054–P0067, P0108–P0115). Shirbai Shar, a minister of Knurka IX, gave Voityuk an ancestral emerald necklace; Semipalov identifies it as payment for secrets and rejects Voityuk's hint of an affair with Anna (P0023–P0027). The payment interpretation is Semipalov's stated evidence, not a separately witnessed exchange here.
- **Places and routes:** Information flows from Semipalov through Voityuk to Cortezia. Semipalov requests a financial channel through a bank in Klur; its geographic or constitutional status is not established here (P0089–P0105).
- **Events and amounts:** Voityuk tries to blackmail Semipalov with recorded conversations. Semipalov's arrest of Anna reverses control. He requests an initial 100 million didans in one hundred anonymous bearer accounts of one million each; he will test access through two or three withdrawals. Voityuk equates the total with ten chuds of gold and one account with one hundred dins; retain these source units without invented conversions (P0091–P0103). Funds are demanded, not yet received.
- **Institutions and technology:** Voityuk's pen records or transmits the conversations; Semipalov recognizes its covert purpose (P0036–P0038). The cover story is a political alliance to replace Gamov with a more negotiable government. Semipalov refuses harmful details about new divisions, energy-water production, and the waterplane fleet. His professed willingness to displace Gamov is an operation, not confirmed treason: Gamov, Woodworth, and Pavel hear and praise the interview (P0071–P0087, P0117–P0128).
- **Continuity and resolutions:** The unnamed prospective arrestee in P3-C02 is now identified as Anna. Voityuk flags that the predicted Gamov speech was delivered by Gonzalez; Semipalov explains delegation, consistent with the approval described in P3-C02 (P0048–P0050). Semipalov tells Voityuk that Gamov will ask enemies for peace terms to test public opinion. Gamov later says he had planned no such appeal. Thus “only truth” includes an announcement invented by Semipalov and subsequently made into policy; this is exposed operational deception, not a narrator continuity error (P0084, P0129–P0132). The great declaration is forecast retrospectively; its actual terms are not yet given. Semipalov recognizes that his enjoyment of coercion and intrigue changes his self-understanding (P0116). The possible actual ambition to replace Gamov is raised as a question, then denied by Gamov and accepted by the narrator as mutual dependence (P0121–P0126).

## P3-C04 — The Declaration on War

**Source:** [Chapter map](../source-map.md#p3-c04); [opening Russian text](../../../src/text/part0023.html), [continuation](../../../src/text/part0024.html). Complete reading: P0001–P0168; the continuation begins at P0034. The two HTML files form one chapter.

**Catalog consulted:** P3-C01–C03; central Gamov, Semipalov, Woodworth, Bar, Gonzalez, Amentola, Marutzian, war-doctrine and Black Court references; searches for Klur, Orest Biber, and Arnold Falk. Earlier appearances, if any, await ordered reconciliation.

- **Characters and relations:** Orest Biber is a philosophy professor and Arnold Falk is a novelist from neutral Klur. They visit Adan and debate Gamov before the Core. Falk admires Gamov's campaigns and coup but glorifies war; Gonzalez says Falk has already been accused, though no execution reward has been set (P0025–P0033, P0046–P0052, P0072–P0077). Falk reports that Marshal Vaksel rejected his offer of military service, preferring his writing. Semipalov explicitly identifies himself as war minister and refuses to call himself a criminal. Woodworth is foreign minister (P0009–P0010, P0018). Gamov explains Woodworth's migration as recognition of Latania's future; this is Gamov's account of Woodworth's motive, not Woodworth's testimony here (P0163).
- **Places and durations:** Klur is explicitly a neutral country. Guests travel from Klur to Adan. Falk says Klur has not fought for thirty years. The audience was allotted two hours and has entered its third hour at the end; the exact total is unstated (P0025–P0026, P0072, P0164).
- **Events and political method:** Gamov publishes the Declaration as his personal appeal, allowing ministers to publicize disagreement through Fagusta. It will both provoke public opinion and reinforce the foreign belief that Semipalov is his rival. Domestic reaction is limited; foreign outrage is widespread. Enemy governments delay replying to the request for peace terms (P0002–P0025). Semipalov records the later interview from memory and guarantees its meaning, not exact wording (P0033).
- **Institutions and doctrine:** Gamov distinguishes this modern war from all historical wars. He accepts that self-defense, liberation, or rescue can justify fighting; he argues that present prosperity removes any necessity for the current war. Economic differences, political rivalry, and spheres of influence do not, in his view, require mass killing. He identifies rulers' unrestrained will as the cause and makes participants and negligent bystanders answerable, including himself (P0041, P0062–P0069, P0094–P0129). These are argued political claims, not independently established world laws. He promises a postwar trial before the peoples and invites Cortezia to fund a Black Court section and capture him first. He says Latania has allocated five billion gold lats to that institution; the suggested Cortezian contribution is several billion didans, a different currency (P0131–P0135).
- **Human nature and political forecast:** Biber argues that rulers succeed because people are naturally warlike. Gamov replies that human creativity is more fundamental than destruction and that modern power requires responsibility. He describes wealthy Cortezia as structurally exhausted and poorer Latania as the beginning of a new stage, where he can abolish interstate war. These are Gamov's theory and ambition, not a demonstrated outcome (P0143–P0163).
- **Continuity and open questions:** The chapter fulfills the declaration forecast in P3-C03. Its expansive wording is qualified during discussion: the condemnation is of the current avoidable war, not every historical act of resistance. Preserve that distinction instead of reporting an unexplained reversal. Semipalov's real disagreement supplies the material for a false political-defection story. Whether Gamov will actually submit to judgment and whether his theory of Latania's future will hold remain open. No confirmed continuity error is established.

## P3-C05 — Enemy financing and the hidden fleet

**Source:** [Chapter map](../source-map.md#p3-c05); [Russian text](../../../src/text/part0025.html). Complete reading: P0001–P0075.

**Catalog consulted:** P3-C01–C04 (Voityuk, Klur, declaration, energy-water questions); central Shtupa, Elena, Woodworth, Peano, country entries, and waterplane technology. Searches cover the new named politicians and Orion bank.

**Name reconciliation:** The shared catalog uses **Omar Isiro** for the source surname. It uses **Mgobo Mardoba**, with **Mordoba** recorded as the source spelling in this chapter (P0014), compared with Mardoba in P2-C06-P0008. These denote the same Sobrana president; the spelling variation is not a plot contradiction.

- **Characters and relations:** Ferdinand Vaksel commands the planned enemy offensive. Kir Kirun is brother of Lon Chudin, president of Great Lepin; Mgobo Mardoba is president of Sobrana. Pavel reports Shirbai Shar negotiating in Cortezia, Kirun meeting Cortezians in Konda, and Mordoba threatening retaliation against Latania (P0007–P0017). Leonard Bernoulli, former farmer, now professor and leader of Cortezian independents, opposes taking on Latania's former allies as burdens; he is Amentola's personal enemy and Woodworth's university contemporary. His opposition concerns this alliance policy, not opposition to war. Elena holds a deputy-minister-level government post and receives a technology award in lats; she complains that a promised larger role has been postponed (P0061–P0075). Danilo Mordasov is recalled as the official who earlier arrived to confiscate captured money; reconcile that earlier action with Part 1/2 (P0048).
- **Places and control:** Orion bank in neutral Klur holds the one hundred accounts offered to Semipalov. Konda is a place visited by Kirun; this chapter does not define its wider status. Shtupa pushes clouds east toward mountains near Lepin to build water reserves. The secret aircraft plant is inside a mountain, reached from Adan through forest and a gorge; no mountain name is supplied (P0022, P0043–P0048).
- **Events, amounts, and season:** Late winter approaches spring; snow is thawing and the sun has been hidden for a week. Pavel forecasts Vaksel will have five times the force he used against Zabon by spring. Cortezia is expanding weather stations, working energy-water plants in three shifts, and restricting civilian electricity for the first time in its history, according to his report (P0007). Shtupa says reliable counter-cyclone defense needs twice his current energy-water supply. The Core has ten members, with Gamov, Semipalov, Woodworth, and Pavel forming its inner four (P0018). Voityuk supplies the hundred account codes that day; successful withdrawals are not yet shown (P0019–P0037).
- **Institutions and technology:** Gamov distinguishes winning this war from using victory to prevent future wars; he expects later condemnation of actions currently rewarded (P0002–P0005). The *Herald of Terror and Mercy* prints sentences and execution rewards, but Semipalov judges the few attacks on minor figures ineffective (P0006). Gamov orders surveillance of Voityuk removed to protect the deception (P0038–P0041). The secret is the fleet's unprecedented size, not the existence of waterplanes. Condensed water resembles glass, weighs twenty times as much as ordinary glass, and releases stored energy as steam. Lower, rear, and nose reservoirs supply lift, propulsion, and braking; a combat waterplane outpaces Shtupa's strongest generated hurricane (P0048–P0056). Plant reports divide real output by a secret coefficient: three here, up to nine elsewhere (P0058–P0060).
- **Continuity and open questions:** The requested Cortezian money in P3-C03 now has account codes; access still needs verification. The pending enemy Declaration of Peace is announced through Voityuk, not yet published. Semipalov's refusal to increase Shtupa's supply is now explained by the enormous fleet reserve, even though it endangers weather defense. The factory security lapse at the first checkpoint contrasts with the second checkpoint's radiation-authenticated pass (P0045–P0047); it is an observed weakness, not a contradiction. Elena's postponed role remains unexplained in this chapter. No confirmed continuity error is established.

## P3-C06 — A referendum on peace and power

**Source:** [Chapter map](../source-map.md#p3-c06); [Russian text](../../../src/text/part0025.html#NQU4G-e57e33ea454c44b1aa8a6263a5c5ad60). Complete reading: P0001–P0059.

**Catalog consulted:** P3-C02 (public opinion), P3-C03–C05 (peace initiative, staged rivalry, pending enemy reply), central Gamov, Semipalov, Pavel, Woodworth, Amentola, and political-mobilization entries. No central referendum record existed at reading.

- **Characters and relations:** The enemy declaration names Gamov, Gonzalez, and Woodworth as officials who must leave office; it leaves other ministers' futures to Latania. Pavel and Gamov read this as encouragement for Semipalov's supposed challenge (P0004–P0011). Pavel proposes manipulating ballot totals; Gamov expressly refuses. Isiro is assigned to run the vote (P0025–P0040). Gamov privately promises to resign if the people reject him and says Semipalov will succeed him. Semipalov insists Gamov can be removed but cannot be replaced in their shared work (P0047–P0059).
- **Places and events:** The enemy Declaration of Peace appears with the first natural spring thunderstorm over Adan. It praises Latania while demanding acknowledgment of aggression, political change, and resolution of economic, ideological, political, and territorial disputes. Semipalov interprets it as subordination to Cortezia (P0002–P0005). The four proposed ballot questions concern Latanian responsibility for aggressive war, resignation of Gamov's government, payment of monetary and goods reparations, and satisfaction of neighboring territorial claims (P0031–P0037).
- **Durations and institutions:** Semipalov says the ministers have worked together for almost half a year. Isiro says Gamov prescribed referendum procedures two months earlier. These durations refer to government work and technical referendum preparation respectively, not to the peace declaration (P0039–P0044). Gamov rejects Marutzian-style falsification because both rulers and people need reliable knowledge of national opinion. The proposed vote requires separate yes/no answers, so support for national resistance need not imply support for Gamov (P0027–P0037, P0047).
- **Continuity and open questions:** The advance referendum preparations answer why Gamov reacts with delight rather than alarm. He had planned a general test of support; the enemy supplies its occasion. This does not contradict the invented declaration in P3-C03: technical preparation is distinct from choosing this particular diplomatic trigger. Gamov predicts the enemy will postpone attack because pressure would unite Latania, but this is a strategic prediction at this point (P0045–P0046). Semipalov suggests three-quarters support is the minimum adequate foundation; this is his political judgment, not a constitutional requirement. Gamov first predicts more than half, then agrees that a mere half would be disastrous for his mission (P0048–P0053). Actual votes and the enemy's response remain unknown.

## P3-C07 — Mandate, invasion, and two shocks

**Source:** [Chapter map](../source-map.md#p3-c07); [Russian text](../../../src/text/part0026.html). Complete reading: P0001–P0093.

**Catalog consulted:** P3-C05–C06 (Bernoulli, enemy preparations, referendum); central Fagusta, Gamov, Bar, Pavel, Peano, Marutzian, Knurka IX, state geography, weather warfare, and aerial weapons. Searches cover Floria, Semyon Serbin, Grigory Varella, Sorbas, Konduk, and Korina; earlier histories await the ordered merge.

- **Characters and relations:** Fagusta, leader of the defunct Optimates, says he has always supported this government as better than any available replacement, including himself. His criticism is sincere support, and his restrained pre-vote coverage is deliberate (P0002–P0017). Amentola orders Vaksel to await the vote. Bernoulli presses for immediate attack and a halt to subsidies for new allies. Woodworth claims Bernoulli cannot become president because voters reject his appearance; this is Woodworth's political assessment, not a demonstrated rule (P0022–P0031). Guard Semyon Serbin takes a knife wound while saving Gamov. Grigory Varella, appointed head of Gamov's security by Pavel, saves one attacker from the crowd; two die (P0055–P0069).
- **Referendum evidence:** Isiro had two weeks, announces readiness in ten days, and says the mechanics were ready on the decision day. Voting runs from local dawn to midnight across time zones. His reported national yes/no totals are: aggression guilt **7/93%**; government resignation **13/87%**; reparations **1/99%**; territorial concessions **4/96%** (P0019–P0020, P0032–P0037). Floria's yes totals are respectively **32%, 37%, 6%, 18%**; the prose lists territory before reparations, so preserve the question mapping (P0038). Gamov welcomes both the mandate and the higher support for national independence than for his government (P0042–P0044).
- **Places and control:** Floria is Latania's western autonomous region beside Patina, with army communications running through it. National voting is broadly uniform except there. After the first enemy breakthrough, Great Lepin, Sobrana, Konduk, Klur, and Korina declare war on the same day, one week into the offensive. Torbash alone among these remaining associates stays formally out, raises new border demands, and sends Shirbai Shar; Gamov orders courteous delay (P0038–P0041, P0052–P0053). Klur's neutrality in P3-C04 is therefore a prior state, not an enduring fact.
- **Military sequence and technology:** The morning after the vote-related government reply, Vaksel attacks across the front. Peano withdraws from the first line before encirclement, preserving men behind the main defenses. He reports lower losses than the enemy and relies on Pavel's buried sensor network in abandoned territory (P0044–P0050). Later, opposing counterclockwise cyclones meet above eastern Patina and western Floria; their storm line shifts slowly east as Vaksel overpowers Shtupa. A separate desert front borders Konduk (P0071–P0074).
- **Sorbas:** A declared peaceful Latanian desert town with gardens, canals, and a dried-fruit factory; Semipalov previously tested desert vehicles there. Fifteen Cortezian-supplied aircraft give Konduk the capability to overfly border defenses. Konduk's aircraft cross at night and bomb at dawn. First reports estimate half the population killed and half escaping into the desert; the split is preliminary reporting, not a verified census. Peano sends mobile units expected to collect survivors by evening (P0077–P0088).
- **Continuity and open questions:** Gamov's expectation that Amentola will wait for the vote is confirmed by the order to Vaksel. The confidence crisis is resolved by the reported 87% opposition to resignation. Fagusta's support clarifies the distinction between criticism and opposition, rather than reversing all his earlier criticism. Serbin's earlier humiliation and subsequent public reconciliation are recalled; check against P1-C06 and P1-C08 P0008–P0013 during ordered reconciliation. Pavel suspects a plot led by Komlin from the surviving attacker's incomplete testimony and arrests Komlin, Marutzian, and about ten associates. Leadership of the plot is not yet proved in this chapter (P0089–P0092). The assassination attempt and Sorbas bombing are distinct shocks within the week; no causal link is asserted.

## P3-C08 — Konduk occupied and Torbash checked

**Source:** [Chapter map](../source-map.md#p3-c08); [Russian text](../../../src/text/part0027.html). Complete reading: P0001–P0099.

**Catalog consulted:** P3-C05–C07 (fleet, former allies, Sorbas, Torbash mission); central country, Black/White Court, and character records. New central P1-C06 records identify Serbin's humiliation and Varella's part in the mutiny; their fuller sequence remains for ordered reconciliation. Searches cover Mamun, Tarkhun-khor, Maravan-khor, and Kondina.

- **Characters, society, and beliefs:** Tarkhun-khor is Konduk's chief religious leader and the seventy-fourth living embodiment of ancient prophet Mamun. Maravan-khor controls parliament and ordered the Sorbas attack without a parliamentary vote. Provincial rivalry substitutes for party competition. Priests and landowners dominate an agricultural export economy with widespread poverty. Sorbas is traditionally Mamun's point of departure from the desert. Pavel suggests that its sacred associations might have produced resistance to the raid; this is an inference, not a recorded parliamentary vote on the bombing (P0004–P0007, P0028). Isiro and Gamov know Mamun's Book of Songs; Gamov reveals he grew up among Mamun's followers (P0036–P0043).
- **Military action and geography:** Gamov initially demands a quick conquest to punish Sorbas and deter imitation. Semipalov objects that using the new fleet will reveal its scale. Gamov argues that this risk will force Cortezia to divert supplies to frightened allies. Semipalov releases one hundred aircraft. Peano promises plans the next day and launch the following morning. Airborne troops land in **Kondina**, Konduk's capital, and capture political, religious, and military leaders while Konduk's armies still fight at the border. Maravan-khor broadcasts surrender; Latanian ground forces cross. The narrator says no foreign soldier had entered armed for 1,500 years (P0010–P0028). Do not merge Kondina with the earlier Konda without independent evidence.
- **Occupation and collective penalties:** Isiro heads the occupation commission, with Gonzalez and Pustovoyt judging parliamentarians jointly. Those who voted for war are hanged, their property seized, and families sent to northern Latania. Abstainers lose half their property and perform forced labor in Konduk until war ends. The 17% who opposed war receive confiscated enterprises and form a government subordinate to the occupation commander. Provinces are punished or rewarded according to their delegates' votes: pro-war provinces lose one-third of property and send soldiers as prisoners to rebuild Sorbas; abstaining provinces pay reparations and supply a labor army; anti-war provinces receive property and have soldiers released. Maravan-khor is displayed hanged. The judicial process discards normal procedural safeguards (P0029–P0035). Clergy are deliberately exempted from these executions as Gamov pursues a separate religious strategy; its full purpose is withheld (P0036–P0043).
- **International outcomes:** Great Lepin halts its advance; Kir Kirun is now explicitly commander-in-chief but Lon Chudin withholds approval to invade. Sobrana concentrates troops along Konduk's border. Klur sends two divisions to Vaksel and Korina one to Nordag. Amentola admits surprise and promises two hundred aircraft to countries bordering Latania. Bernoulli condemns the diversion from the western front. Gamov instructs Semipalov to use Voityuk to encourage Amentola's policy and discredit Bernoulli (P0046–P0056). Shtupa warns he cannot hold back the western cyclones.
- **Torbash negotiations and exact terms:** In a recorded meeting one day before the invasion, Shirbai demands land up to one hundred leagues deep, supposedly annexed 217 years earlier; a loan of one hundred million kalons converted to gold lats; and free equipment, arms, and experts, with a three-day response deadline. In a second recording one day after occupation, Woodworth rejects the demands and seeks two roads to move troops toward Sobrana and paid Torbash food supplies, partly on postwar credit (P0057–P0077). These recordings are retrospectively shown after the occupation; their narrative placement does not date them after the executions.
- **Continuity and open questions:** Knurka seeks shares in the White Court so he can sit in judgment if accused. Semipalov sees the share-voting structure as a weakness; Gamov welcomes self-judgment and does not exclude judging himself (P0083–P0099). No actual Torbash admission is completed here. The disparity between roughly one-third approval of the bombing and the war-vote categories is not a discrepancy: they refer to different decisions, and the bombing was not put to a formal vote (P0007, P0032). The religious mission remains open, and the anonymous surviving assassin's evidence has not yet been examined further. No confirmed continuity error is established.

## P3-C09 — Preparing Bernoulli's ruin

**Source:** [Chapter map](../source-map.md#p3-c09); [Russian text](../../../src/text/part0028.html). Complete reading: P0001–P0076.

**Catalog consulted:** P3-C03, C05, C07–C08 for Voityuk, money, hidden fleet, allied-resource strategy, Nordag, and Bernoulli; central propaganda/continuity entries. Revisions below preserve the distinction between operational cover stories and narrator-reported plans.

- **Military position and places:** Vaksel advances slowly to exhaust Latanian equipment before meeting Peano's mobile reserves. Peano abandons costly positions and avoids counterattacks. Nordag again crosses the border and blocks Zabon from north and east, awaiting Vaksel from the southwest. The western front leaves the remaining Patina regions and enters Floria. Artificial storms flood fields around Adan, destroy riverbanks and bridges, and threaten the harvest; available energy-water only reduces their severity (P0002–P0005). The attrition continues over days and weeks; no exact elapsed span is stated.
- **Bernoulli operation:** Pavel proposes assassination, abduction, or discrediting the senator. The first two alone would advertise the accuracy of his warnings. The selected scheme sends a donation to Bernoulli's Vaksel Fund from a fictitious Society of Sympathetic Industrialists. Exposure of the donor as a Latanian agent will permit Amentola's police to frame Bernoulli as an agent too. Abduction before exoneration will then be represented as flight from justice. The operation will use the very Cortezian funds meant to overthrow Gamov (P0006–P0024). These are plans, not completed acts in this chapter.
- **Information channel and relations:** Pavel reports two Voityuk–Shirbai meetings, the second lasting half an hour on the day after Konduk's invasion, immediately before Shirbai's second Woodworth audience. Despite Gamov's earlier ban on surveillance in P3-C05-P0040, the source does not say how Pavel obtained these facts; record an unresolved information-source question, not proved disobedience (P0025–P0026). Semipalov tells Voityuk some of the money has been used; within this deliberately staged conversation, the line confirms his claim of access but is not independent bank evidence (P0028–P0030).
- **Deception content:** Semipalov blames the enemy peace declaration for uniting Latanians around Gamov and says he himself voted for Gamov. He claims Gamov plans conquests south and east to replace the ruined Latanian harvest with food seized from Konduk, Torbash, Sobrana, and Lepin, then turn west. He falsely tells Voityuk that every serviceable waterplane was used in Konduk, although P3-C08-P0017 explicitly says one hundred is not the entire fleet. He also plants the claim that an unnamed influential Cortezian politician secretly serves Gamov, preparing the frame against Bernoulli (P0033–P0065). Genuine disagreement with Gamov's willingness to accept suffering lends emotional force to the cover story; Gamov detects that real doubt while approving the performance (P0048–P0054, P0066–P0070).
- **Continuity and open questions:** Semipalov's statement that he “did not lie” in P0048 sits beside the demonstrably false fleet claim in P0047. Treat this as tension in his description of a deceptive performance, not proof that the fleet shrank. **Possible dialogue/name error:** P0022 follows Pavel's proposed scheme but contains an address to “Pavel” within that speaker's apparent turn; P0023 then has Semipalov undertake the Voityuk conversation. Preserve this textual ambiguity for review rather than silently correcting the speaker. The chapter ends with a new, confirmed report: waterplane crews have arrested commanders, armed their aircraft, and threatened military action. Gamov will fly with Semipalov and Pavel to suppress the mutiny (P0071–P0076).

## P3-C10 — The patriotic mutiny

**Source:** [Chapter map](../source-map.md#p3-c10); [Russian text](../../../src/text/part0029.html). Complete reading: P0001–P0085.

**Catalog consulted:** P3-C05, C08–C09 for hidden fleet, Konduk deployment, and mutiny report; central military technology, Serbin/Varella mutiny history, and searches for the newly identified aircrew officers. No new base name is invented.

- **Characters and identities:** The four leaders are Alfred Palman, Ivan Kordobin, Sergei Skripnik, and Jean Vilta. Kordobin speaks for the pilots (P0021–P0024, P0037–P0042). Their commander is Colonel Korney Kaplin, the former major and pilot who brought Danilo Mordasov to the encircled force; this identifies the returning pilot, whose earlier scene requires Part 1 reconciliation (P0048). Pavel's unnamed liaison reported unrest the previous day, vanished from contact roughly two hours later, and was apparently arrested; the narrator reports the arrest as an inference at that point (P0004–P0007).
- **Places, quantities, and security:** The unnamed base has six two-story barracks and underground hangars for 120 fully operational waterplanes, mountains to the east and forest elsewhere. All such bases lack public names/numbers, civilian lines, and outside contact; recruits cannot correspond with relatives. Sensors distinguish approaching humans from animals. Pavel communicates through paired transmitters keyed to his individual radiation. The narrator says interception and replication are impossible; this is the source's technical assertion, not a real-world scientific fact (P0002–P0009).
- **Cause and sequence:** Crews spent winter training, expected spring combat, and became desperate as the army retreated and fields flooded. Konduk's attack raised hopes of action, followed by renewed idleness. They concluded a senior official was sabotaging victory, imprisoned officers who threatened discipline, and prepared an unauthorized sortie. They still obey Gamov and release their commanders immediately at his command (P0022–P0042). Their motive is patriotic impatience, not enemy allegiance.
- **Resolution and military organization:** Gamov thanks the pilots for patriotism, condemns their method, and pardons both them and the officers. He credits the commanders with forming devoted soldiers. He reveals that the base is one of many air divisions; partial deployment would waste the decisive surprise before the whole fleet is ready. He calls for willingness to die for the country, creating an emotional reconciliation. The crowd carries him around the square until shaking nearly makes him lose consciousness and Semipalov asks Kaplin to restore order (P0047–P0059). Air divisions have eight regiments. Gamov creates four two-regiment brigades in this division, gives the four mutiny leaders command, and orders appropriate rank promotions without naming the ranks (P0062–P0075).
- **Continuity and open questions:** Kaplin confirms the four were already top performers and candidates for promotion; the appointments are therefore supported by prior training reports, though Semipalov doubts their lack of combat experience (P0062–P0074). Gamov refuses to pardon the liaison's professional failure to explain morale; he retains the arrest order and requires simpler future work (P0079–P0081). His promise of coming combat does not set a new launch date: he explicitly preserves the larger plan and says aircraft will be used when necessary (P0083–P0085). The 120-aircraft base and multiple divisions further disprove the cover story that the hundred sent to Konduk exhausted the fleet. No confirmed continuity error is established.

## P3-C11 — Bernoulli abducted, but the deception remains fragile

**Source:** [Chapter map](../source-map.md#p3-c11); [Russian text](../../../src/text/part0030.html). Complete reading: P0001–P0078.

**Catalog consulted:** P3-C05, C07–C09 for Bernoulli, Woodworth, donation plan, reserves, and assassination inquiry; central Serbin, Komlin, Marutzian, and propaganda records.

- **Events and amounts:** A donation of **five million didans** from the fictitious Society of Sympathetic Industrialists produces a public scandal. Amentola accuses Bernoulli, who demands his removal; the senate orders an inquiry. Bernoulli's reputation for honesty leads many to suspect Amentola of fabrication. Amentola holds reserves back from both allies and Vaksel, so the scheme has not achieved its full strategic aim (P0002–P0006). Pavel has already abducted Bernoulli to an unnamed neutral country and promises to bring him in three days. The meeting occurs on the third day (P0009, P0025–P0030). Do not identify that transit country as Klur, which is now a belligerent.
- **Characters and relations:** Woodworth says they have not met for fourteen years; the narrator says Bernoulli is two or three years older but looks twenty years older. Fourteen years since a meeting is not automatically inconsistent with Woodworth's roughly ten years in Latania (P0032–P0035). Bernoulli calls Woodworth a traitor to both Cortezia and Marutzian and refuses to shake his hand. He correctly explains Gamov's plan to burden Cortezia with allies and wear down Vaksel. He refuses cooperation and says that, if kept alive, he will expose the fraud (P0040–P0068).
- **False evidence and limits:** Pavel prepares an announcement that Bernoulli is a rescued Latanian agent, a decree giving honors and money, and photomontages showing friendship with Gamov's ministers. He asks Woodworth for a false account of a youthful anti-Cortezian pact. Woodworth dislikes the lie but agrees to write it (P0011–P0017). Negatives could reveal montage, while newspaper reproductions conceal it; Pavel says the component photographs were privately taken by his agents rather than copied from public images (P0015–P0016, P0046–P0047). Bernoulli anticipates the false decorations and proposes a pension sarcastically; it is not yet an independently granted benefit (P0050–P0053).
- **Psychological turn:** Bernoulli argues that abduction will privately vindicate his warnings in Amentola's eyes, even if the president publicly accepts the treason accusation. Gamov responds that lavish public recognition of Bernoulli's intellect will give even friends a believable motive for defection: the validation Cortezia denied him. He intends to make the captive serve the strategy through this reputation, not through genuine consent (P0049–P0068).
- **Other developments:** Serbin is recovering. Marutzian and Komlin have given testimony about the attempt, but Gamov postpones hearing it; its contents remain undisclosed (P0070–P0073). The group leans toward publishing the frauds, yet Gamov still wants a larger action that will force Amentola to disperse his reserves. Semipalov has an idea but withholds it (P0074–P0078).
- **Continuity and open questions:** **Fund-description discrepancy, open:** the P3-C09-P0018 plan targets Bernoulli's “Fund for Vaksel”; P3-C11-P0002 describes the received donation as entering his electoral fund. The text supplies no explicit change of target or explanation. Record the wording difference without assuming two donations or silently selecting one fund. The official draft saying Bernoulli is already in Latania is prepared while he is in transit; it is intended propaganda, not proof of simultaneous locations. The strategic success of the operation remains explicitly unresolved.

## P3-C12 — Semipalov sacrifices his public identity

**Source:** [Chapter map](../source-map.md#p3-c12); [Russian text](../../../src/text/part0031.html). Complete reading: P0001–P0158.

**Catalog consulted:** P3-C03, C06, C09–C11 (staged rivalry, funds, Bernoulli, aircrew appeal); central Semipalov/Elena, Pustovoyt, Gonzalez, Serbin, and continuity C006. Searches cover Pimen Georgiu and the court/mercy institutions.

- **Motive and plan:** Semipalov interprets the pilots' response as a hunger to be uniquely needed. He proposes that his own alleged betrayal and death sentence provide the decisive confirmation of Voityuk's information. Bernoulli can be credited with exposing the high-ranking traitor, strengthening the false account of his own allegiance. Gamov, Pavel, and Woodworth approve. Semipalov consents to condemnation, **not actual death**. Gonzalez is initially to judge him without knowing the deception; Pustovoyt will know and arrange a safe false hanging. Semipalov is to resume command against Vaksel when the counteroffensive starts (P0002–P0028).
- **Timing and intelligence sequence:** Semipalov asks for twenty-four hours before arrest. He tells Voityuk that Bernoulli learned from a banker about a large transfer to a Latanian traitor. This is an invented exposure narrative. Voityuk proposes escape to Cortezia and will seek instructions overnight. Semipalov asks Gamov to arrest him about an hour before the escape plan arrives, so he need not make a genuine escape attempt. Arrest shifts from the next afternoon to the next morning (P0030–P0061). No evacuation route is learned here.
- **Arrest and confinement:** Pavel arrests Semipalov publicly while he meets military-factory directors. Serbin, recovered enough for guard duty, treats him with hatred, pushes him, and urges him toward execution; Pavel checks the abuse and excludes Serbin from the prisoner's vehicle. Ten minutes later Semipalov is in solitary custody under the Black Court. Gamov refuses his request for a meeting. Despite knowing the plan, he experiences real imprisonment and loss of agency (P0062–P0084). The “division of guards” he complains about is a rhetorical protest, not a literal troop count.
- **Mercy technology and changed trial plan:** Pustovoyt visits that evening. His engineers have designed a hanging apparatus that suspends the enclosing sack while the person continues breathing; the body will appear to move on the rope. The proposed display height is eight lans; do not convert without source authority (P0092–P0102). He also obtains Gamov's agreement to abandon a real public confrontation with Gonzalez: they can announce a secret trial whose transcript is withheld until war ends. Thus the initial genuine-judge plan is modified, not carried out in this chapter (P0105–P0108). Pustovoyt's care establishes a new personal intimacy with Semipalov.
- **Public narrative:** The *Herald of Terror and Mercy* prints Pavel's accusation and Gonzalez's announcement of a closed hearing. Its editor, Pimen Georgiu, calls for harsher intolerance. The notice names Pavel Minister of State Protection and Semipalov deputy dictator and defense minister. Earlier “war minister” usage refers to Semipalov's same role, not a separate new person. Fagusta instead discusses the moral incoherence of opposite alleged defections by Bernoulli and Semipalov; this is public commentary within the fraud, not proof he knows its mechanism (P0113–P0115).
- **Elena and outcome:** Semipalov deliberately withholds the truth from Elena because he thinks her expression would reveal it. Pustovoyt nevertheless permits her to visit the next morning after she pleads and cries (P0023–P0025, P0109–P0112). She demands to know whether he is guilty, recalls his resentment at being second to Gamov, and fears that ambition has corrupted him. These are her perceptions, not confirmation of treason. He demands trust but will not explain; their exchange becomes abusive. He finally repeats the false treason narrative, including money and a struggle for power, while predicting that the full truth will horrify her. She leaves declaring hatred and contempt; he is left in physical and emotional pain (P0121–P0158).
- **Continuity and open questions:** The apparent betrayal is fully disclosed to the reader as a voluntary state deception. Elena's belief is induced by the withheld information and staged arrest, not an authorial reversal of his loyalty. His hurt at Pavel's coldness coexists with Pavel's authorized role in the plan. No hanging, actual trial scene, public verdict, or battlefield return occurs within this chapter. Whether the apparatus works and what Elena will do remain open. Serbin's hostility develops the earlier ominous continuity thread without yet resolving its eventual consequences.

## P3-C13 — The public hanging and the apparent betrayal

**Source:** [Chapter map](../source-map.md#p3-c13); [Russian text](../../../src/text/part0031.html#id35). Complete reading: P0001–P0005. This is genuinely a short chapter, not a missing continuation; the following part title is excluded by the verified source boundaries.

**Catalog consulted:** P3-C12 (staged execution, secrecy, sack apparatus, Elena), P3-C07/C10 (Peano's manner, secrecy and discipline), and central Semipalov/Peano/Pustovoyt/Gonzalez entries. This entry closes the part's reading, not the unresolved execution story.

- **Place, time, and event:** The execution takes place at noon in Adan. Shtupa uses all weather-generator resources to keep the sky clear. A prison vehicle arrives between two guard vehicles in front and two behind. The gallows crossbar stands eight lans above ground, as Pustovoyt had promised. A band is present to drown out any final cries (P0002).
- **Characters and observation:** Gonzalez and Pustovoyt attend with court officials. Peano sits in a vehicle and cries. Semipalov sees no other ministers and does not find Elena in the crowd. The crowd neither cheers nor condemns the sentence. A court employee reads it, but the chapter does not reproduce its words. Semipalov declines a farewell speech (P0003–P0004).
- **Apparatus and immediate outcome:** Executioners enclose him in a leather sack, strap his legs and waist, and fit the noose. He first assumes Peano weeps because he does not know the hanging is staged. Inside the sack, he abruptly reverses that interpretation: he believes Peano knows this is a real execution and that the others have deceived him. He jerks in terror, is lifted, feels weightless, and experiences darkness “forever” (P0005).
- **Continuity and open question:** **Deliberate cliffhanger, unresolved at the part boundary.** Semipalov's conviction that the execution is real contradicts his prior expectation, not an independently established outcome. The chapter provides no external confirmation of death, no explanation of the apparatus, and no testimony from Peano. Preserve both interpretations of the tears as the narrator's successive beliefs. Do not infer irreversible death or successful rescue before Part 4 supplies evidence. This is not a confirmed authorial contradiction.
